# Contract mechanisms to prevent, divert or end consumer lawsuits at inception — Michigan and federal law as of September 2026

*Scope: a sole-author Michigan licensor (LLC planned) distributing a VS Code extension ("Ashley") to university students in Michigan and other states, free trial then paid one-time term licenses (tens of dollars), Michigan governing law, Michigan forum acceptable. Students are consumers. These notes separate (a) what the text of a statute/opinion says, (b) inferences, and (c) points counsel must decide. Method note: adr.org and law.justia.com were unreachable from the research tool; AAA fee figures below were read from an archived copy of the AAA Consumer Arbitration Rules "Costs of Arbitration" (amended and effective August 1, 2023, reproduced in a February 2025 copy of the rules) and a February 2024 law-firm alert on the mass-arbitration schedule; counsel should confirm current figures at adr.org before drafting.*

## Q1. Arbitration with a class waiver for consumer software: enforceability, delegation, mass arbitration, fees, opt-out, EFAA, small claims, MCPA arbitrability, and the arbitration-vs-court recommendation matrix

### Takeaway
Under the FAA a bilateral consumer arbitration clause with a class waiver and a properly drafted delegation clause is enforceable in Michigan and the Sixth Circuit (Concepcion, Epic, Rent-A-Center, Henry Schein, Blanton, In re StockX), and MCPA claims are routinely sent to arbitration. But for a vendor selling tens-of-dollar licenses, AAA's consumer fee schedule puts the business's minimum out-of-pocket cost per single claim at roughly $3,275 (desk arbitration) plus a $600 clause-registry fee and $600 per year, and mass-arbitration protocols designed to blunt that exposure were struck as unconscionable in Heckman (9th Cir. 2024, cert. denied Oct. 6, 2025), with 2025 district courts split. A Michigan-court forum clause with a small-claims carve-out avoids per-claim fees entirely, but the enforceability of a stand-alone (non-arbitration) class waiver under Michigan law is unsettled — no Michigan authority was found.

### Cited Findings

**Federal baseline (standard citations; not re-fetched this session)**
- *AT&T Mobility LLC v. Concepcion*, 563 U.S. 333 (2011): the FAA preempts California's *Discover Bank* rule conditioning enforceability of consumer arbitration agreements on class-arbitration availability — [Justia](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/563/333/)
- *American Express Co. v. Italian Colors Restaurant*, 570 U.S. 228 (2013): class waiver enforced even where the cost of individually arbitrating exceeds potential recovery — [Justia](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/570/228/)
- *Epic Systems Corp. v. Lewis*, 584 U.S. 497 (2018): class and collective action waivers in arbitration agreements enforced under the FAA — [Justia](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/584/16-285/)
- *Rent-A-Center, West, Inc. v. Jackson*, 561 U.S. 63 (2010): a delegation clause is a separately enforceable agreement; a party must challenge the delegation provision "specifically" for a court to decide enforceability — [Justia](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/561/63/)
- *Henry Schein, Inc. v. Archer & White Sales, Inc.*, 586 U.S. 63 (2019): no "wholly groundless" exception; when the parties clearly and unmistakably delegate arbitrability, the court must send it to the arbitrator — [Justia](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/586/17-1272/)
- *First Options of Chicago v. Kaplan*, 514 U.S. 938 (1995): "clear and unmistakable" evidence standard for delegating arbitrability — [Justia](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/514/938/)
- *Smith v. Spizzirri* (U.S. May 16, 2024): FAA § 3 requires courts to stay, not dismiss, when a party requests a stay pending arbitration; unanimous — [Cozen](https://www.cozen.com/news-resources/news/2024/supreme-court-says-stay-means-stay-not-dismiss-in-resolving-faa-circuit-split); [LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/22-1218)
- *Coinbase, Inc. v. Suski* (U.S. May 23, 2024): where two contracts conflict (one delegating arbitrability, one sending disputes to court), "a court, not an arbitrator, must decide which contract governs" — [Faegre Drinker](https://www.faegredrinker.com/en/insights/publications/2024/5/supreme-court-decides-coinbase-inc-v-suski); [SCOTUS slip op.](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/23pdf/23-3_879d.pdf)

**Sixth Circuit / Michigan**
- *Blanton v. Domino's Pizza Franchising LLC*, 962 F.3d 842 (6th Cir. June 17, 2020): "incorporation of AAA rules and procedures is clear and unmistakable evidence that questions of arbitrability are for the arbitrator"; eleven of twelve sister circuits agree — [6th Cir. opinion](https://www.opn.ca6.uscourts.gov/opinions.pdf/20a0184p-06.pdf); [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=33b1b8ec-e824-4116-b6f2-bf2747a94a51)
- *I.C. v. StockX, LLC (In re StockX Customer Data Security Breach Litigation)*, No. 21-1089 (6th Cir. Dec. 2, 2021): StockX's sign-up presented terms with "By signing up, you agree to the Terms of Service and Privacy Policy" with hyperlinked text; login screens said "By logging in, you agree to the Terms of Service and Privacy Policy"; the 2018 Terms said "YOUR CONTINUED USE OF THE SITE AND/OR SERVICES AFTER WE CHANGE THESE TERMS CONSTITUTES YOUR ACCEPTANCE OF THE CHANGES" and "IF YOU DO NOT AGREE TO ANY OF THE CHANGES, YOU MUST CANCEL YOUR ACCOUNT"; StockX proved assent by a Vice President's affidavit that notification emails were sent and that the plaintiffs "have all continued to login to and use their StockX accounts"; since 2015 the terms included "instructions for how to opt out of the arbitration agreement" — [FindLaw](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/us-6th-circuit/2153818.html); [Justia](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca6/21-1089/21-1089-2021-12-02.html)
- Same case, delegation holding: "a party's mere statement that it is challenging the delegation provision is not enough; courts must look to the substance of the challenge"; unconscionability arguments aimed at the arbitration agreement generally did not reach the delegation clause; fraud-in-the-inducement of the whole contract goes to the arbitrator under *Prima Paint* — [FindLaw](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/us-6th-circuit/2153818.html); [McDonald Hopkins](https://www.mcdonaldhopkins.com/insights/news/In-nationwide-data-security-class-action-lawsuit-S)
- *Lozada v. Dale Baker Oldsmobile, Inc.*, 91 F. Supp. 2d 1087 (W.D. Mich. 2000) (pre-*Concepcion*): held the class-remedy waiver in a car-dealer arbitration clause "substantively unconscionable" under Michigan law, stressing that "the contract must adequately and clearly disclose that contractual provisions alter significant statutory rights" and that the clause "makes no disclosures concerning the limitations on remedies and fails to state that the consumer waives either remedy or forum" — [Justia](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/FSupp2/91/1087/2510882/)
- *West v. Legacy Motors, Inc.*, No. 16-12101 (E.D. Mich. Nov. 2, 2016) (Cleland, J.): plaintiff alleged TILA, "the Michigan Consumer Protection Act, Mich. Comp. Laws 445.903, and common-law fraud"; court granted motions to compel arbitration, rejected substantive unconscionability ("a term is substantively unreasonable where the inequity of the term is so extreme as to shock the conscience"), found "Congress has not precluded arbitration of Plaintiff's claims," and dismissed without prejudice — [A&O Shearman archive (PDF)](https://us-arbitration.aoshearman.com/siteFiles/14542/2016.11.2%20West%20v.%20Legacy%20Motors,%202-16-CV-12101%20(E.D.%20Mich.%20Nov.%202,%202016)....pdf)
- Michigan Uniform Arbitration Act, MCL 691.1684: before a controversy arises a party "may not waive or agree to vary the effect of the requirements of section 5(1), 6(1), 8, 17(1) or (2), 26, or 28," may not waive the right under section 16 "to be represented by a lawyer at any proceeding or hearing," and may not unreasonably restrict rights to notice of initiation (section 9) or arbitrator disclosure (section 12) — [legislature.mi.gov](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-691-1684)
- Michigan unconscionability standard (*Clark v. DaimlerChrysler Corp.*, 268 Mich App 138 (2005)): "both procedural and substantive unconscionability must be present"; procedural exists "where the weaker party had no realistic alternative to acceptance of the term"; substantive where "the inequity of the term is so extreme as to shock the conscience" — [FindLaw](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/mi-court-of-appeals/1205678.html). Note: *Clark* was overruled in July 2025 as to shortened limitations periods in employment agreements (see Q3), not as to this general standard — [Rayford opinion (PDF)](https://www.michbar.org/Portals/0/opinions/supreme/2025/073125/84115.pdf)

**Mass arbitration: Heckman and after**
- *Heckman v. Live Nation Entertainment, Inc.*, 120 F.4th 670 (9th Cir. Oct. 28, 2024): affirmed denial of motion to compel arbitration under New Era ADR rules; delegation clause and whole agreement unconscionable under California law; severance refused — [9th Cir. opinion (PDF)](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2024/10/28/23-55770.pdf); [McGlinchey copy with reporter cite](https://www.mcglinchey.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Heckman-v-Live-Nation-Entertainment-Inc.pdf)
- Substantive defects identified: bellwether rulings "binding claimants to bellwether rulings from cases where they had no right or opportunity to participate violates basic principles of due process"; arbitrator selection (New Era could override disqualifications; side-based rather than party-based strikes; one arbitrator over many cases); no discovery and a 10-page complaint limit made the rules "inadequate vehicles for the vindication of plaintiffs' claims"; appeal only from injunctive awards (non-mutual) — [Sidley](https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/newsupdates/2024/10/ninth-circuits-live-nation-decision-evaluates-enforceability-of-mass-arbitration-protocols)
- Procedural defects: terms "may be changed without notice" and "changes may be applied retroactively"; changes effective on posting with continued use as acceptance; arbitration provision applied retroactively to prior purchases — [Sidley](https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/newsupdates/2024/10/ninth-circuits-live-nation-decision-evaluates-enforceability-of-mass-arbitration-protocols); [MSK](https://www.msk.com/newsroom-alerts-heckman-v-live-nation-ninth-circuit-hold-mass-arbitration-clause-to-be-unconscionable)
- FAA-scope holding: "As applied to the Expedited/Mass Arbitration procedures set forth in Ticketmaster's Terms and New Era's Rules, the Discover Bank rule poses no such obstacle, because those procedures do not apply to the forms of arbitration covered by the FAA"; Judge VanDyke's concurrence said mass arbitration lies "outside the bounds of the norm of bilateral arbitration as our precedents conceive of it" — [MSK](https://www.msk.com/newsroom-alerts-heckman-v-live-nation-ninth-circuit-hold-mass-arbitration-clause-to-be-unconscionable)
- Certiorari denied October 6, 2025; the case proceeds as a certified nationwide class with a July 6, 2026 opt-out deadline and a July 2027 trial date (the latter two facts come from a claims-aggregator blog and should be verified on the docket) — [SCOTUSblog](https://www.scotusblog.com/cases/case-files/live-nation-entertainment-inc-v-heckman/); [Daily Journal](https://www.dailyjournal.com/article/387910-high-court-declines-live-nation-bid-to-compel-mass-claims-to-new-era-adr); [MassArbitrationClaims blog](https://www.massarbitrationclaims.com/blog/heckman-live-nation-ticketmaster/)
- *Jones v. Starz Entertainment, LLC* (9th Cir. Feb. 28, 2025): JAMS's consolidation of 7,300 individual arbitrations into one proceeding (which would otherwise have generated over $12 million in initiation fees; consolidated filing fee invoiced at $1,750) is not a "refusal to arbitrate" under the FAA; "[t]he reality is that she agreed to an arbitration that permits consolidation and got what she agreed to"; the argument that the class waiver became unconscionable in a consolidated setting was rejected — [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en-us/insights/publications/2025/03/ninth-circuit-issues-first-opinion-addressing-mass-arbitration); [9th Cir. opinion (PDF)](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2025/02/28/24-1645.pdf)
- *Frazier v. X Corp.* (2d Cir. Sept. 2025): "the payment of fees is merely a procedural issue that must be decided and enforced by an arbitration panel"; a business cannot be compelled under FAA § 4 to pay provider fees as a "failure to arbitrate" — [Privacy World 2025 review](https://www.privacyworld.blog/2025/12/2025-mass-arbitration-year-in-review/)
- *Burkhardt v. Extra Space Mgmt., Inc.*, No. 2:25-cv-00547 (E.D. Cal. July 31, 2025), 2025 WL 2172287: bellwether clause not unconscionable, "particularly where the claimant could have opted out of arbitration upon the determination that a mass arbitration was filed" — [Privacy World](https://www.privacyworld.blog/2025/12/2025-mass-arbitration-year-in-review/)
- *Tercero v. Sacramento Logistics LLC*, No. 2:24-cv-00953 (E.D. Cal. Jan. 7, 2025): bellwether provision with limitations tolling not unconscionable; later vacated for lack of subject-matter jurisdiction (Oct. 3, 2025) — [Privacy World](https://www.privacyworld.blog/2025/12/2025-mass-arbitration-year-in-review/)
- *Rios v. HRB Digital LLC*, No. 25-cv-03530-EMC (N.D. Cal. Oct. 27, 2025) (Chen, J.): H&R Block's bellwether protocol created a "systemic failure" because it provided "no mechanism for relief when unreasonable delays occur"; motion to compel denied — [Privacy World](https://www.privacyworld.blog/2025/12/2025-mass-arbitration-year-in-review/); [Altivor summary](https://altivorconsult.substack.com/p/issue-10-what-h-and-r-block-got-wrong)
- 2024 AAA statistics: 92 mass arbitrations with about 280,000 claims; 1% of consumer and 2% of employment cases ended in awards; 59% of consumer cases settled; first half of 2025: 26 closed mass arbitrations with 37,648+ claimants and zero merits dismissals — [Privacy World](https://www.privacyworld.blog/2025/12/2025-mass-arbitration-year-in-review/)
- Commentary as of spring 2026: courts, providers and litigants "are still grappling with foundational questions, such as what arbitration means in the mass context and whether bellwether and batching procedures facilitate or frustrate dispute resolution" — [ABA Business Lawyer, Spring 2026](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/business_law/resources/business-lawyer/2026-spring/mass-arbitration-update-momentum-maneuvers-mounting-pushback/)

**AAA consumer fees (Costs of Arbitration, amended and effective Aug. 1, 2023, as reproduced in the Feb. 2025 rules copy)**
- Single consumer case: consumer filing fee "capped in the amount of $225"; business filing fee "$375" for one arbitrator or "$500" for three, "payable by the business once the individual claimant meets the filing requirements"; if the business files, business pays "$600 for a single arbitrator or $725 for 3 arbitrators" and "both the Individual's Filing Fee and Business's Filing Fee" — [AAA Consumer Rules (archived PDF)](https://localnewsmatters.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Consumer-Arbitration-Rules.pdf)
- "Case Management Fee: $1,400 for 1 arbitrator or $1,775 for 3 arbitrators will be assessed and must be paid prior to the arbitrator appointment process" — [same](https://localnewsmatters.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Consumer-Arbitration-Rules.pdf)
- "The business shall pay the arbitrator's compensation unless the individual, post dispute, voluntarily elects to pay a portion"; desk/documents-only: "$1,500 per case" (capped at 100 pages and 7 arbitrator hours; beyond that "$300 per hour"); hearing cases: "$2,500 per day of hearing per arbitrator" plus a "$500" hearing fee; "Arbitrator compensation is not subject to reallocation by the arbitrator(s)" — [same](https://localnewsmatters.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Consumer-Arbitration-Rules.pdf)
- Consumer Clause Registry: "the cost of reviewing the clause and maintaining that clause on the Registry is $600. A yearly Registry fee of $600 will be charged"; if a demand arrives on an unregistered clause, "an additional $300 fee for the AAA to conduct an immediate review"; amendments trigger another "$600" review — [same](https://localnewsmatters.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Consumer-Arbitration-Rules.pdf)
- Non-payment: "Where the AAA determines that a business's failure to pay their portion of arbitration costs is a violation of the Consumer Arbitration Rules, the AAA may decline to administer future consumer arbitrations with that business" — [same](https://localnewsmatters.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Consumer-Arbitration-Rules.pdf)
- Mass-arbitration per-case business filing fees (same schedule): "$325, $250, $175, or $100 per case depending on tier"; individual "$125 or $75 per case depending on tier" — [same](https://localnewsmatters.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Consumer-Arbitration-Rules.pdf)
- AAA Mass Arbitration Supplementary Rules and fee schedule effective January 15, 2024: initiation fee $3,125 (individuals) / $8,125 (business) per mass arbitration (not per claimant), credited toward per-case fees; per-case fees $325 (cases 1–500), $250 (501–1,500), $175 (1,501–3,000), $100 (3,001+); merits-arbitrator appointment $450 business / $50 individual; rank/process appointment $600 business / $75 individual; "Final Fee: $600 per case"; merits arbitrators $300/hour; process arbitrator/mediator at market rates; AAA-administered mediation "$3,250 plus $2,500 every six months the case is open"; appointment fees "non-refundable in the event of settlement or withdrawal"; process arbitrator may decide conditions precedent and administrative disputes — [Fenwick (Feb. 5, 2024)](https://www.fenwick.com/insights/publications/aaa-issues-updates-to-consumer-mass-arbitration-rules-and-fee-schedule); [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2024/02/american-arbitration-association-adopts-new-mass-arbitration-rules-and-fee-schedules); [AAA press release](https://www.adr.org/press-releases/aaa-announces-updated-mass-arbitration-supplementary-rules/)
- AAA Consumer Rules amendments effective May 1, 2025: hearings virtual by default; arbitrators may sanction; claims under $25,000 resolved on documents "unless [1] a party requests hearing and [2] arbitrator decides hearing necessary"; AAA discretion to consolidate claims "arising out of same contract as single case"; consumer appeals under new Rule 58; AAA may publish redacted awards — [Privacy World](https://www.privacyworld.blog/2025/12/2025-mass-arbitration-year-in-review/)
- JAMS Mass Arbitration Procedures (effective May 1, 2024): "75 or more similar Demands for Arbitration" by coordinated counsel; flat filing fee "$7,500, regardless of the number of cases"; in consumer cases "the most that the consumers, in the aggregate, can be required to pay is $2,500" — [Duane Morris](https://www.duanemorris.com/alerts/2024_sees_newly_enacted_aaa_jams_arbitration_rules_0724.html); [JAMS](https://www.jamsadr.com/mass-arbitration-procedures); [Skadden](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2024/05/jams-adopts-mass-arbitration-procedures-and-guidelines)

**Opt-out clauses**
- *Mohamed v. Uber Technologies, Inc.*, 836 F.3d 1102 (9th Cir. 2016): an agreement "is not adhesive, and thus not procedurally unconscionable, if a party has a meaningful chance to opt out, even if the method is burdensome"; a delegation clause that is clear and unmistakable must be enforced — [FindLaw](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/us-9th-circuit/1761734.html); [Eric Goldman](https://blog.ericgoldman.org/archives/2016/09/ninth-circuit-sends-uber-driver-claims-to-arbitration.htm)
- *Burkhardt* (E.D. Cal. 2025) credited an opt-out triggered by a mass-arbitration determination in upholding a bellwether clause — [Privacy World](https://www.privacyworld.blog/2025/12/2025-mass-arbitration-year-in-review/)
- StockX's terms carried opt-out instructions since 2015 and were enforced — [FindLaw](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/us-6th-circuit/2153818.html)
- Plaintiff-side commentary describes 30-day opt-outs as a "trap" that courts credit against unconscionability — [Plaintiff Magazine](https://plaintiffmagazine.com/recent-issues/item/the-opt-out-trap); [Fox Rothschild](https://www.foxrothschild.com/publications/can-opt-out-provisions-save-arbitration-clauses)

**Class waivers outside arbitration**
- *Pace v. Hamilton Cove* (N.J. 2024): stand-alone class waivers in consumer contracts without an arbitration clause "are not per se contrary to public policy," though they may be unenforceable if unconscionable; the Appellate Division had held such waivers unenforceable because the FAA and *Concepcion* are "irrelevant" absent arbitration — [Consumer Finance Monitor](https://www.consumerfinancemonitor.com/2024/12/31/new-jersey-supreme-court-enforces-stand-alone-class-action-waiver/); [Kilpatrick](https://ktslaw.com/en/Blog/classaction/2024/12/New-Jersey-Supreme-Court-affirms-class-action-waiver-untethered-to-an-arbitration-agreement)
- Rhode Island's Supreme Court refused a stand-alone waiver because its Deceptive Trade Practices Act authorizes representative actions; Arkansas upheld one; California "may" refuse under certain conditions — [Florida Bar Journal](https://www.floridabar.org/the-florida-bar-journal/are-arbitration-agreements-necessary-for-class-action-waivers-to-be-enforceable/); [Mitchell Williams](https://www.mitchellwilliamslaw.com/arkansas-supreme-court-upholds-validity-of-class-action-waiver-clause-even-in-absence-of-arbitration-clause)
- *Killion v. KeHE Distributors, LLC*, 761 F.3d 574 (6th Cir. 2014), cert. denied Apr. 6, 2015: FLSA collective-action waiver in a separation agreement with no arbitration clause is unenforceable; the Sixth Circuit held employees may waive collective procedures only "so long as they retain the right to individually arbitrate" — [FindLaw](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/us-6th-circuit/1674682.html); [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=2b2712aa-2421-4938-bc9a-b6f36a5c7e13)
- MCPA remedies: a person may "recover actual damages or $250.00, whichever is greater, together with reasonable attorney fees"; the statute separately authorizes consumer class actions on behalf of Michigan residents — [legislature.mi.gov (MCL 445.911)](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-445-911)
- MCL 445.903(1)(t) makes it an unfair practice to enter "a consumer transaction in which the consumer waives or purports to waive a right, benefit, or immunity provided by law, unless the waiver is clearly stated and the consumer has specifically consented to it" — [legislature.mi.gov](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-445-903)

### Inferences
- **Arbitration is a fee-exposure device against a tiny vendor, not a shield.** Using the schedule above, a single AAA desk arbitration over a $30 license costs the vendor at least $375 + $1,400 + $1,500 = $3,275 in non-reallocable fees before any lawyer time, plus $600 registry and $600/yr; a one-day hearing case is at least $375 + $1,400 + $2,500 + $500 = $4,775. Ten coordinated demands are already a mass arbitration under AAA's fee tiers (per-case fees of $325 + $450 + $600 plus the $8,125 initiation). *Frazier* and *Jones v. Starz* mean a court will not police the fee dispute for the vendor, and the AAA may blacklist a non-paying business. This is the plaintiffs' bar's leverage that produced 280,000 claims in 2024.
- **Mass-arbitration "protection" is legally the weakest part of the clause.** Heckman-style bellwether/batching terms are exactly what got the whole clause and the class waiver thrown out; even the pro-business 2025 district rulings (*Burkhardt*, *Tercero*) leaned on an opt-out at the point of mass filing, and *Rios* struck a protocol with no delay-relief mechanism. For this vendor, a bespoke mass protocol adds unconscionability risk without a realistic upside: the cheaper defenses are (i) provider rules already permitting consolidation (*Jones v. Starz* shows JAMS consolidated 7,300 claims for $1,750) and (ii) a small-claims carve-out.
- **Recommendation matrix (interpretation, for counsel to weigh):**
  - *Option A — AAA/JAMS arbitration + class waiver + delegation.* Pros: FAA preemption of hostile state rules; class actions barred with strong authority (Concepcion/Epic/StockX); MCPA claims arbitrable (*West*). Cons: per-claim business cost $3,275–$4,775+ (AAA) on a $30 product; registry $600 + $600/yr; mass filings scale linearly; provider may refuse to administer if unpaid; the vendor cannot itself use small claims against students in any meaningful way.
  - *Option B — exclusive Michigan state/federal forum + jury waiver + class waiver + small-claims permission + notice-and-cure.* Pros: zero provider fees; the student's realistic path is small claims (limit $7,000) where lawyers rarely appear; Michigan will enforce forum and jury waivers between contracting parties (Q4). Cons: a stand-alone class waiver has **no Michigan authority** either way; *Killion* shows the Sixth Circuit's willingness to void a stand-alone collective waiver where a statute authorizes group procedures, and the MCPA does authorize class actions — a court could treat the waiver as an MCPA-445.903(1)(t) "waiver of a right provided by law" requiring clear statement and specific consent, and, without FAA preemption, could apply Michigan unconscionability directly. Out-of-state students may resist the Michigan forum on § 187 grounds.
  - *Option C (hybrid) — arbitration clause that the consumer may opt out of within 30 days, with small-claims carve-out for either side, informal notice-and-cure, and a delegation clause; no bellwether/batching protocol; class waiver stated to survive if arbitration is opted out of or invalidated, severable.* This captures the opt-out's procedural-unconscionability benefit (*Mohamed*, StockX) and lets the vendor decide case-by-case whether to invoke arbitration (an arbitration agreement is a right the vendor may waive by litigating). The fee exposure exists only if the vendor or the student actually invokes arbitration.
- Delegation clause drafting (from Rent-A-Center, Henry Schein, Blanton, StockX, Suski): state expressly that "the arbitrator, and not any court, shall have exclusive authority to resolve any dispute relating to the interpretation, applicability, enforceability, formation, scope or validity of this Agreement to Arbitrate, including any claim that all or any part of it is void or voidable"; incorporate the AAA Consumer Rules by name (independent delegation evidence under *Blanton*); make the delegation a separately headed paragraph; ensure no other document (checkout page, Marketplace listing, refund policy) contains a conflicting forum clause (*Suski*).
- Michigan-law drafting caution from *Lozada*: whatever forum is chosen, say in plain words that the consumer is giving up the right to sue in court (other than small claims), to a jury, and to participate in a class action, and that the MCPA and other statutory remedies remain available individually — Michigan's own unconscionability cases penalize non-disclosure of altered statutory rights, and MCL 445.903(1)(t) requires the waiver to be "clearly stated" with specific consent.
- Do not draft any term that purports to bar EFAA-covered claims; state that the arbitration agreement does not apply where prohibited by federal law.

### Gaps

## Q2. Pre-suit informal dispute resolution / notice-and-cure (30–60 days)

### Takeaway
Courts enforce mandatory, clearly drafted multi-step clauses as conditions precedent — dismissing or staying claims filed without the required negotiation/mediation step — but when an arbitration clause is present the question whether the precondition was satisfied is presumptively for the arbitrator (and, under AAA's mass rules, for a Process Arbitrator). No Michigan consumer-software decision was found; the enforceable model is the language large platforms now use (personal written notice, 60-day negotiation, tolling, court power to enjoin non-compliant arbitrations).

### Cited Findings
- *Lange v. Schilling* (Cal.): "Failures to meet clear and unambiguous condition precedents are not excused"; *Golden State Foods Corp. v. Columbia/Okura LLC* (Cal.): summary adjudication because "Plaintiff failed to satisfy the condition precedent of submitting its claims to mediation," leaving the party "without recourse"; *Archstone Dev. LLC v. Renval Construction LLC* (N.Y.): claim dismissed where plaintiff "did not pursue mediation as required"; *Ferguson Elec. Co. v. Kendal at Ithaca* (N.Y.): enforcement requires "clear, explicitly and unequivocal intent"; *Patterson v. Melman* (Fla. 2d DCA): arbitrator decides whether a mediation precondition was met, and a curable failure "may not constitute waiver" — [Orrick (Jan. 2025)](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2025/01/Tiered-Dispute-Resolution-Clauses-Navigating-Pitfalls-in-the-US-and-UK)
- Orrick's drafting recommendations: clear language making the tiers a condition precedent; "defined, objectively identifiable trigger points"; "realistic timelines"; identified responsible individuals; narrow carve-outs for urgent interim relief; no contradictory steps across tiers — [Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2025/01/Tiered-Dispute-Resolution-Clauses-Navigating-Pitfalls-in-the-US-and-UK)
- A county court dismissed a complaint where neither party had complied with the contract's claims/dispute procedure, noting the right to arbitrate would be waived if conditions precedent were not followed — [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=d87e9d5f-b7f9-491e-bf61-9f2c9e024e14)
- Under the AAA Mass Arbitration Supplementary Rules a Process Arbitrator has authority to determine "satisfaction of filing requirements, disputes over applicable conditions precedent, and other issues submitted" — [Cleary Gottlieb](https://www.clearygottlieb.com/news-and-insights/publication-listing/state-and-federal-court-decisions-address-recurring-issues-in-consumer-arbitration); [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2024/02/american-arbitration-association-adopts-new-mass-arbitration-rules-and-fee-schedules)
- Amazon's current terms: "contacting customer service and completing the Notice of Dispute, the 60-day negotiating period, and any timely requested settlement conference are prerequisites to filing an arbitration"; "the Notice of Dispute must be personally signed by you, completed accurately and in full, accompanied by documents reasonably necessary to support your claim"; "a court can enjoin the initiation or proceeding of an arbitration in breach of these pre-arbitration dispute resolution requirements" — [Amazon Pre-Arbitration Dispute Resolution Procedure](https://www.amazon.com/gp/help/customer/display.html?nodeId=T3eg9AqRtYxwA0PxpP)
- Uber's terms: "before either party demands arbitration against the other, you and Uber will personally meet and confer, via telephone or videoconference, in a good-faith effort to resolve informally any claim" — [Uber Terms](https://www.uber.com/us/en/legal/general-terms-of-use/)
- NAM-administered consumer clauses provide that limitations periods and filing-fee deadlines are tolled during informal resolution, that a court "may enjoin the filing or prosecution of an arbitration" begun without compliance, and that the administrator "may not accept, administer, assess, or demand fees" for such an arbitration — [NAM sample clause (Lose It)](https://www.namadr.com/content/uploads/2024/03/Lose-It-Arbitration-Agmt-2024.pdf); [NAM sample clause (Melio)](https://www.namadr.com/content/uploads/2024/11/Melio-Arbitration-Clause-NAM.pdf)
- Where a stay rather than dismissal is entered under FAA § 3, the consumer may return to court if the business fails to pay fees or participate — [NCLC](https://library.nclc.org/article/new-supreme-court-arbitration-ruling-double-edged-sword)

### Inferences
- A notice-and-cure step is the cheapest and least legally risky of the mechanisms requested: it costs nothing, is consistently enforced when drafted as an express condition precedent, and functions in either forum option. Its practical value for Ashley is that most complaints (license key failure, university block, upstream API change) can be cured with a refund or fix inside the window, converting a potential claim into a closed ticket.
- Suggested elements (drafting, for counsel): (1) "Before filing any claim in any forum, the claiming party must send a written Notice of Dispute to [email/postal address] containing the person's name, account email, install ID, a description of the problem and the specific relief requested, personally signed"; (2) "The parties will attempt in good faith to resolve the dispute for 60 days after the Notice is received; either may request one video or telephone conference"; (3) "Completion of this process is a condition precedent to any arbitration or lawsuit, other than a small-claims action"; (4) "All limitations periods and fee deadlines are tolled while this process is pending"; (5) "A court may enjoin any proceeding begun in violation of this section"; (6) a mirror obligation on the vendor. Use "must," name the recipient, and avoid open-ended "attempt to resolve" language (*Ferguson*, Orrick).
- Because the arbitrator decides procedural preconditions once the agreement is otherwise valid (*Patterson*; AAA process arbitrator), the clause should say that the sufficiency of a Notice and compliance with the informal step may be decided by a court at either party's election if the vendor wants a judge, not the arbitrator, to police it (this is how the NAM clauses are drafted).

### Gaps
- No reported Michigan consumer-contract case applying a notice-and-cure condition precedent to dismiss or stay a suit was found; the decisions above are California, New York and Florida commercial/consumer cases summarized by a law firm rather than read in full.
- Whether the informal step can be made a condition precedent to a small-claims filing without itself being challenged as a barrier to statutory remedies was not addressed in any source found.

## Q3. Contractual limitation periods under Michigan law (Rory and progeny; MCPA; UCC 2-725; 2020–2026 decisions)

### Takeaway
*Rory* (2005) holds that an unambiguous shortened limitations clause "is to be enforced as written unless the provision would violate law or public policy," rejecting judicial "reasonableness" review and special treatment of adhesion contracts; but on July 31, 2025 the Michigan Supreme Court in *Rayford v. American House Roseville I, LLC* overruled *Clark* and *Timko*, held that adhesive boilerplate employment agreements shortening limitations periods "must be examined for reasonableness" under the three-part *Camelot* test and are also subject to unconscionability review, and characterized *Rory* as an insurance case whose reach was limited by the Insurance Commissioner's review. The majority said its decision "is focused on boilerplate employment agreements"; the dissent said it is "unclear ... what 'close judicial scrutiny' ... might mean in other contexts." No Michigan decision on shortening the MCPA's six-year period by contract was found.

### Cited Findings
- *Rory v. Continental Insurance Co.*, 473 Mich 457; 703 NW2d 23 (2005): "an unambiguous contractual provision providing for a shortened period of limitations is to be enforced as written unless the provision would violate law or public policy"; "A mere judicial assessment of 'reasonableness' is an invalid basis upon which to refuse to enforce contractual provisions"; "it is of no legal relevance that a contract is or is not described as 'adhesive.' In either case, the contract is to be enforced according to its plain language"; the suit absent the clause "would be governed by the general six-year period of limitations applicable to contract actions" — [FindLaw](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/mi-supreme-court/1266749.html); [Casemine](https://www.casemine.com/judgement/us/5914b61dadd7b04934776fcc)
- *Rayford v. American House Roseville I, LLC*, No. 163989 (Mich. July 31, 2025) (Welch, J., for the Court; Zahra, J., dissenting; Hood, J., not participating): "We hold that an adhesive boilerplate employment agreement that shortens a limitations period must be examined for reasonableness. Additionally, these agreements are subject to traditional contract defenses, including unconscionability, and, as adhesion contracts, may be procedurally and substantively unconscionable"; "we overrule the Court of Appeals' decision in Clark v DaimlerChrysler Corp ... which extended this Court's decision in Rory ... to employment agreements"; "We also overrule Timko v Oakwood Custom Coating, Inc" — [Opinion (PDF)](https://www.michbar.org/Portals/0/opinions/supreme/2025/073125/84115.pdf); [courts.michigan.gov copy](https://www.courts.michigan.gov/4a4c3b/siteassets/case-documents/uploads/opinions/final/sct/163989_73_01.pdf)
- The test: "Camelot's reasonableness test requires [1] that the claimant have sufficient opportunity to investigate and file an action, [2] that the time not be so short as to work a practical abrogation of the right of action, and [3] that the action not be barred before the loss or damage can be ascertained. [Camelot, 410 Mich at 127.]" — [Rayford opinion](https://www.michbar.org/Portals/0/opinions/supreme/2025/073125/84115.pdf)
- Scope statements: footnote 13: "It is significant that Rory involved an insurance dispute governed by an insurance policy ... In Michigan, the Legislature has statutorily empowered a commissioner to review insurance contracts for reasonableness ... Thus, Rory's review of the insurance contract at issue was curtailed by the commissioner's approval of the policy. Such is not the case for employment contracts"; footnote 18: "our decision is focused on boilerplate employment agreements where the ability to change or reject offered terms in a contract is effectively curtailed by its adhesive nature--take the contract as-is or lose the job" — [Rayford opinion](https://www.michbar.org/Portals/0/opinions/supreme/2025/073125/84115.pdf)
- Dissent (Zahra, J.): the majority "resurrects the 'reasonableness' requirement for contractual limitations periods, a requirement that we rejected two decades ago in Rory"; "It remains unclear, however, what 'close judicial scrutiny'--an undefined and vague standard now resurrected by the Court--might mean in other contexts" — [Rayford opinion](https://www.michbar.org/Portals/0/opinions/supreme/2025/073125/84115.pdf)
- Law-firm reading: courts will now analyze "when the employee discovered they had a potential claim ..., when they learned of the contractually shortened limitations period, the steps they needed to take to file a claim, how long those steps usually take, and when they were able to assess their damages" — [Miller Johnson](https://millerjohnson.com/publication/michigan-supreme-court-contractually-shortened-limitations-periods/); [Honigman](https://www.honigman.com/alert-3058)
- *Liparoto Construction, Inc. v. General Shale Brick, Inc.* (Mich. App. May 21, 2009) (published): one-year limitations period in a brick supplier's invoice enforced against a UCC warranty claim otherwise governed by the four-year period of MCL 440.2725, applying *Rory*: "an unambiguous contractual provision providing for a shortened limitations period is to be enforced as written unless the provision violates the law or public policy or is otherwise unenforceable under traditional contract defenses" — [FindLaw](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/mi-court-of-appeals/1207327.html); [Justia](https://law.justia.com/cases/michigan/court-of-appeals-published/2009/20090521-c282920-50-88o-282920.html)
- MCPA limitations: an action may not be brought "more than 6 years after the occurrence" of the violation or "more than 1 year after the last payment" in the transaction, "whichever period of time ends at a later date" (the legislature page as fetched places this language in a later-numbered subsection than the "(7)" cited in older sources; confirm numbering) — [legislature.mi.gov (MCL 445.911)](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-445-911)
- The Sixth Circuit had applied Michigan's *Camelot* standard to uphold a six-month contractual period in an employment discrimination case (*Myers v. Western-Southern Life Ins. Co.*), as recounted in *Rayford* — [Rayford opinion](https://www.michbar.org/Portals/0/opinions/supreme/2025/073125/84115.pdf)

### Inferences
- The safe reading after *Rayford*: a one-year contractual period in a consumer clickwrap is still presumptively enforceable under *Rory*/*Liparoto*, but a Michigan court now has Supreme Court language inviting *Camelot* reasonableness review of any "adhesive boilerplate" contract, and the majority's reasons (no negotiation, weaker party, no regulator's approval) apply at least as strongly to a $30 student license as to an employment acknowledgment. Expect a plaintiff to argue *Rayford* extends to consumer adhesion contracts; expect the vendor to argue footnote 18 confines it to employment. This is a live "counsel must decide" point.
- A one-year period passes the *Camelot* factors far more comfortably than 180 days (the period *Herweyer* implied was unreasonable and *Rayford* remanded). For UCC warranty claims, MCL 440.2725(1) itself permits the parties to reduce the four-year period "to not less than one year" (statutory text not re-fetched; see Gaps) so one year is the floor if Article 2 applies to the license.
- For MCPA claims, no Michigan case was found either enforcing or refusing a contractual shortening of the six-year period. Two structural obstacles: MCL 445.903(1)(t) (a waiver of "a right, benefit, or immunity provided by law" is itself an unfair practice unless "clearly stated" and "specifically consented to"), and *Rayford*'s reasonableness framework for adhesive terms. Drafting choice: either (a) exempt statutory consumer-protection claims from the shortened period and apply one year only to contract/warranty claims, or (b) apply one year to all claims "to the fullest extent permitted by law" with a separate, specifically acknowledged consent line. Option (a) is lower-risk; option (b) buys more but invites the (1)(t) argument.
- Any shortened period should run from when the claim "accrues" and should be tolled during the notice-and-cure window (Q2), which directly addresses *Camelot* factors [1] and [3].

### Gaps
- No Michigan decision (2005–2026) addressing whether a contractual limitations clause can shorten the MCPA's six-year period was found.
- No Michigan appellate decision applying *Rayford* outside employment was found as of September 2026 (the opinion is 14 months old).
- The text of MCL 440.2725(1) ("may reduce the period of limitation to not less than one year") was not re-fetched; it is cited here from the general UCC and should be confirmed at legislature.mi.gov.

## Q4. Pre-dispute jury trial waivers in Michigan

### Takeaway
Michigan courts enforce clear contractual jury waivers as written (*Barton-Spencer*, Mich. 2017: "fixed by the court" was an express waiver, no magic words required; *Egan v. Prop Services*, Mich. App. 2018: courts "are not permitted to override a contractual jury trial waiver"), and federal courts in Michigan apply the Sixth Circuit's "knowing, voluntary and intentional" standard with the burden on the party resisting the waiver (*K.M.C. Co. v. Irving Trust*). Conspicuousness is the factor the vendor controls: a stand-alone, headed, capitalized paragraph with its own checkbox.

### Cited Findings
- *K.M.C. Co. v. Irving Trust Co.*, 757 F.2d 752, 755–58 (6th Cir. 1985), as applied in *Chesterfield Exchange, LLC v. Sportsman's Warehouse, Inc.*, No. 07-12254 (E.D. Mich.) (Lawson, J.): the right to a jury "is governed by federal law, not state law" in federal court even in diversity; "the constitutional right to jury trial may only be waived if done knowingly, voluntarily and intentionally"; "in the context of an express contractual [jury] waiver the objecting party should have the burden of demonstrating that its consent to the provisions was not knowing and voluntary" (noting the Fourth Circuit places the burden the other way, *Leasing Serv. Corp. v. Crane*, 804 F.2d 828, 833 (4th Cir. 1986)); a general fraud-in-the-inducement allegation does not defeat the waiver absent a "well-founded claim of fraud in the inducement of the clause itself, standing apart from the whole agreement" (*Moses v. Business Card Express*, 929 F.2d 1131, 1138 (6th Cir. 1991)); motion to strike jury demand granted — [E.D. Mich. opinion (PDF)](https://www.mied.uscourts.gov/PDFFIles/07-12254%20Chestefield%20Opinion%20Striking%20Jury%20Demand.pdf)
- Factors courts use for "knowing and voluntary": "(1) whether both parties had an opportunity to negotiate the terms of the agreement, (2) whether the provision waiving jury trial was conspicuous, (3) the relative bargaining power of the parties, and (4) the business acumen or professional experience of the party opposing the waiver"; a waiver "is more conspicuous if it is contained in a paragraph of its own, instead of being included within a general provision addressing the waiver of various other rights"; the Michigan Court of Appeals has said there is no basis to treat jury waivers under Michigan law differently from federal law — [Michigan Bar Journal (May 2004)](https://www.michbar.org/file/barjournal/article/documents/pdf4article692.pdf); [Vaquill state survey](https://www.vaquill.ai/blog/jury-trial-waivers-enforceability-by-state)
- *Barton-Spencer v. Farm Bureau Life Insurance Co. of Michigan*, Nos. 153655 & 153656 (Mich. Apr. 19, 2017) (unanimous, without oral argument): a contract requiring reimbursement of "attorney fees and costs as may be fixed by the court" was an express jury waiver as to that issue; "the word 'court,' which refers in common parlance ... to judges, as opposed to juries"; no requirement of words like "jury," "jury trial" or "waive"; "fixed by the court is not ambiguous" — [Warner Norcross](https://www.wnj.com/updates/msc-contracting-parties-waive-their-right-to-jury-trial-where-contract-requires-an-issue-to-be-fixed-by-the-court/)
- *Egan v. Prop Services* (Mich. App. June 2018, No. 336358): "unambiguous contracts are not open to judicial construction and must be enforced as written"; courts "are not permitted to override a contractual jury trial waiver" even where a non-party co-defendant retains a jury right; reversed and remanded for bench trial — [Demorest Law](https://detroitbusinesslaw.com/2018/09/04/the-michigan-court-of-appeals-holds-that-courts-cannot-override-a-contractual-jury-trial-waiver/)
- The Sixth Circuit has also invoked the *K.M.C.* knowing-and-voluntary standard when evaluating arbitration clauses (*Walker v. Ryan's Family Steak Houses*, 400 F.3d 370, 381 (6th Cir. 2005)) — [E.D. Mich. opinion](https://www.mied.uscourts.gov/PDFFIles/07-12254%20Chestefield%20Opinion%20Striking%20Jury%20Demand.pdf)

### Inferences
- In a consumer clickwrap, factors (1), (3) and (4) will cut against the vendor; the waiver will stand or fall on conspicuousness and on the assent record (Q8). Draft it as its own numbered section headed "WAIVER OF JURY TRIAL," in capitals or bold, one or two sentences, mutual ("each party"), with its own checkbox or a combined "I have read the Dispute Resolution, Class Waiver and Jury Waiver sections" checkbox positioned adjacent to the button, and log the checkbox event.
- If arbitration is chosen (Option A/C in Q1), the jury waiver is inherent in arbitration but should still be stated separately so that it survives if the arbitration agreement is opted out of or invalidated.
- MCL 445.903(1)(t) applies here too: a jury right is "a right ... provided by law"; the waiver must be "clearly stated" and "specifically consented to."

### Gaps
- No published Michigan Court of Appeals decision applying the four-factor "knowing and voluntary" test to a *consumer* online contract was found; the 2022 COA opinion surfaced by search (No. 353828) turned out to be a criminal case and was discarded.
- The Michigan constitutional and court-rule basis (Const 1963, art 1, § 14; MCR 2.508) was not fetched.

## Q5. Assumption of risk, release and waiver of claims in a consumer software EULA under Michigan law

### Takeaway
Michigan abolished assumption of risk as a tort defense outside employment and express contract (*Felgner*, 1965), so the clause functions as an express contractual release plus a disclosure. Releases are enforced for ordinary negligence but not for gross negligence or willful and wanton misconduct (*Xu v. Gay*, 2003; *Swanson v. Bittersweet Ski Resort*, Mich. App. Jan. 9, 2026), cannot be signed for a minor by a parent (*Woodman v. Kera*, 2010), and in a consumer transaction are subject to MCL 445.903(1)(t)'s "clearly stated and ... specifically consented" requirement.

### Cited Findings
- *Felgner v. Anderson*, 375 Mich 23; 133 NW2d 136 (1965): assumption of risk is confined to "cases between employee and employer for injuries incurred in the course of employment"; the defense is abolished "except in cases involving an employment relationship between the parties or an express contractual assumption of risk" — [Justia](https://law.justia.com/cases/michigan/supreme-court/1965/375-mich-23-2.html); [CaseMine commentary](https://www.casemine.com/commentary/us/restricting-the-doctrine-of-assumption-of-risk-to-employment-relationships-in-michigan:-felgner-v.-anderson/view)
- *Xu v. Gay*, 257 Mich App 263; 668 NW2d 166 (2003): a contractual waiver of liability "insulates against ordinary negligence, but not gross negligence"; gross negligence is "conduct so reckless as to demonstrate a substantial lack of concern for whether an injury results"; evidence of ordinary negligence "does not create a question of fact regarding gross negligence" — [vLex](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/xu-v-gay-docket-887015981); [CourtListener](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1616125/xu-v-gay/)
- Willful and wanton misconduct is established "if the conduct alleged shows an intent to harm or, if not that, such indifference to whether harm will result as to be the equivalent of a willingness that it does" — [Michigan Legislative Council, Gross Negligence in Michigan](https://council.legislature.mi.gov/Content/Files/mlrc/1996/gross1.htm)
- *Swanson v. Bittersweet Ski Resort, Inc.* (Mich. App. Jan. 9, 2026): liability waivers may bar ordinary-negligence claims but not gross negligence; public policy prohibits waivers absolving a defendant from conduct "so reckless as to demonstrate a substantial lack of concern for whether an injury results" — [Kallas & Henk](https://www.kallashenk.com/michigan-court-of-appeals-draws-a-clear-line-on-liability-waivers-and-gross-negligence); [Speaker Law](https://www.speakerlaw.com/blog/court-of-appeals-holds-ski-area-safety-act-does-not-preempt-gross-negligence-claims)
- *Woodman v. Kera LLC*, 486 Mich 228; 785 NW2d 1 (2010): under Michigan common law a minor lacks capacity to enter a pre-injury waiver and a parent cannot bind the child to one; a minor's contract is voidable at the minor's election — [FindLaw](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/mi-supreme-court/1527931.html); [Opinion (PDF)](https://www.courts.michigan.gov/4a246c/siteassets/case-documents/uploads/opinions/final/sct/20100618_s137347_74_woodman-op.pdf)
- MCL 445.903(1)(t): unfair practice to enter "a consumer transaction in which the consumer waives or purports to waive a right, benefit, or immunity provided by law, unless the waiver is clearly stated and the consumer has specifically consented to it"; (1)(n): "Causing a probability of confusion or of misunderstanding as to the legal rights, obligations, or remedies of a party to a transaction"; (1)(s): "Failing to reveal a material fact, the omission of which tends to mislead or deceive the consumer, and which fact could not reasonably be known by the consumer"; (1)(p) addresses disclaiming implied warranties without clear disclosure; last amended by 2022 PA 152, eff. Jan. 1, 2023 — [legislature.mi.gov](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-445-903)
- MCL 440.2719(3): "Limitation of consequential damages for injury to the person in the case of consumer goods is prima facie unconscionable but limitation of damages where the loss is commercial is not" — [legislature.mi.gov](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-440-2719)

### Inferences
- What the clause can do in Michigan: (i) allocate to the student the economic risk of identified external events (a university IT department blocking the extension or the grid; Anthropic changing its API, model availability, pricing or usage policies; Microsoft removing or de-listing the extension from the VS Code Marketplace; loss of student status), (ii) release ordinary-negligence and contract claims arising from those events, and (iii) create a record that the risks were disclosed (which also blunts MCPA (1)(s)/(cc) omission theories). What it cannot do: release gross negligence, willful misconduct, fraud, or statutory rights that are non-waivable; bind a minor.
- Drafting model consistent with (1)(t) and *Lozada*'s disclosure emphasis: a separately headed section "Known Risks You Accept" listing each named dependency in plain language with the consequence ("Ashley may stop working, in whole or part, without notice, and your sole remedy is [refund of the unused portion of the license fee / the remedy in § X]"), followed by a separate affirmative act (checkbox: "I understand and accept these specific risks"), logged with timestamp and terms version. State expressly that nothing releases liability for gross negligence, willful misconduct or fraud, or limits rights that cannot be limited by law — this avoids the over-breadth that invites a court to strike the whole section (see *Heckman*'s refusal to sever).
- Because Ashley is delivered to students (some under 18), pair the risk section with the age attestation in Q9; a parental signature does not help under *Woodman*.

### Gaps
- No Michigan decision applying an exculpatory clause to a *software* or purely economic-loss consumer transaction was found; the release cases are recreational-injury cases.
- The exact text of MCL 445.903(1)(p) (implied-warranty disclaimer disclosure) was reported only in summary by the fetch and should be quoted from the statute before drafting the warranty section.

## Q6. Fee-shifting: prevailing-party attorney fee clauses in Michigan consumer contracts

### Takeaway
Michigan enforces contractual attorney-fee provisions as an exception to the American rule, subject to reasonableness (*Zeeland Farm Services*, 1996; *Fleet Business Credit v. Krapohl Ford*, 2007). The MCPA gives prevailing consumers "reasonable attorney fees" by statute; a one-way vendor-favoring clause invites an unconscionability attack under the two-prong Michigan test and a (1)(t) argument, so a mutual, reasonable-fee clause is the defensible form.

### Cited Findings
- Michigan follows the American rule; "One commonly recognized exception is where the parties have contractually stipulated to the payment of reasonable attorney fees"; contractual provisions "for payment of reasonable attorney fees are judicially enforceable"; the claimant bears the burden of proving reasonableness — [Michigan Judicial Institute Civil Benchbook, Attorney Fees](https://www.courts.michigan.gov/490820/siteassets/publications/benchbooks/civil/civilresponsivehtml5.zip/Civil/Ch_8_Posttrial_Proceedings/Attorney_Fees.htm)
- *Fleet Business Credit, LLC v. Krapohl Ford Lincoln Mercury Co.*, 274 Mich App 584; 735 NW2d 644 (2007) and *Zeeland Farm Services, Inc. v. JBL Enterprises, Inc.*, 219 Mich App 190; 555 NW2d 733 (1996) are the leading Court of Appeals authorities enforcing contractual fee clauses — [Butzel Long](https://www.butzel.com/alert-Yes-Attorneys-Fees-Clauses-are-Enforceable-in-Non-Compete-Cases); [Plunkett Cooney](https://www.plunkettcooney.com/dontbetthebusinessblog/paying-attorney-fees)
- *Pransky v. Falcon Group, Inc.* (Mich. App. 2015): defendant prevailed on a contract with a prevailing-party clause and was awarded fees; courts sometimes borrow MCL 600.2591's definition of "prevailing party" ("a party who wins on the entire record") — [Plunkett Cooney](https://www.plunkettcooney.com/dontbetthebusinessblog/paying-attorney-fees)
- MCPA: a person who suffers loss may "recover actual damages or $250.00, whichever is greater, together with reasonable attorney fees" — [legislature.mi.gov (MCL 445.911)](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-445-911)
- Michigan unconscionability requires both procedural and substantive unconscionability (*Clark*; *West v. Legacy Motors* quoting "so extreme as to shock the conscience") — [FindLaw](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/mi-court-of-appeals/1205678.html); [West (PDF)](https://us-arbitration.aoshearman.com/siteFiles/14542/2016.11.2%20West%20v.%20Legacy%20Motors,%202-16-CV-12101%20(E.D.%20Mich.%20Nov.%202,%202016)....pdf)
- Scholarship on unilateral fee clauses documents that many states blue-pencil one-way clauses into reciprocal ones by statute (e.g., California) and that consumer-contract one-way clauses draw unconscionability scrutiny — [Drake Law Review](https://drakelawreview.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/06/lrvol61-1-bright.pdf); [RWO Law](https://rwolaw.com/when-do-unilateral-attorneys-fees-provisions-become-mutual/)
- A 2024 proposal to amend Michigan Court Rule 2.110 concerning prevailing-party attorney fees was circulated by the State Bar (status not determined) — [State Bar of Michigan proposal (PDF)](https://www.michbar.org/Portals/0/generalinfo/pdfs/4-20-24_prevailing-party_proposal.pdf)

### Inferences
- Practical reality: fee-shifting against a student is rarely collectible and, in a small-claims posture, rarely awarded; its main value is deterrence of nuisance suits and a counterweight to the MCPA's one-way fee provision. A mutual prevailing-party clause capped at "reasonable" fees, expressly not applying to small-claims matters and not displacing statutory fee awards, is the form least likely to be struck.
- A one-way clause (vendor recovers, consumer does not) is the kind of term a Michigan court could call substantively unconscionable in an adhesion context and is arguably a (1)(t) waiver of the consumer's statutory fee entitlement under MCL 445.911; avoid it.

### Gaps
- No Michigan reciprocity statute (comparable to Cal. Civ. Code § 1717) was identified in the sources found; whether Michigan courts will read a one-way consumer clause as mutual is not established by any decision located.
- No Michigan decision striking a fee clause in a consumer form contract as unconscionable was found.

## Q7. No-reliance, integration and "no oral modification" clauses as defenses to fraud/misrepresentation under Michigan law

### Takeaway
Michigan's Court of Appeals treats an integration clause as conclusive against claims based on pre-contract oral promises: parol evidence is admissible only where the fraud "would invalidate the merger clause itself" or the whole contract (*UAW-GM v. KSL*, 1998), and the clause renders reliance on contrary oral statements unreasonable (*Hamade v. Sunoco*, 2006). But fraud in the inducement that voids the contract cannot be contracted away (*Custom Data Solutions v. Preferred Capital*, 2006; the older Supreme Court line from *Plate v. Detroit Fidelity*, 1924), and MCPA misrepresentation/omission counts are statutory claims outside the parol evidence rule.

### Cited Findings
- *UAW-GM Human Resource Center v. KSL Recreation Corp.*, 228 Mich App 486 (1998): when the parties include an integration clause, "it is conclusive and parol evidence is not admissible to show that the agreement is not integrated except in cases of fraud that invalidate the integration clause or where an agreement is obviously incomplete 'on its face'"; parol evidence is admissible to prove fraud in the inducement "only where the alleged fraud would invalidate the merger clause itself or where the fraud would invalidate the entire contract, including the merger clause"; "Fraud that relates solely to an oral agreement that was nullified by a valid merger clause has no effect on the validity of the contract" — [FindLaw](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/mi-court-of-appeals/1178264.html)
- Same case as characterized in the Michigan Bar Journal: the fraud claims "require[d] reliance on a misrepresentation .... Here, the merger clause made it unreasonable for plaintiff's agent to rely on any representations not included in the letter of agreement"; the author notes the Court of Appeals "has applied UAW-GM in the only known MCPA appellate case involving a parol evidence issue" — [Lederman, "Freedom to Defraud," Mich. B.J. (Mar. 2008) (PDF)](https://www.michbar.org/file/barjournal/article/documents/pdf4article1340.pdf)
- *Hamade v. Sunoco, Inc. (R&M)*, 271 Mich App 145; 721 NW2d 233 (2006): fraud claim based solely on an oral representation that Sunoco would not authorize a competing station nearby; reliance held unreasonable as a matter of law in light of the written integrated agreement; parol evidence barred — [FindLaw](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/mi-court-of-appeals/1238080.html); [CourtListener](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1651823/hamade-v-sunoco-incr-m/)
- *Custom Data Solutions, Inc. v. Preferred Capital, Inc.* (Mich. App. Dec. 19, 2006) (published): "parol evidence is admissible for purposes of demonstrating that the agreement is void or voidable despite the existence of a merger clause"; summary disposition for the customer affirmed where the lessor's assignor fraudulently induced the agreement — [Justia](https://law.justia.com/cases/michigan/court-of-appeals-published/2006/20061219-c270752-43-28o-270752-opn-coa.html)
- *Titan Insurance Co. v. Hyten*, 491 Mich 547; 817 NW2d 562 (2012): fraud claims require reasonable reliance; reliance "cannot qualify as reasonable" where contrary to a statutory duty/public policy — [Michigan Supreme Court opinion (PDF)](https://www.courts.michigan.gov/siteassets/case-documents/uploads/OPINIONS/FINAL/SCT/20120615_S142774_49_titan-op.pdf)
- Michigan Supreme Court line: *Plate v. Detroit Fidelity & Surety Co.*, 229 Mich 482 (1924): "the 'seal of silence as to actual fraud is not imposed by ... [merger clause] provisions'"; reaffirmed in *Hanson v. Fletcher Auto Sales* (1927), *Gloeser v. Moore* (1938), *Bendford v. National Life & Accident* (1959); *Ditzik v. Schaffer Lumber* (Mich App 1984) and *UAW-GM* are the Court of Appeals cases used to dismiss fraud claims — [Altior Law](https://altiorlaw.com/can-fraud-claims-survive-in-the-face-of-merger-and-integration-clauses/)
- MCPA statutory counts that do not depend on the parol evidence rule: (1)(bb) "Making a representation of fact or statement of fact material to the transaction such that a person reasonably believes the represented or suggested state of affairs to be other than it actually is"; (1)(cc) "Failing to reveal facts that are material to the transaction in light of representations of fact made in a positive manner"; (1)(s) omission of a material fact — [legislature.mi.gov](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-445-903)
- Chesterfield (E.D. Mich.) applied the parallel federal rule that severable dispute-resolution clauses survive a general fraud-in-the-inducement claim absent fraud in the inducement of "the clause itself" — [E.D. Mich. opinion](https://www.mied.uscourts.gov/PDFFIles/07-12254%20Chestefield%20Opinion%20Striking%20Jury%20Demand.pdf)

### Inferences
- What the clause must say to get *UAW-GM*/*Hamade* treatment: (1) an express integration statement ("This Agreement is the complete and exclusive statement of the agreement and supersedes all prior or contemporaneous proposals, marketing statements, README text, demonstrations, emails, forum posts and oral statements"); (2) an express non-reliance statement in the first person ("You acknowledge that you have not relied on any statement, promise or representation not set out in this Agreement"); (3) a no-oral-modification clause ("No amendment is effective unless in a writing that identifies this Agreement and is accepted through the in-product acceptance flow or signed by both parties"); (4) a specific list of things the vendor does *not* promise (model quality, uptime, university acceptance, grades or outcomes). Michigan cases treat an integration clause plus a specific disclaimer as making reliance on contrary pre-contract statements unreasonable.
- Limits to state plainly in the file: the clause cannot bar (a) fraud that goes to the making of the whole contract or the merger clause itself, (b) MCPA statutory misrepresentation/omission claims about the product itself (which look to what was represented in advertising and the product page, not to contract parol), or (c) claims that the vendor knew a stated fact in the EULA itself was false. Marketing copy on the Marketplace listing and the README should therefore be written to the same standard as the contract — that is where a (1)(bb)/(1)(cc) claim would come from.

### Gaps
- The MCPA appellate case that applied *UAW-GM* to a parol-evidence issue is referenced only by footnote in the Bar Journal article; the case name was not extracted and should be identified.
- No Michigan decision addressing a *consumer clickwrap* integration/non-reliance clause against an MCPA claim was found.

## Q8. Assent evidence: what plaintiffs' challenges look like, what records win, scrollwrap/typed-name value, modified-terms re-acceptance, Michigan UETA

### Takeaway
Courts apply a two-part test — reasonably conspicuous notice and an unambiguous act manifesting assent (*Berman*, *Oberstein*, *Domer*, *Kauders*) — and decide most cases on screenshots of the exact flow. Clickwrap with a checkbox or button whose label matches the "By clicking X you agree" sentence is "routinely enforced"; sign-in-wraps fail on small gray text, off-flow placement, and mismatched button labels (*Sellers*, *Berman*, *Chabolla*). For modified terms, the business bears the burden of proving notice and continued use is not assent without it (*Jackson v. Amazon*), and "changes effective on posting, applied retroactively" language is procedurally unconscionable (*Heckman*). When the consumer swears they never clicked, business records must be specific enough to defeat a fact issue (*Boykin*; contrast StockX's affidavit and account records). Michigan UETA gives electronic records and signatures full effect and lets attribution be shown "in any manner, including a showing of the efficacy of any security procedure."

### Cited Findings
- *Berman v. Freedom Financial Network, LLC*, 30 F.4th 849 (9th Cir. Apr. 5, 2022): an enforceable online agreement requires "(1) the website provides reasonably conspicuous notice of the terms to which the consumer will be bound; and (2) the consumer takes some action, such as clicking a button or checking a box, that unambiguously manifests his or her assent to those terms"; the pages "did not conspicuously indicate" that clicking "continue" accepted terms; commentary: consumers should "(1) check the box and (2) be advised that checking the box will indicate assent" — [9th Cir. opinion (PDF)](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2022/04/05/20-16900.pdf); [Eric Goldman](https://blog.ericgoldman.org/archives/2022/04/poorly-executed-sign-in-wrap-contract-formation-process-fails-berman-v-freedom-financial.htm)
- *Oberstein v. Live Nation Entertainment, Inc.*, 60 F.4th 505 (9th Cir. Feb. 13, 2023): Ticketmaster's flow enforced under the same test; the court warned that hybrid agreements "invite second-guessing" and that clickwrap is "the safest choice" — [9th Cir. opinion (PDF)](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2023/02/13/21-56200.pdf); [Eric Goldman on Chabolla](https://blog.ericgoldman.org/archives/2025/03/the-ninth-circuit-has-a-lot-to-say-about-online-contract-formation-much-of-it-confusing-chabolla-v-classpass.htm)
- *Sellers v. JustAnswer LLC*, 73 Cal.App.5th 444 (Cal. Ct. App. Dec. 30, 2021): $5 trial that converted to a monthly membership; notices on the "Start my trial" screens "were not sufficiently conspicuous to put a user on inquiry notice of an arbitration agreement" — [Justia](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/2021/d077868.html); [Eric Goldman guest post](https://blog.ericgoldman.org/archives/2022/02/california-appellate-court-rejects-poorly-executed-sign-in-wrap-sellers-v-justanswer-guest-blog-post.htm)
- *Kauders v. Uber Technologies, Inc.*, 486 Mass. 557; 159 N.E.3d 1033 (Mass. 2021): enforceability requires "reasonable notice of the terms and a reasonable manifestation of assent to those terms"; Uber failed to provide adequate notice of the existence of the terms and the hyperlink — [FindLaw](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/ma-supreme-judicial-court/2105839.html); [Boston Bar Journal](https://bostonbar.org/journal/enforceability-of-online-contracts-under-massachusetts-law-kauders-v-uber-technologies-inc/)
- *Chabolla v. ClassPass Inc.*, 2025 WL 630813 (9th Cir. Feb. 27, 2025) (2–1): no contract formed across three screens: (screen 1) hyperlink "in a small gray font against a white background," placed "below the 'Sign up with Facebook' option, on the periphery"; (screen 2) "By signing up you agree to our Terms of Use" but the button said "continue" — "too much ambiguity"; (screen 3) "I agree to the Terms of Use" next to a "Redeem now" button lacked an if/then structure; taxonomy: browsewrap (courts "consistently decline to enforce"), clickwrap ("routinely enforce"), scrollwrap ("usually enforce"), sign-in wrap ("often enforce depending on certain factors") — [Eric Goldman](https://blog.ericgoldman.org/archives/2025/03/the-ninth-circuit-has-a-lot-to-say-about-online-contract-formation-much-of-it-confusing-chabolla-v-classpass.htm); [Hunton](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/its-a-wrap-the-latest-from-the-ninth-circuit-on-sign-in-wrap-agreements); [Crowell (rehearing petition)](https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/classpass-petition-for-rehearing-will-tell-the-future-of-sign-in-wrap-agreements-on-the-internet)
- *Godun v. JustAnswer, LLC* (9th Cir. Apr. 15, 2025) and *Dhruva v. CuriosityStream, Inc.* (4th Cir. 2025) also addressed sign-in-wrap enforceability in 2025 — [Frankfurt Kurnit](https://ipandmedialaw.fkks.com/post/102katf/circuit-courts-provide-contrasting-perspectives-on-the-enforceability-of-sign-in); [Fenwick](https://www.fenwick.com/insights/publications/thats-a-wrap-ninth-circuit-signals-tougher-standards-for-enforceability-of-online-agreements)
- *Domer v. Menard, Inc.*, No. 23-2672 (7th Cir. Sept. 3, 2024): hybrid-wrap checkout (no checkbox, terms link near the purchase button) enforced; "There is nothing automatically offensive about such agreements, as long as the layout and language of the site give the user reasonable notice that a click will manifest assent"; consumer-protection and unjust-enrichment claims fell within the clause — [Justia](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca7/23-2672/23-2672-2024-09-03.html); [Troutman](https://www.consumerfinancialserviceslawmonitor.com/2024/09/seventh-circuit-provides-how-to-successfully-present-consumer-arbitration-agreements/)
- *Jackson v. Amazon.com, Inc.* (9th Cir. Apr. 19, 2023): "the burden is on Amazon, as the party seeking arbitration, to show that it provided notice of a new TOS and that there was mutual assent"; no evidence the email "adequately notified drivers of the update"; without notice, continued use "could not demonstrate assent"; "generalized notice that new terms might be coming are not enough" — [9th Cir. opinion (PDF)](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2023/04/19/21-56107.pdf); [Proskauer](https://www.proskauer.com/blog/amazon-drivers-avoid-arbitration-claiming-non-delivery-of-updated-tos)
- *Heckman*: terms changeable "without notice," applied "retroactively," effective on posting with continued use as acceptance, were procedurally unconscionable for lack of "prior notice or affirmative acceptance" — [Sidley](https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/newsupdates/2024/10/ninth-circuits-live-nation-decision-evaluates-enforceability-of-mass-arbitration-protocols); [MSK](https://www.msk.com/newsroom-alerts-heckman-v-live-nation-ninth-circuit-hold-mass-arbitration-clause-to-be-unconscionable)
- *In re StockX* (6th Cir. 2021): checkbox/notice "By signing up, you agree to the Terms of Service"; update clause requiring cancellation if the user disagreed; VP affidavit that update emails were sent and that plaintiffs continued to log in; arbitration compelled — [FindLaw](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/us-6th-circuit/2153818.html)
- *Boykin v. Family Dollar Stores of Michigan, LLC*, 3 F.4th 832 (6th Cir. July 1, 2021): employer's records showed the arbitration module completed "at 10:00:58 a.m. on July 15, 2013" by clicking "I ACCEPT," but the records "did not include any arbitration agreement"; the employee's sworn denial created "a genuine issue of fact over whether he electronically accepted the contract"; order compelling arbitration reversed for a trial on formation — [Justia](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca6/20-1153/20-1153-2021-07-01.html); [Stites & Harbison](https://www.stites.com/resources/client-alerts/ensuring-enforcement-of-electronically-executed-arbitration-agreements/)
- Michigan UETA, MCL 450.837: "(1) A record or signature shall not be denied legal effect or enforceability solely because it is in electronic form. (2) A contract shall not be denied legal effect or enforceability solely because an electronic record was used in its formation. (3) If a law requires a record to be in writing, an electronic record satisfies the law. (4) If a law requires a signature, an electronic signature satisfies the law." — [legislature.mi.gov (Act 305 of 2000, PDF)](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/documents/mcl/pdf/mcl-Act-305-of-2000.pdf)
- MCL 450.835(2): "This act applies only to transactions between parties each of which has agreed to conduct transactions by electronic means. Whether the parties agree ... is determined from the context and surrounding circumstances, including the parties' conduct." — [same](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/documents/mcl/pdf/mcl-Act-305-of-2000.pdf)
- MCL 450.839: "(1) An electronic record or electronic signature is attributable to a person if it is the act of the person. The act of the person may be shown in any manner, including a showing of the efficacy of any security procedure applied to determine the person to which the electronic record or electronic signature was attributable. (2) The effect ... is determined from the context and surrounding circumstances at the time of its creation, execution, or adoption, including any agreements of the parties" — [legislature.mi.gov](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-450-839)
- MCL 450.842(1): a retention requirement is satisfied by an electronic record that "(a) Accurately reflects the information set forth in the record after it was first generated in its final form ... (b) Remains accessible for later reference" — [legislature.mi.gov (PDF)](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/documents/mcl/pdf/mcl-Act-305-of-2000.pdf)
- Restatement of the Law, Consumer Contracts (ALI; approved at the 2022 Annual Meeting; published June 26, 2024): § 2 (adoption of standard terms) reflects the approach that businesses may set terms "as long as they provide reasonable notice and meaningful opportunity to review the terms and to avoid the transaction"; § 3: "A modification of a standard contract term in a consumer contract is enforceable only if it is proposed in good faith," with notice and an opportunity to reject or terminate — [ALI](https://www.ali.org/news/articles/restatement-law-consumer-contracts-available); [Consumer Finance Monitor (2022)](https://www.consumerfinancemonitor.com/2022/06/27/ali-circulates-revisions-to-tentative-draft-no-2-of-the-restatement-of-the-law-consumer-contracts/); [ALI Adviser](https://www.thealiadviser.org/consumer-contracts/the-draft-restatement-of-the-law-consumer-contracts-follows-the-law/)

### Inferences
- **The flow, for a VS Code extension.** The Marketplace listing and README are browsewrap and prove nothing. Assent must be captured either in a first-run webview inside VS Code or on the web checkout for the paid license, using a two-step clickwrap: (1) terms rendered in a scrollable pane (scrollwrap is "usually enforced" and removes the "I never saw them" argument), (2) an unchecked checkbox immediately above the button with the sentence "By checking this box and clicking **Accept and Continue**, I agree to the License Agreement (v[X], [date]), including the Dispute Resolution, Class Action Waiver and Jury Waiver in § [N]" — the button label must match the sentence exactly (*Chabolla*); the link must be in contrasting color and underlined (*Berman*); no marketing text on the same screen.
- **What record wins.** From *Boykin* (records without the agreement text lost) and StockX (affidavit + account records won): store for each acceptance event an immutable row containing the terms version id and SHA-256 hash of the exact HTML/Markdown shown, the rendered screen (or the template id and a stored snapshot of that template), UTC timestamp, the checkbox-checked event and button-click event, account email or Marketplace identity, install id and machine id, IP, extension and VS Code versions, and — if used — the typed name; keep the archived terms text for every version; keep a signed declaration template so a custodian can attest to the system (UETA 450.839 lets attribution be proved by "the efficacy of any security procedure"). Retain per 450.842 (accurate, accessible).
- **Typed name and scroll-to-end.** Neither is legally required (UETA treats a click as a signature if adopted with intent), but scroll-to-end converts sign-in-wrap risk into scrollwrap, and a typed name is direct evidence of identity and intent that defeats a *Boykin*-style denial more cleanly than a click log. Both are cheap in a webview. Inference: adopt scroll-to-end always; adopt typed name for the paid license purchase and for any re-acceptance of changed dispute terms.
- **Modifications.** Do not use "effective on posting / continued use = acceptance" for dispute-resolution changes (*Heckman*; *Jackson*). Instead: version terms; on material change, block use until the same two-step flow is completed; give a plain option to decline with a pro-rata refund of the term license; never apply changed dispute terms to disputes already noticed; and keep the prior version's acceptance record intact.
- **Age and identity.** Capture an age attestation as a separate checkbox (see Q9) in the same logged event.

### Gaps
- No Sixth Circuit or Michigan appellate decision from 2024–2026 on clickwrap/scrollwrap formation was found; Michigan courts have not been shown to depart from the two-part test.
- The Restatement's black-letter text of §§ 2–3 was not obtained (paywalled); the descriptions above are from ALI-affiliated summaries.
- No decision was found squarely holding that requiring a typed name adds enforceability; that point is an inference from the evidentiary cases.

## Q9. Minors: disaffirmance, age representations, parental consent, and StockX

### Takeaway
A Michigan minor's contract is voidable at the minor's election and a parent cannot bind the child (*Woodman*). MCL 600.1403 removes the nonage defense in actions to recover for goods/money/loans where the minor "wilfully represented his age to be over 18" and the representation was "in writing in a separate instrument containing only the statement of age, date of signing and the signature" (or admitted in open court) — a template for an age-attestation screen. Where a delegation clause exists, the Sixth Circuit sends the infancy defense to the arbitrator because a minor's contract is voidable, not void (StockX, over a dissent).

### Cited Findings
- *Woodman v. Kera LLC*, 486 Mich 228 (2010): a minor lacks capacity to enter a pre-injury waiver; "a guardian, including a parent, cannot contractually bind his minor ward"; "Any contract entered into by a minor is voidable at the minor's discretion" — [FindLaw](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/mi-supreme-court/1527931.html); [Studicata summary](https://www.studicata.com/case-briefs/case/woodman-v-kera-llc)
- MCL 600.1403 ("Nonage as defense"): in actions to recover for goods, money or loans, the defense of nonage is unavailable where "the person against whom the action is brought wilfully represented his age to be over 18 years to the seller" with intent to obtain the goods or loan, knowing the representation false, the seller lacking actual knowledge of the true age, and "The representation was made in writing in a separate instrument containing only the statement of age, date of signing and the signature, or the representation is admitted in open court" (1961 PA 236, eff. Jan. 1, 1963, amended 1972) — [legislature.mi.gov](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-600-1403)
- General law: estoppel ordinarily does not apply to infants, but "many cases recognize an exception where the infant has reached the age of discretion and has intentionally and fraudulently made misrepresentations," with "two diametrically opposite views" among states — [Michigan Law Review note](https://repository.law.umich.edu/mlr/vol24/iss4/5); [U. Chicago L. Rev.](https://chicagounbound.uchicago.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=2524&context=uclrev)
- *In re StockX* (6th Cir. 2021): "under Michigan law ... a minor's contract is not synonymous with a nonexistent contract"; because an infant's contract "is voidable," the infancy challenge concerns "enforceability," which the delegation provision assigns to the arbitrator; Judge Moore dissented that capacity defenses go to the "making" of the contract and are for the court (citing *Spahr v. Secco*, 330 F.3d 1266, 1273 (10th Cir. 2003)) — [FindLaw](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/us-6th-circuit/2153818.html)

### Inferences
- An age representation clause buried in the EULA does little; a separate attestation screen containing only "I am 18 years of age or older," the date, and a typed name tracks MCL 600.1403(b)'s "separate instrument" language and is the strongest cheap record available. Its statutory effect is limited to actions by the seller to recover for the goods/loan (i.e., it protects the vendor's right to keep the fee), but it is also the evidence a court or arbitrator would weigh on any estoppel argument.
- Parental consent screens add little under *Woodman* for waivers; they may still matter for other purposes (payment authorization; the vendor's own comfort about users under 18) — counsel to decide whether to (i) bar users under 18, (ii) allow under-18 use only in the free tier with no waiver relied on, or (iii) accept the disaffirmance risk (refund exposure equals tens of dollars).
- With a delegation clause, StockX means a minor's disaffirmance argument will be heard by the arbitrator in the Sixth Circuit — but that also means the vendor pays the arbitration fees to get that ruling (Q1).

### Gaps
- Michigan's age of majority statute (MCL 722.52) and any Michigan case applying MCL 600.1403 to online age attestations were not fetched or found.
- No decision applying MCL 600.1403 to a software license or subscription was found.

## Q10. Liability caps with a small fixed floor, exclusion of consequential damages, and the "failure of essential purpose" trap (MCL 440.2719)

### Takeaway
MCL 440.2719 permits an exclusive limited remedy (e.g., refund or repair/replace) and permits exclusion of consequential damages "unless the limitation or exclusion is unconscionable," but "[w]here circumstances cause an exclusive or limited remedy to fail of its essential purpose, remedy may be had as provided in this act." Whether Article 2 applies to a downloaded extension is decided by Michigan's predominant-purpose test and is uncertain; drafting should work under both Article 2 and common law: an exclusive remedy the vendor can always actually deliver (refund), an independent consequential-damages exclusion, and a cap at the greater of fees paid or a small fixed amount.

### Cited Findings
- MCL 440.2719 (full text): "(1) ... (a) the agreement may provide for remedies in addition to or in substitution for those provided in this article and may limit or alter the measure of damages recoverable under this article, as by limiting the buyer's remedies to return of the goods and repayment of the price or to repair and replacement of nonconforming goods or parts; and (b) resort to a remedy as provided is optional unless the remedy is expressly agreed to be exclusive, in which case it is the sole remedy. (2) Where circumstances cause an exclusive or limited remedy to fail of its essential purpose, remedy may be had as provided in this act. (3) Consequential damages may be limited or excluded unless the limitation or exclusion is unconscionable. Limitation of consequential damages for injury to the person in the case of consumer goods is prima facie unconscionable but limitation of damages where the loss is commercial is not." — [legislature.mi.gov](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-440-2719)
- Commentary on Michigan law: § 2719(2) "is only triggered when the remedy the parties agreed to fails in its essential purpose—when unanticipated circumstances make the seller unable to provide the buyer with the agreed remedy"; "Where a limitation of liability provision fails, an exclusion on consequential damages may fail along with it" — [USLegal (Michigan warranty clauses)](https://civilprocedure.uslegal.com/articles/validity-of-warranty-clauses-limiting-damages-in-michigan/); [Clark Hill drafting note (PDF)](https://media.clarkhill.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/10100926/drafting_liability.pdf)
- Michigan applies the "predominant factor" test to mixed goods/services transactions — "whether the acquisition of goods is the buyer's primary purpose or 'ultimate goal'" — and courts nationally remain split on whether software is a good or a service, with a "growing shift toward finding that specially produced software is service rather than a good" — [Butzel Long](https://www.butzel.com/alert-are-you-supplying-goods-or-services-the-latest-from-the-michigan-court-of-appeals); [Michigan Law Review](https://michiganlawreview.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/03/113MichLRev739_Gottlieb.pdf); [Eckland & Blando](https://www.ecklandblando.com/blog/2021/11/so-good-its-a-service-the-changing-legal-perspective-on-computer-software/)
- MCL 440.2725: four-year period for UCC warranty claims, which the parties may shorten by agreement (see *Liparoto*, Q3) — [FindLaw (Liparoto)](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/mi-court-of-appeals/1207327.html)
- Magnuson-Moss: "consumer product" means "any tangible personal property which is distributed in commerce and which is normally used for personal, family, or household purposes"; FTC guidance treats software sold at retail or "distributed over the Internet to individual users" as within the Act's disclosure rules; the Act "prohibits implied warranty disclaimers, at least during the term of any written warranty" — [uscode.house.gov (15 U.S.C. ch. 50)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?path=%2Fprelim%40title15%2Fchapter50&edition=prelim); [FindLaw (performance warranties)](https://corporate.findlaw.com/litigation-disputes/performance-warranties-in-computer-contracts.html); [FTC Businessperson's Guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/businesspersons-guide-federal-warranty-law)
- Magnuson-Moss and arbitration: the FTC's 2015 review retained its position that binding pre-dispute arbitration in a written warranty is prohibited under Rule 703, while the Fifth and Eleventh Circuits (e.g., *Davis v. Southern Energy Homes*) hold the Act does not bar binding arbitration and decline to defer ("the FTC's interpretation of the MMWA is unreasonable"); Commissioner Ohlhausen dissented from retaining the position — [Federal Register (July 20, 2015)](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2015/07/20/2015-14065/final-action-concerning-review-of-interpretations-of-magnuson-moss-warranty-act-rule-governing); [NCLC](https://library.nclc.org/article/new-magnuson-moss-rules-aid-consumer-warranty-litigation); [Ohlhausen dissent (PDF)](https://search.ftc.gov/system/files/documents/public_statements/644521/150522mag-mosswarrantystatement.pdf)
- MCPA (1)(p) concerns disclaiming implied warranties of merchantability and fitness without clear disclosure (summary from fetch; quote the text before drafting) — [legislature.mi.gov](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-445-903)

### Inferences
- **Avoiding the 2719(2) trap.** The exclusive remedy fails of its essential purpose when the vendor cannot deliver it (e.g., "repair or replace" for a product the vendor can no longer ship because Microsoft de-listed it or Anthropic cut the API). Make the exclusive remedy one the vendor can always perform: "refund of the license fee paid for the current term, pro-rated for the unused portion," with repair/replacement as an option at the vendor's election, not the sole path. State that the consequential-damages exclusion and the cap are "independent of, and shall survive the failure of, any exclusive remedy" (the drafting response to the "fails along with it" line of cases; Michigan commentary treats the clauses as separable when so drafted).
- **Cap.** "Aggregate liability shall not exceed the greater of (a) the amounts you paid for the license in the 12 months before the claim or (b) $100" is consistent with 2719(1)(a) ("repayment of the price") and gives a court a non-illusory floor for the free tier; 2719(3)'s prima facie unconscionability applies only to personal-injury consequential damages in consumer goods, which is not the loss profile for a coding assistant, but the exclusion should still carve out "liability that cannot be limited by law, including for death or personal injury caused by negligence, fraud, or gross negligence" so that it is not facially overbroad.
- **Magnuson-Moss decision point for counsel.** If the EULA gives *no* written warranty ("AS IS"), the Act's disclaimer prohibition and its arbitration controversy do not arise; if the vendor gives any written promise about performance over time (a "30-day money-back guarantee" phrased as a warranty may count), the Act's rules on implied-warranty disclaimers and the FTC/Circuit split on binding arbitration attach — and whether a downloaded extension is "tangible personal property" is itself unsettled. Safest: no written warranty; a refund *policy* stated as a remedy, not a warranty; implied warranties disclaimed conspicuously (capitals; separate heading) with the MCPA (1)(p) disclosure and a "some states do not allow ..." savings clause.
- Because Article 2's application is uncertain, draft warranty disclaimers to satisfy both MCL 440.2316's conspicuousness rules (not fetched; standard) and common-law standards.

### Gaps
- No Michigan appellate decision applying MCL 440.2719(2) to a software license was found; the failure-of-essential-purpose discussion above is from secondary Michigan commentary.
- No Michigan decision resolving whether a downloaded software license is a "good" under Article 2 was found.
- No authority was found on whether downloadable software is "tangible personal property" under Magnuson-Moss.

## Q11. Michigan and federal rules that void or penalize specific clauses in consumer form contracts (MCPA 445.903(1)(t); Consumer Review Fairness Act; Magnuson-Moss; FTC 2025–2026; California 1670.8; New Jersey TCCWNA; New York GBL 399-c)

### Takeaway
Four rules directly police clause text for a vendor selling to students nationwide: MCL 445.903(1)(t) (waivers must be "clearly stated" and "specifically consented to"), the federal Consumer Review Fairness Act (non-disparagement and review-IP clauses in form contracts are void and merely offering them is unlawful), California Civil Code § 1670.8 (anti-review clauses; penalties up to $2,500/$5,000/$10,000, applies to any business with California consumers), and New Jersey's TCCWNA (statutory $100 penalty plus fees for terms that violate clearly established rights — narrowed by *Spade* to consumers who suffered an adverse consequence). New York GBL 399-c's ban on mandatory consumer arbitration is preempted by the FAA where interstate commerce is involved. The FTC's click-to-cancel rule was vacated by the Eighth Circuit in 2025 and a new rulemaking began in March 2026; Section 5 enforcement continues.

### Cited Findings
- MCL 445.903(1)(t) (quoted in Q1/Q5) and (1)(n) ("Causing a probability of confusion or of misunderstanding as to the legal rights, obligations, or remedies of a party to a transaction") — [legislature.mi.gov](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-445-903)
- Consumer Review Fairness Act, 15 U.S.C. § 45b: "form contract" is "a contract with standardized terms—(i) used by a person in the course of selling or leasing the person's goods or services; and (ii) imposed on an individual without a meaningful opportunity ... to negotiate," excluding employer-employee/independent-contractor contracts; a provision is void if it "prohibits or restricts the ability of an individual ... to engage in a covered communication," "imposes a penalty or fee ... for engaging in a covered communication," or transfers IP in reviews "with the exception of a non-exclusive license"; "It shall be unlawful for a person to offer a form contract containing a provision described as void in subsection (b)"; exceptions preserve confidentiality duties, defamation claims, and removal of unlawful/false content; FTC and state AG enforcement — [LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/45b); [FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/statutes/consumer-review-fairness-act)
- California Civil Code § 1670.8: a consumer contract "may not include a provision waiving the consumer's right to make any statement regarding the seller or lessor or its employees or agents, or concerning the goods or services"; unlawful "to threaten or to seek to enforce" such a provision; civil penalty "not to exceed two thousand five hundred dollars ($2,500) for the first violation, and five thousand dollars ($5,000) for the second and for each subsequent violation," and "not to exceed ten thousand dollars ($10,000)" for a "willful, intentional, or reckless violation," recoverable by the consumer or public prosecutors; commentary: "no geographic limitations, so the law applies to any companies or persons doing business in California" — [california.public.law](https://california.public.law/codes/civil_code_section_1670.8); [Fenwick](https://www.fenwick.com/insights/publications/litigation-alert-california-bans-non-disparagement-clauses-in-consumer-contracts); [Latham (website terms risk)](https://www.lw.com/admin/upload/SiteAttachments/Website-Terms-of-Use-May-Risk-Liability-Under-California-Law.pdf)
- New Jersey TCCWNA, N.J.S.A. 56:12-14 to -18: no seller shall "offer to any consumer ... or enter into any written consumer contract ... which includes any provision that violates any clearly established legal right of a consumer or responsibility of a seller ... as established by State or Federal law"; remedy "a civil penalty of not less than $100.00 or for actual damages, or both at the election of the consumer, together with reasonable attorney's fees and court costs"; applies to businesses in all states whose terms reach New Jersey consumers — [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/courts-continue-crackdown-new-jersey-s-truth-consumer-contract-warranty-and-notice); [Hunton](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/the-new-wave-of-consumer-class-actions-targeting-retailers-what-is-the-tccwna)
- *Spade v. Select Comfort Corp.* (N.J. 2018) (7–0): "an 'aggrieved consumer' is a consumer who has been harmed by a violation of N.J.S.A. 56:12-15"; a consumer who received a non-compliant contract but "has not suffered any adverse consequences" is not aggrieved — [Justia](https://law.justia.com/cases/new-jersey/supreme-court/2018/a-57-16.html); [McCarter & English](https://www.mccarter.com/insights/spade-wegner-supreme-court-delivers-crushing-blow-to-tccwna-plaintiffs-by-enforcing-aggrieved-consumer-requirement-as-written/)
- New York GBL § 399-c prohibits mandatory arbitration clauses in consumer-goods contracts and declares them "null and void," but *Schiffer v. Slomin's, Inc.*, 48 Misc.3d 15 (App. Term 2015) held it "is a categorical rule prohibiting mandatory arbitration clauses in consumer contracts, and thus, at least where there exists a nexus with interstate commerce, is displaced by the FAA"; *Marino v. Salzman*, 51 Misc.3d 131(A) (App. Term 2016) same — [Justia (statute)](https://law.justia.com/codes/new-york/gbs/article-26/399-c/); [CourtListener (Schiffer)](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/6315750/schiffer-v-slomins-inc/); [Farrell Fritz/fhnylaw](https://www.fhnylaw.com/federal-preemption-the-faa-trumps-gbl-399-c-s-prohibition-of-mandatory-arbitration-agreements)
- FTC: the 2024 Negative Option ("Click-to-Cancel") Rule required clear disclosure of material terms, express informed consent and simple cancellation; it "was vacated in 2025 by the Eighth Circuit on procedural grounds"; the FTC continues Section 5 enforcement against subscription practices and "In March 2026 the FTC launched an Advance Notice of Proposed Rulemaking ('ANPRM') to revive the Rule"; roughly 30 states have their own automatic-renewal laws — [Jones Day (May 2026)](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2026/05/ftc-revives-clicktocancel-rule-new-risks-for-subscription-businesses); [WilmerHale (Aug. 2025)](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/client-alerts/20250801-eighth-circuit-vacates-the-ftcs-click-to-cancel-rule-but-federal-and-state-regulators-likely-to-remain-active); [Goodwin (Feb. 2026)](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/02/alerts-practices-ba-ftcs-click-to-cancel-rule-gets-new-life)
- Magnuson-Moss (see Q10) — [Federal Register 2015](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2015/07/20/2015-14065/final-action-concerning-review-of-interpretations-of-magnuson-moss-warranty-act-rule-governing)
- EFAA (see Q1) — [Congress.gov](https://www.congress.gov/117/plaws/publ90/PLAW-117publ90.htm)
- Michigan UAA nonwaivable provisions (see Q1) — [legislature.mi.gov](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-691-1684)
- Choice of law: Michigan adopted Restatement (Second) of Conflict of Laws §§ 187–188 (*Chrysler Corp. v. ### Inferences
- Clauses to omit entirely: any non-disparagement or review restriction, any assignment of IP in reviews beyond a non-exclusive license, any fee or penalty tied to reviews (CRFA void + unlawful to offer; California penalties; TCCWNA hook). Also omit "we may change these terms at any time without notice, effective immediately and retroactively" (*Heckman*) and any term that could be read as an absolute bar on statutory claims (TCCWNA "clearly established legal right"; MCPA (1)(n)).
- Michigan-law drafting discipline: put every waiver (jury, class, forum, limitations, release, warranty) under its own heading, in plain words, each stating what right is being given up and that the consumer's statutory remedies otherwise remain — this is what "clearly stated" and "specifically consented" in MCL 445.903(1)(t) demand, and what *Lozada* penalized the absence of.
- Free trial mechanics: if the trial converts automatically to a paid term, state auto-renewal laws and the FTC's Section 5 position apply (disclosure before consent; cancellation as easy as sign-up); if the trial simply expires and a purchase is a separate affirmative act, most of that regime is avoided. Counsel to confirm the intended mechanics.
- Out-of-state students: a Michigan choice-of-law clause will generally be honored, but a California or New Jersey student can invoke § 187(2) and their state's statutes (1670.8; TCCWNA) regardless of the clause, so the terms should comply with those statutes on their face rather than rely on the Michigan choice of law.

### Gaps
- No FTC policy statement specifically on "unfair contract terms" in software terms of service for 2025–2026 was found beyond the negative-option rulemaking and Section 5 enforcement described in law-firm alerts; the FTC policy-statement index was not read in full.
- No Michigan decision applying MCL 445.903(1)(t) to a jury waiver, class waiver or arbitration clause was found.
- The TCCWNA's application to a software license (as opposed to goods) and post-*Spade* case law from 2019–2026 were not researched.
