## Key question 1: What the Grid's own policies say (workloads, services/daemons, login node, network, credentials, sharing, software, commercial use, external collaborators)

### Takeaway
The Grid's written rules are nine short numbered items (KB 20254) plus a FAQ (KB 20229) and a VS Code article (KB 20349). They are explicit and strict on one thing that Ashley touches directly — nothing may run on the login node Warrior beyond 20 processes, VS Code Remote-SSH included — and they are silent on outbound network access, daemons inside allocated jobs, credential files, commercial use and external collaborators. Eligibility is stated once: a WSU AccessID and "a valid research-related computational task".

### Cited Findings
- Rule 1 (login node): "The WSU Grid uses Slurm to schedule jobs. You must run all processes on compute nodes by requesting a node through Slurm. This may be done with either an interactive job or a job script. If you are caught running processes on Warrior, the Grid's head node, they will be deleted immediately and your account will be temporarily suspended. If you continuously run processes on Warrior then you will be banned from using the Grid." — [HPC Grid Policies, KB 20254](https://services.wayne.edu/TDClient/277/Portal/KB/ArticleDet?ID=20254)
- Rule 2 (process cap): "You may run no more than 20 processes on Warrior. Some things like interactive jobs and screen sessions may use more than a single process, so please pay attention to what you leave running on the Grid." — [KB 20254](https://services.wayne.edu/TDClient/277/Portal/KB/ArticleDet?ID=20254)
- Rule 3 (home directories and sharing): "All home directories are shared across all clusters by a global file system that spans all Grid nodes and the master node. Your home directory is where you should place your files and work. Your home directory is protected from other users being able to see your files. If you need to share files then request a group directory by sending an email to the High Performance Computing team including the group name, the AccessIDs of the members, and the amount of space needed." — [KB 20254](https://services.wayne.edu/TDClient/277/Portal/KB/ArticleDet?ID=20254)
- Rule 4 (group membership): "If you are a member of an existing group then the head of the group must send a request to the High Performance Computing team to have you added." — [KB 20254](https://services.wayne.edu/TDClient/277/Portal/KB/ArticleDet?ID=20254)
- Rule 5 (temporary files): "When running jobs you should keep all temporary files in $TMPDIR. $TMPDIR is erased once your job is finished. Both /tmp and /wsu/tmp are cleaned out on a rotating monthly basis, so if your files are not stored in $TMPDIR then they are in danger of being erased. We do not backup /tmp or /wsu/tmp, so be warned not to store your files there." — [KB 20254](https://services.wayne.edu/TDClient/277/Portal/KB/ArticleDet?ID=20254)
- Rule 6 (SSH keys — the only credential rule): "SSH-2 has been enabled on all nodes and SSH keys are used for you to be able to move between nodes without re-authenticating each time. The first time you log in, keys are automatically generated. We suggest you let the system auto-generate your keys and that you use a blank password for the SSH-key pair. This will allow you to move around the Grid easily." — [KB 20254](https://services.wayne.edu/TDClient/277/Portal/KB/ArticleDet?ID=20254)
- Rules 7–8 (maintenance): "The Grid may be brought down for maintenance at the following times: Third week of May; First week of December; First week of March" and "The Grid may be brought down at other times for major hardware upgrades as well. The Grid Computing Team will always provide as much notification as possible so that you may prepare your workload to account for the downtime." — [KB 20254](https://services.wayne.edu/TDClient/277/Portal/KB/ArticleDet?ID=20254)

### Inferences
- The only Grid rule Ashley could break by design is Rule 1/2: the VS Code Remote-SSH server, any helper daemon, and the Claude Code process must all live inside the Slurm allocation, not on Warrior. KB 20349 shows the HPC team already polices this for VS Code specifically and warns then kills.
- A long-lived Slurm job hosting an interactive assistant is not addressed one way or the other. Nothing in KB 20254/20229/20353 forbids a service-like process inside an allocation, and the "requeue" QoS is described as "Unlimited use" (see Key question 8). The "valid research-related computational task" eligibility language in the FAQ is the nearest thing to a workload restriction.
- Rule 3's promise that "Your home directory is protected from other users" is the written basis for keeping token files (mode 600) there; no rule speaks to credentials in files at all (see Key question 4 for the campus-wide standards that do).
- Sharing read-only data via POSIX ACLs on a lab volume is outside the written rules, which only describe requested group directories; it is not prohibited, but the documented route is a "Grid group request".
- Because the Merit AUP makes WSU responsible for its "Sub-Users", C&IT has a documented interest in what leaves the Grid over the network; a license could sensibly require users to keep outbound traffic to the named endpoints.

### Gaps
- No Grid document addresses: outbound network access from compute nodes, running daemons/services inside a job, storing credentials, account sharing (covered instead by UP 00-1, Key question 2), commercial/for-profit use (covered by UP 00-1 and the software page), or external collaborators (covered by Guest IDs, Key question 7). Absent means absent, not permitted.
- The Grid account request form's own terms/acknowledgement text could not be read (Shibboleth sign-in). KB 20199 "How to apply for a Grid account" contains only three procedural steps and no terms. — [KB 20199](https://services.wayne.edu/TDClient/277/Portal/KB/ArticleDet?ID=20199)
- None of the TeamDynamix articles display a last-modified date.
- Whether compute nodes have unrestricted internet egress is not written down anywhere found; the conda tutorial (KB 20351) implies package downloads from interactive jobs, which is an inference from a how-to, not a rule.

## Key question 2: University Policy 00-1 (Acceptable Use of Information Technology Resources) — exact text on for-profit use, incidental personal use, sharing AccessIDs/passwords, unauthorized services

### Takeaway
UP 00-1 (signed May 12, 2000; no revision shown) flatly prohibits "Using University information technology resources for personal for-profit purposes" (§3.3) and says account sharing for convenience is not acceptable (§3.1). It has no "incidental personal use" clause and no clause about running servers or services; the closest items are "Modifying system or network facilities" and "Subverting restrictions associated with computer accounts".

### Cited Findings
- Identification: "Policy Number: University Policy 00-1", "Effective Date: Upon issuance", signed "May 12, 2000" by President Irvin D. Reid; "No revision date shown after May 12, 2000". Published at two URLs with identical text. — [wayne.edu/policies/acceptable-use](https://wayne.edu/policies/acceptable-use); [policies.wayne.edu/it/00-1-acceptable-use-of-technology](https://policies.wayne.edu/it/00-1-acceptable-use-of-technology). (The URL `policies.wayne.edu/administrative/00-1-acceptable-use-of-information-technology-resources` returns 404.)
- §1.0: "This policy is designed to guide students, faculty and staff in the acceptable use of computer systems, networks, and other information technology resources at Wayne State University." — [UP 00-1](https://wayne.edu/policies/acceptable-use)
- §2.1: "The University community is encouraged to make innovative and creative use of information technologies in support of educational, scholarly, and administrative purposes." §2.4: "Information technology resources are provided to support the University's scholarly, educational, and administrative activities." — [UP 00-1](https://wayne.edu/policies/acceptable-use)
- §3.1 (full): "Users are expected to use computer and network resources in a responsible manner. Users should take appropriate precautions to ensure the security of their passwords and prevent others from obtaining access to their computer resources. Convenience of file or printer sharing is not a sufficient reason for sharing computer accounts." — [UP 00-1](https://policies.wayne.edu/it/00-1-acceptable-use-of-technology)
- §3.2: "Users may not encroach on others' use of computer resources." — [UP 00-1](https://wayne.edu/policies/acceptable-use)
- §3.3 prohibited behaviors, complete list: "Modifying system or network facilities, or attempting to crash systems or networks"; "Using, duplicating or transmitting copyrighted material without first obtaining the owner's permission, in any way that may reasonably be expected to constitute an infringement"; "Tampering with software protections or restrictions placed on computer applications or files"; "Using University information technology resources for personal for-profit purposes"; "Sending messages that are malicious or that a reasonable person would find to be harassing"; "Subverting restrictions associated with computer accounts"; "Using information technology resources to obtain unauthorized access to records, data"; "Accessing another person's computer account without permission"; "Intentionally introducing computer viruses, worms, Trojan Horses, or other rogue programs"; "Physically damaging information technology resources"; "Using information technology resources in any manner that would violate this or other University policies or applicable state or federal law"; "Falsely reporting or accusing another of conduct that violates this policy, without good faith basis". — [UP 00-1](https://policies.wayne.edu/it/00-1-acceptable-use-of-technology)
- §4.1: "While respecting users' privacy to the fullest extent possible, the University reserves the right to examine any computer files" (purposes listed include enforcing policies, protecting resources, legal requests, copyright investigations, integrity, preserving data, upgrades, liability). §4.2: "The University may restrict the use of its computers and network systems when presented with evidence of violation." §4.3: "Abuse of computing privileges may subject the user to disciplinary action as established by applicable University policies." — [UP 00-1](https://wayne.edu/policies/acceptable-use)
- Student-side enforcement instrument: Student Code of Conduct (amended June 24, 2022) §2.15 "Technology resources means any and all technologies that produce, manipulate, store, communicate, or disseminate information."; §4.12 "Unauthorized entrance into, or use of, institutional facilities, including computing and telecommunication facilities and systems."; §4.17 "Misuse or intentional disruption of the University's technological resources." — [Student Code of Conduct](https://doso.wayne.edu/care/student-conduct/code) (also codified at [BOG 2.31.02](https://bog.wayne.edu/code/2-31-02))
- Related C&IT statement on software (Software Webstore scope, not UP 00-1): "Access to the site and available software is limited to students, faculty, staff and select university affiliates for administrative, academic and non-commercial research use." and "Non-commercial research refers to research conducted by faculty, staff and/or degree-seeking students that 1) does not involve direct financial gain, monetary compensation or intention of commercial advantage, and/or 2) results made from the software restrict the academic study or research of the university community." Also: "All software must be purchased via the Software Webstore, WayneBuy, or other institutional purchasing processes." — [tech.wayne.edu/services/software](https://tech.wayne.edu/services/software)

### Inferences
- §3.3's "personal for-profit purposes" is the clause a paid Ashley license must be squared with. It bars a *user* from using WSU IT for their own profit; it does not on its face bar a user from paying a vendor for a tool they run on WSU IT for research. Whether a vendor who is also a WSU member (running the tool on the Grid) is himself "using University information technology resources for personal for-profit purposes" is the ambiguous case and is not resolved by the text.
- The "non-commercial research" definition on the software page is C&IT's working definition of the boundary; it applies to Webstore software, but a license could adopt it as the definition of permitted use.
- "Convenience … is not a sufficient reason for sharing computer accounts" plus §3.3 "Accessing another person's computer account without permission" together give the account-sharing rule; a per-person license key tied to one AccessID is consistent with it.

### Gaps
- UP 00-1 contains no "incidental personal use" allowance and no "unauthorized servers/services" clause. Both are absent, not implied.
- No revision of UP 00-1 since 2000 was found; the policy predates cloud services, SSO and API tokens entirely.

## Key question 3: May a third-party application use the WSU Microsoft Entra tenant sign-in? Is there a policy on third-party apps consuming WSU identity or on developers registering apps?

### Takeaway
No public WSU policy, standard or KB article on third-party applications consuming WSU identity, on Entra app registrations, or on user-consent settings was found. The relevant written material is the C&IT "Standards for access, authentication, and authorization" (which favors central AccessID/SSO and mandates MFA for cloud services holding WSU data) and UP 07-1's requirement that systems interacting with enterprise systems be developed "in collaboration with" C&IT.

### Cited Findings
- "Credentials should utilize central LDAP or Active Directory sources for authentication. Where possible, the central Single-Sign-On (SSO) system should be used." — [Standards for access, authentication, and authorization](https://tech.wayne.edu/policies/access-auth)
- "Two-factor or multi-factor authentication should be used whenever technically feasible." For cloud services without SSO, MFA is mandatory "where offered, for any cloud service that processes or stores WSU data." — [access-auth standard](https://tech.wayne.edu/policies/access-auth)
- University IT staff will "design and deploy information systems using centrally-managed AccessID". — [access-auth standard](https://tech.wayne.edu/policies/access-auth)
- "Using accounts that require a shared password are prohibited from use for normal user authentication and should only be used for authentication of an application or service." Shared accounts require an owner and "passwords must be stored in an approved Enterprise Password Manager." — [access-auth standard](https://tech.wayne.edu/policies/access-auth)
- Least privilege: users should "access and operate systems at the minimum security level needed in order to perform their work". — [access-auth standard](https://tech.wayne.edu/policies/access-auth)
- UP 07-1 (effective November 30, 2007): any system interacting with enterprise systems must be developed "in collaboration with Computing and Information Technology"; major acquisitions and enterprise-system changes require Information Technology Steering Committee approval; enterprise IT systems are those used university-wide, e.g. "Banner, Blackboard, Cognos, OneCard, and WSU email" (summary of definitions; the quoted fragment is verbatim). — [UP 07-1 Information Technology System Policy](https://policies.wayne.edu/it/07-01-information-technology-system)
- The only SSO KB article is end-user guidance: SSO "allows you to use your AccessID and password to sign in to most applications, such as Canvas, Zoom, and Microsoft 365." It says nothing about integration requests, SAML/OIDC, or Entra app registration. — [Single Sign On or SSO for Wayne State websites, KB 20270](https://services.wayne.edu/TDClient/277/Portal/KB/ArticleDet?ID=20270)
- UP 02-2 (signed April 30, 2002) assigns "responsibility for the campus network infrastructure" to C&IT, defines core network services to include "directory services, domain naming services, electronic messaging including e-mail and voicemail, institutional file and web services, and access to the Internet", and says units "must comply with all standards governing such attachments, and are prohibited from tampering with or removing any component of the infrastructure." — [UP 02-2](https://policies.wayne.edu/it/02-2-network-infrastructure-responsibility)
- C&IT's AI tools page (relevant because Ashley hosts Claude): "The following tools have been reviewed and approved for secure use at Wayne State." Table row: "Claude | AI assistant | Purchase approval granted by C&IT exception | Use your department's standard process for software purchase requests to initiate C&IT review". Same wording for ChatGPT. "Additional tools may be requested for a C&IT exception, with appropriate business-need justification, via your department's standard purchasing process." Listed approved tools: WarriorGPT, Cursor AI, Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform, Microsoft Copilot, Microsoft Copilot Chat, Microsoft Teams Premium, Zoom Smart AI Assistant, plus ChatGPT and Claude "(requires C&IT exception)". WarriorGPT is available to "Students, faculty"; other tools are limited to "Administrative staff", "Administrative staff, faculty", or "Researchers". — [AI at Wayne State](https://tech.wayne.edu/help/wsuai)

### Inferences
- VS Code's built-in Microsoft authentication provider signs in through Microsoft's own first-party "Visual Studio Code" application, not an app the developer registers; whether WSU's tenant allows user consent to it (or has already admin-consented) is a tenant setting that is not published. This is an inference from how VS Code works, not from any WSU document.
- The AI-tools page is the closest thing to a rule on Claude: C&IT treats Claude as requiring a "C&IT exception" obtained through a departmental software-purchase request. A tool that puts Claude Code in front of students would most naturally go through that route; the page gives no self-service path for students.
- UP 07-1's collaboration clause is written for enterprise systems (Banner, email); whether an authentication flow against the WSU Entra tenant "interacts with" an enterprise system is ambiguous.

### Gaps
- No WSU document on: Entra app registration by developers, user-consent policy for third-party apps, a vendor SSO-integration request process, HECVAT/security review requirements for software (a search for WSU-specific HECVAT procedure returned only other institutions), or data-handling rules for external AI tools (the AI page contains no sentence using "data", "sensitive", "confidential", "FERPA", "HIPAA" or "institutional").
- The AI page's audience column for the Claude row (which of "Researchers" / "Administrative staff, faculty" applies) was not captured verbatim.

## Key question 4: Data classification / security policies — storing API tokens and personal identifiers (AccessID, e-mail) in files and third-party tools

### Takeaway
WSU has no published tiered data-classification standard (no "UP 10-1" exists; the IT policy index lists UP 00-1, 02-2, 07-1, 07-2, 11-1, 18-1 and APPM 10.10). UP 07-2 defines "confidential information" and explicitly includes "Access ID/ password combinations"; the Strong Password Standard says never to save passwords "in web browsers or computer programs"; the personal-devices standard bars storing "non-public data" and "administrator or root type credentials" on personal devices. None of these mention API tokens.

### Cited Findings
- No policy numbered 10-1: "No policy numbered 10-1 appears on this page. The closest reference is APPM 10.10 listed above." IT policies listed: UP 00-1, UP 02-2, UP 07-1, UP 07-2, UP 11-1 (Video Surveillance), UP 18-1 (Web Accessibility), APPM 10.10. — [policies.wayne.edu/information-technology](https://policies.wayne.edu/information-technology)
- C&IT's own index of IT policies, standards and guidelines (Acceptable Use; Merit AUP; AccessIDs/Email/Directory; Administrative Rights Access; UP 07-1; UP 07-2; UP 02-2; Strong Password Standard; Audit and Accountability; Identification and Authentication; Incident Response; IT Investigations; Personal Devices; Physical and Environmental Protection; Security Awareness Training; Vulnerability Management; System Integrity; IT Access to Administrative Systems; OneDrive guidelines; Data and Log Retention Practices; GLBA program). No "data classification" standard is listed. — [tech.wayne.edu/policies](https://tech.wayne.edu/policies)
- KB "What are the security policies at Wayne State?" says only: "Find Wayne State IT security policies, guidelines and standards at tech.wayne.edu/about/policies." and names no classification tiers. — [KB 20195](https://services.wayne.edu/TDClient/277/Portal/KB/ArticleDet?ID=20195)
- UP 07-2 Confidential Information (signed November 30, 2007) lists as confidential: "social security numbers, credit card numbers, bank account information"; student records (excluding directory information); protected health information; "Access ID/ password combinations that permit access to restricted university electronic resources"; IRB research data with privacy obligations; proprietary or classified data; information excluded from FOIA. Storage: "When feasible, the storage media and/or the files themselves should be password-protected and/or encrypted." Transmission of electronic documents should be "encrypted". Disposal: electronic data "permanently deleted". Names and e-mail addresses are not listed. — [UP 07-2](https://policies.wayne.edu/it/07-02-confidential-information)
- Strong Password Standard (effective August 1, 2008; revised May 18, 2022): "Never share your password with anyone, including managers, co-workers, administrative assistants, system administrators, family members, friends, or employees of the C&IT Help Desk." "Never write passwords down and store them near your computer." "Never save passwords in web browsers or computer programs; instead, type them in each time you need them." — [Strong Password Standard](https://tech.wayne.edu/about/strong-password-standard)
- Personal devices standard (scope: "all personal devices that are used to access Wayne State electronic systems"): "Non-public data must not be stored on personal devices without an approved exception"; with an exception, data requires encryption using "an approved methodology"; devices must "Be capable of obtaining security updates and keep up-to-date", run "up-to-date anti-virus/anti-malware software", be "secured with a strong password, passphrase or passcode"; prohibited: "accessing or storing administrator or root type credentials to an enterprise system" on personal devices; MFA mandatory for remote access; "Direct Access or RDP without the use of VPN are not permitted for personal devices." No effective date shown. — [Standards for personal devices accessing university resources](https://tech.wayne.edu/policies/personaldevices)
- Standard for System Integrity (scope: "all electronic systems that provide computer services, or that store or process university data, whether managed centrally by C&IT or at a departmental support level"): external data flows "must use secure protocols, if a secure protocol is not available the data must be encapsulated by a VPN or other similar method"; data at rest "should be encrypted based on data sensitivity"; systems need "a C&IT-approved mechanism for protecting against malicious code". — [Standard for System Integrity](https://tech.wayne.edu/policies/system-integrity)
- Access/auth standard on stored credentials: shared-account "passwords must be stored in an approved Enterprise Password Manager." — [access-auth standard](https://tech.wayne.edu/policies/access-auth)
- FERPA directory information is the exception in UP 07-2's student-records item; C&IT's index links the Registrar's FERPA page for what counts as directory information. — [tech.wayne.edu/policies](https://tech.wayne.edu/policies)

### Inferences
- An API token is not a WSU "Access ID/ password combination", so UP 07-2 does not literally classify it; but a token that unlocks a paid Anthropic account is the same kind of secret, and the standards' direction (password-protect or encrypt at rest; do not save credentials in programs; use secure protocols in transit) is the standard a license would hold users to. Mode-600 files in a home directory that Rule 3 (KB 20254) says is "protected from other users" satisfy the letter of "password-protected"; they do not satisfy "encrypted".
- AccessID and e-mail address are not in UP 07-2's confidential list and are routinely public in the WSU directory; a license file containing them is "non-public" only in the loose sense used by the personal-devices standard, which is undefined.
- The personal-devices standard is written for accessing "enterprise systems"; whether the Grid is an enterprise system is not stated (UP 07-1's examples are Banner, Blackboard, Cognos, OneCard, email).

### Gaps
- No definition of "non-public data" anywhere found; no data-classification tiers; no rule on API tokens, secrets management for research users, or third-party tool data handling (beyond MFA for cloud services holding WSU data).
- "Data and Log Retention Practices" PDF (linked from tech.wayne.edu/policies) was not read.

## Key question 5: Using "Wayne State" or "WSU" in a software product name — APPM 10.12 and brand guidelines

### Takeaway
APPM 10.12 defines University Trademarks broadly ("names, nicknames … and other indicia"), restricts their use "in conjunction with their organization's name" to Registered Student Organizations and official departments, forbids use alongside another entity's name without written permission from both, and gives approval authority to the Office of Marketing and Communications; every approval is one-time. The brand pages address merchandise and logos, not software names; none describes a permission process for naming an app.

### Cited Findings
- Definition: University Trademarks are "registered trademarks, as well as other symbols, marks, names, nicknames, seals, logos, mascots, slogans, school colors, landmarks, and other indicia." — [APPM 10.12](https://policies.wayne.edu/appm/10-12-licensing-trademarks-and-merchandise)
- Purpose (summary; fragments verbatim): to ensure "the Wayne State University name is protected and its use is compatible with Wayne State's brand", that products or services bearing University Trademarks "are of high quality", and to prevent "misleading or inaccurate representations" of Wayne State's relationship to other entities. — [APPM 10.12](https://policies.wayne.edu/appm/10-12-licensing-trademarks-and-merchandise)
- Scope: applies to "Wayne State University employees, students and alumni groups sponsoring the production or sale of any goods or services bearing University Trademarks or indicia for sale or giveaways", manufacturers/distributors, and "any external for-or-nonprofit entity or individual seeking to use University Trademarks in any printed or digital collateral materials or media." — [APPM 10.12](https://policies.wayne.edu/appm/10-12-licensing-trademarks-and-merchandise)
- Who may use the marks with their own name: "Only Registered Student Organizations ("RSOs") of Wayne State and official University departments or groups are allowed to use University Trademarks in conjunction with their organization's name." — [APPM 10.12](https://policies.wayne.edu/appm/10-12-licensing-trademarks-and-merchandise)
- Co-branding: "University Trademarks may not be used in conjunction with the name or trademark(s) of any other entity without the prior written permission of that entity and Wayne State." — [APPM 10.12](https://policies.wayne.edu/appm/10-12-licensing-trademarks-and-merchandise)
- Approval authority and one-time nature: approvals come from the "Office of Marketing and Communications"; "Approval to use a University Trademark for a one-time application only (i.e. on a t-shirt) does not constitute approval to use the University Trademark again." — [APPM 10.12](https://policies.wayne.edu/appm/10-12-licensing-trademarks-and-merchandise)
- Exempt use: "Use of University Trademarks in the ordinary course of conducting University business provided no changes are made to the design or approved colors of University Trademarks." — [APPM 10.12](https://policies.wayne.edu/appm/10-12-licensing-trademarks-and-merchandise)
- Merchandising: "All merchandising activity, whether internal or external including promotional giveaways must be conducted through a company licensed to use University Trademarks." — [APPM 10.12](https://policies.wayne.edu/appm/10-12-licensing-trademarks-and-merchandise)
- Licensing office page: internal units must source through "a company licensed with Wayne State" or "submit a waiver request before you can place an order"; commercial producers "contact our licensing agents, CLC"; small crafters may apply for a "crafter's license"; contact brittani.hobbs@wayne.edu. The page addresses physical merchandise only; apps, software, websites and domain names are not mentioned. — [Licensing and branded merchandise](https://universityrelations.wayne.edu/ucam/licensing) (mac.wayne.edu/marketing/licensing redirects here)
- Editorial style: "Use Wayne State University on first reference." "Wayne State can be used on second reference, along with WSU (though please be mindful of this for SEO purposes)." "Never use Wayne by itself when referring to the university." For affiliated entities: "On first reference, include Wayne State University", as "Wayne State University's [entity name]" or "[Entity name] at Wayne State University". — [Editorial style guide](https://universityrelations.wayne.edu/ucam/style-guide) (mac.wayne.edu/editorial/style-guide redirects here)
- Identity guidelines: "Other logo graphics that exist outside of the standards in this document may not be supported by University Communications and Marketing." Questions to universityrelations@wayne.edu. — [Identity guidelines](https://universityrelations.wayne.edu/ucam/identity-guidelines)

### Inferences
- A product named with "Wayne State" or "WSU" by an individual (not an RSO or department) falls outside the only class APPM 10.12 permits to pair the marks with its own name, and pairing "WSU" with a vendor/product name is the co-branding case requiring written permission from Marketing and Communications. A name that avoids the marks entirely (e.g. "Ashley") sidesteps the policy; describing it as "for the Wayne State University Grid" in text follows the style guide's "[Entity] at Wayne State University" pattern but is still descriptive use the policy does not expressly exempt.
- No APPM 10.12 revision date is shown on the policy page or discoverable from the APPM index.

### Gaps
- No written process for approving a software/app name or a domain using the marks; the licensing page's process (licensed vendors, CLC, waiver form) is for merchandise. The full "Identity Style Guide" PDF referenced by the identity page was not read.

## Key question 6: Rules on students installing browser/editor extensions or "unsanctioned software" on personal laptops used with WSU resources

### Takeaway
No WSU rule on installing extensions or unsanctioned software on personal devices exists in the documents found. The personal-devices standard sets hygiene requirements (updates, anti-malware, strong passcode, MFA, no non-public data, no admin credentials) but does not restrict software; the software page's purchasing rule and the Administrative Rights policy concern university-owned or enterprise systems.

### Cited Findings
- Personal devices standard requirements (see Key question 4 for quotations): update capability, anti-virus/anti-malware, strong passcode, MFA for remote access, no non-public data without exception, no storage of "administrator or root type credentials to an enterprise system". Nothing on software installation or extensions. — [Personal devices standard](https://tech.wayne.edu/policies/personaldevices)
- System Integrity standard "does not specifically prohibit unauthorized software installation or browser extensions" (fetcher's explicit finding after full read). — [Standard for System Integrity](https://tech.wayne.edu/policies/system-integrity)
- Software page: "All software must be purchased via the Software Webstore, WayneBuy, or other institutional purchasing processes." "Exceptions may be made with special approval before purchase." "Software not included here is not currently supported by the Help Desk." No statements on free/open-source software, extensions or AI tools. — [tech.wayne.edu/services/software](https://tech.wayne.edu/services/software)
- C&IT's policy index lists an "Administrative Rights Access Policy" (controls granting of admin privileges) at `/position/admin-rights`. — [tech.wayne.edu/policies](https://tech.wayne.edu/policies) (not read in full)
- Student Code of Conduct §2.3 restricts "artificial intelligence (AI) tools/systems in academic coursework, except where such use has been authorized in advance by the instructor and disclosed through appropriate referencing or citation." — [Student Code of Conduct](https://doso.wayne.edu/care/student-conduct/code)
- AI tools page: Claude requires "Purchase approval granted by C&IT exception" (see Key question 3). — [AI at Wayne State](https://tech.wayne.edu/help/wsuai)

### Inferences
- Installing a VS Code extension on a personal laptop is unregulated; the constraints bite on what the extension then does with WSU resources (login node use, credentials, data) rather than on installation.
- The AI-tools page and Code of Conduct §2.3 mean a student using Ashley for coursework needs instructor authorization; for research use the page's "C&IT exception" language applies to purchases, and it is unclear whether a free or self-funded Claude account by a student is within its scope.

### Gaps
- No rule on unsanctioned software on personal devices; the Administrative Rights Access Policy was not read (it governs admin rights on managed systems).

## Key question 8: Grid rules on walltime, "requeue"/preemptible QoS use, and courtesy limits a per-student persistent job must respect

### Takeaway
A time limit is mandatory on every job; primary QoS allows up to 30 days and secondary up to 90; the requeue QoS is "Unlimited use" but jobs "might get killed and requeued" and are "Single node only". The written courtesy rules are the 20-process login-node cap and the stated purpose of walltimes (owners' timely access to purchased hardware); there is no published per-user job-count or fair-share rule.

### Cited Findings
- "A maximum walltime for a job is required under all conditions. Jobs submitted without providing a time limit will be rejected by the scheduler. Walltime is required in order to keep the job wait times reasonable. Without a walltime, resources would be tied up indefinitely by some users leaving other users unable to submit any work." — [Why walltime requirements are important, KB 20274](https://services.wayne.edu/TDClient/277/Portal/KB/Article/20274/Why-walltime-requirements-are-important)
- "The job duration limits are tuned in an effort to get the best value from the hardware we have. This prevents jobs from going defunct and forgotten about by the job owner indefinitely consuming resources that could be used by others." — [KB 20274](https://services.wayne.edu/TDClient/277/Portal/KB/Article/20274/Why-walltime-requirements-are-important)
- "A significant portion of the available resources is purchased by grants, departments, individual researchers, etc. Walltime is required to ensure the respective owners have timely access to their hardware." — [KB 20274](https://services.wayne.edu/TDClient/277/Portal/KB/Article/20274/Why-walltime-requirements-are-important)
- Per-QoS maxima as stated in the same article: primary QoS maximum walltime 30 days; secondary QoS maximum walltime 90 days (the fetcher reported these figures from the article; the search engine's rendering of the article reads "The primary QoS has a maximum walltime of 30 days. The secondary QoS has a maximum walltime of 90 days."). GPU, requeue, debug and express maxima are not given. — [KB 20274](https://services.wayne.edu/TDClient/277/Portal/KB/Article/20274/Why-walltime-requirements-are-important)
- Accepted formats: "minutes", "minutes:seconds", "hours:minutes:seconds", "days-hours", "days-hours:minutes" and "days-hours:minutes:seconds"; directive `-t, --time=<time>`. — [KB 20274](https://services.wayne.edu/TDClient/277/Portal/KB/Article/20274/Why-walltime-requirements-are-important)
- QoS descriptions: Primary — "This contains our fastest publicly available nodes. It has a core limit of 512 cores"; Secondary — "This contains our old equipment. Slow nodes that may still be useful for high throughput jobs"; GPU — "Gpu may be found here"; Express — "If you own equipment, you can submit your jobs here and kick everyone else off them"; Debug — "If you have filled your allocations and want some space to work on stuff"; Requeue — "Unlimited use. Your jobs might get killed and requeued though. Single node only". Also: "A maximum time limit for the job is required under all conditions." — [Introduction to Slurm, KB 20353](https://services.wayne.edu/TDClient/277/Portal/KB/ArticleDet?ID=20353)
- The run-and-monitor article contains no QoS limits, preemption rules, login-node statements or etiquette language (explicit negative finding after full read). — [KB 20269](https://services.wayne.edu/TDClient/277/Portal/KB/ArticleDet?ID=20269)
- Login-node caps and consequences: see Key question 1 (Rules 1–2, KB 20254; KB 20349).
- Maintenance windows a persistent job will hit: third week of May, first week of December, first week of March (KB 20254, Rule 7).

### Inferences
- An hours-to-a-day per-student job is well inside the 30-day primary limit and needs only an explicit `--time`. On requeue QoS the job is preemptible by design ("might get killed and requeued"), so the tool must tolerate being killed and restarted from its batch script, and must be single-node.
- "Express" is owner-only ("kick everyone else off"); a student tool should not target it unless the lab owns the nodes.
- The absence of a per-user concurrent-job cap in writing does not mean none is enforced in Slurm; only `sacctmgr`/`scontrol` on the cluster would show it.

### Gaps
- No published per-user job-count, core-hour, or fair-share limits; no published maxima for gpu/requeue/debug/express; no published rule on idle interactive allocations; no last-modified dates on any of the KB articles.
