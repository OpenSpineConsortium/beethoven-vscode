# Anthropic's current terms as they apply to a third-party tool that runs Claude Code on a user's behalf (Ashley)

Research date: 2026-09-28. All quotations are from the live pages on that date unless a different date is stated.

How to read the quotations. Six pages were retrieved as full page text and are quoted verbatim from that text: the Claude Code "Legal and compliance", "Authentication", "Run Claude Code programmatically" (headless), "GitHub Actions", "Agent SDK overview" and "Remote Control" pages. The Consumer Terms, Commercial Terms, Usage Policy, Trademark Guidelines, the two Help Center articles and the press articles came back through an extraction pass rather than as raw page text; the quoted sentences are as that pass rendered them and should be checked against the live page before anyone relies on the exact wording. Nothing here is legal advice; each section separates "the text says" (Cited Findings) from "this likely means" (Inferences).

## Key question 1: Consumer Terms of Service (claude.ai) — account sharing, one person per account, automated access, third-party software, reselling, credentials

### Takeaway
The live Consumer Terms are "Effective October 8, 2025". They forbid sharing account credentials or "mak[ing] your Account available to anyone else", forbid access "through automated or non-human means" except via an API key "or where we otherwise explicitly permit it", and forbid reselling. There is no clause that names third-party software as such; the automation and credential clauses are the operative ones.

### Cited Findings
- Effective date: "Effective October 8, 2025", with a link to a previous version in the legal archive. — [Consumer Terms of Service](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/consumer-terms)
- Scope: "These Terms are a contract between you and Anthropic, PBC...for individuals, along with any associated apps, software, and websites." Commercial users are directed to the Commercial Terms. — [Consumer Terms of Service](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/consumer-terms)
- Section 2 (Account creation and access): "You may not share your Account login information, Anthropic API key, or Account credentials with anyone else." "You also may not make your Account available to anyone else." "You are responsible for all activity occurring under your Account". — [Consumer Terms of Service](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/consumer-terms)
- Section 2, age: "You must be at least 18 years old or the minimum age required to consent to use the Services in your location, whichever is higher." — [Consumer Terms of Service](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/consumer-terms)
- Section 3 (Use of our Services), prohibited uses, automated access: "Except when you are accessing our Services via an Anthropic API Key or where we otherwise explicitly permit it, to access the Services through automated or non-human means, whether through a bot, script, or otherwise." — [Consumer Terms of Service](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/consumer-terms)
- Section 3, scraping: "To crawl, scrape, or otherwise harvest data or information from our Services other than as permitted under these Terms." — [Consumer Terms of Service](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/consumer-terms)
- Section 3, competing products and resale: "To develop any products or services that compete with our Services, including to develop or train any artificial intelligence or machine learning algorithms or models or resell the Services." — [Consumer Terms of Service](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/consumer-terms)

- Section 3 (Evaluation and Additional Services): "Use of our Services for evaluation purposes are for your personal, non-commercial use only." — [Consumer Terms of Service](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/consumer-terms)
- Section 4 (Inputs, Outputs, Actions, and Materials): "Actions" are described as features that "enable the Services to take actions on your behalf, such as software manipulation, data processing, and system interactions." By submitting Inputs the user warrants "that you have all rights, licenses, and permissions that are necessary for us to process the Inputs...including for example, to integrate with third-party services, to share Materials with others at your direction." — [Consumer Terms of Service](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/consumer-terms)
- Section 7 (third-party content): "Our Services may use or be used in connection with third-party content...services, or integrations. We do not control or accept responsibility for any loss or damage." — [Consumer Terms of Service](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/consumer-terms)
- Section 12 (General terms), termination: "We may suspend or terminate your access to the Services (including any Subscriptions) at any time without notice to you if we believe that you have breached these Terms." — [Consumer Terms of Service](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/consumer-terms)
- The extraction found no clause in the Consumer Terms on usage throttling, "fair use" or "ordinary use"; that language lives in the Claude Code legal page (see Key question 3). — [Consumer Terms of Service](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/consumer-terms)

### Inferences
- The credential clause is written around persons ("with anyone else", "available to anyone else"). Copying a student's own token to the student's own HPC account moves the credential between machines, not between people; the text does not address multi-machine use by one person. Whether a vendor's software handling the copy counts as the vendor "collecting or storing" the credential is governed by the Claude Code legal page (Key question 3), not by this clause.
- The automation prohibition has an explicit carve-out, "where we otherwise explicitly permit it". Anthropic's own docs describe `claude setup-token` as being "For CI pipelines, scripts, or other environments where interactive browser login isn't available" (Key question 3). This likely means `claude -p` under a subscription is a permitted form of automation when run by the subscriber in the unmodified binary; it does not by itself say a third party may orchestrate it.
- "Resell the Services" is the only resale language in the Consumer Terms. A per-seat license for the vendor's own software, with no charge for Claude usage, is not the same act as reselling Claude; the Claude Code legal page addresses this more precisely (Key question 3).

### Gaps
- Section 2's full paragraph (including any wording about organizational accounts) could not be quoted in full; the extraction summarized it. Re-read the live page for exact wording.
- No text in the Consumer Terms names "third-party software" or "harnesses". That concept exists only in the Claude Code docs and in Anthropic's 2026 statements to the press.

## Key question 2: Usage Policy (universal) — consumer-account tooling, automation, medical/clinical context

### Takeaway
The live Usage Policy is "Effective September 15, 2025". It has no clause on third-party tools or automation per se, but it applies to "passthrough access", it treats healthcare/medical guidance as a High-Risk use case with a professional-review and AI-disclosure requirement, and it points agentic use to a Help Center article (updated March 16, 2026) that lists concrete prohibited agent behaviours.

### Cited Findings
- Effective date: "Effective September 15, 2025". — [Usage Policy](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/aup)
- Who it binds: the policy applies to "anyone who can submit inputs to Anthropic's products and/or services, including via any authorized resellers or passthrough access". — [Usage Policy](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/aup)
- Guardrail bypass: users may not "Intentionally bypass capabilities, restrictions, or guardrails established within our products for the purposes of instructing the model to produce harmful outputs (e.g., jailbreaking or prompt injection) without prior authorization from Anthropic". — [Usage Policy](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/aup)
- Distillation: prohibits "Utilization of inputs and outputs to train an AI model (e.g., 'model scraping' or 'model distillation') without prior authorization from Anthropic". — [Usage Policy](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/aup)
- Ban evasion / access facilitation: prohibits "Circumvent a ban through the use of a different account, such as the creation of a new account, use of an existing account, or providing access to a person or entity that was previously banned" and "Access or facilitate account or API access to Claude to persons, entities, or users in violation of our Supported Regions Policy". — [Usage Policy](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/aup)
- High-Risk use case, healthcare: "Use cases related to healthcare decisions, medical diagnosis, patient care, therapy, mental health, or other medical guidance. Wellness advice (e.g., advice on sleep, stress, nutrition, exercise, etc.) does not fall under this category". — [Usage Policy](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/aup)
- High-Risk safeguards: "When using our products or services to provide advice, recommendations, or in subjective decision-making directly affecting individuals or consumers, a qualified professional in that field must review the content or decision prior to dissemination or finalization." and "If model outputs are presented directly to individuals or consumers, you must disclose to them that you are using AI to help produce your advice, decisions, or recommendations." — [Usage Policy](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/aup)
- Agentic use: "Agentic use cases must still comply with the Usage Policy. We provide examples of Usage Policy prohibitions in the context of agentic use in this Help Center article". — [Usage Policy](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/aup)
- The agentic Help Center article ("Last Updated: March 16, 2026") lists, among others: "Access or modify another person's account using their stored credentials without authorization"; "Install malware, backdoors, or monitoring software without authorization"; "Execute commands that attempt privilege escalation or system exploitation"; "Create or manage multiple accounts to evade detection or circumvent platform safeguards"; "Overwhelm servers with traffic to disrupt services (e.g., DDoS attacks)". — [Using Agents According to Our Usage Policy](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/12005017-using-agents-according-to-our-usage-policy)
- The extraction found no Usage Policy clause on circumventing rate or usage limits, and none specifically on wrappers or programmatic use of consumer accounts. — [Usage Policy](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/aup)

### Inferences
- A research-only tool for medical students that does not produce patient-facing or clinical advice is outside the stated High-Risk definition ("healthcare decisions, medical diagnosis, patient care..."). If Ashley ever surfaces model output that a student could take as medical guidance, the professional-review and disclosure duties would attach.
- The agentic article's "monitoring software without authorization" and "another person's account using their stored credentials" items are the ones an automated diagnostics pipeline or an HPC-side agent should be designed away from: authorization from the student, and only the student's own account, are the distinguishing facts.

### Gaps
- The Usage Policy's own text on "Supported Regions" and any per-product terms were not retrieved.
- No text was found that speaks to a vendor collecting diagnostic reports from its users; that is a privacy-law and vendor-privacy-policy matter, not something the Usage Policy addresses.

## Key question 3: Claude Code specific terms — Legal and compliance page, setup-token, CLAUDE_CODE_OAUTH_TOKEN, Agent SDK note, headless `-p` under subscriptions, and 2026 enforcement statements

### Takeaway
The Claude Code "Legal and compliance" page is the most specific text. It says OAuth authentication "is intended exclusively for purchasers of Claude Free, Pro, Max, Team, and Enterprise subscription plans and is designed to support ordinary use of Claude Code and other native Anthropic applications"; that "Anthropic does not permit third-party developers to offer Claude.ai login into their own applications, or to route requests through Free, Pro, or Max plan credentials on behalf of their users"; and that "developers may not collect, store, or intermediate Claude.ai credentials or session tokens — sign-in to a Claude account must complete through Anthropic's own flow." It also sets three conditions for anyone who preinstalls or runs Claude Code in their own product: agree to the Commercial Terms, do not modify the binary or remove any sign-in method, and never pay for, resell or intermediate Claude usage for end users, each of whom must authenticate with their own credentials. `claude setup-token` is documented for "CI pipelines, scripts", requires a paid plan, and "can only make model requests". Since April 4, 2026 Anthropic has stated publicly that "Using Claude subscriptions with third-party tools isn't permitted under our Terms of Service" and has technically diverted such usage to paid "extra usage".

### Inferences
- Ashley's feature (1), as described, does three things the text treats differently. Running the unmodified `claude -p` binary under the student's own subscription is squarely within "an end user from signing in to the unmodified Claude Code binary with their own Claude subscription". Storing the setup-token in the OS keychain and copying it to the HPC account is where the text bites: the sentence "developers may not collect, store, or intermediate Claude.ai credentials or session tokens" has no carve-out for tokens the user minted themselves; the only carve-outs are for "their own API keys or third-party inference provider credentials" and for the user signing in to the unmodified binary. The GitHub Actions docs do show Anthropic itself telling users to store a setup-token as a repository secret, so storing one's own token is a documented act by the user; the open question is whether a vendor's extension doing the storing and copying on the user's behalf is "the developer" storing it. That question is not answered by any text retrieved.
- "Route requests through Free, Pro, or Max plan credentials on behalf of their users" is the sentence most directly aimed at a vendor whose product issues the model calls. If Ashley's persistent HPC process is the thing issuing `claude -p` calls on the student's behalf, a plain reading puts it within that sentence; the counter-reading is that the requests are routed by Claude Code itself, not by the vendor, and that Claude Code is the "native Anthropic application" the OAuth clause contemplates. Anthropic's April 2026 enforcement drew the line at "third-party tools like OpenClaw", i.e. products that call the model themselves; whether a product that only orchestrates the official binary is on the same side of the line is unknown from the text. The claude-mem maintainer read subprocess spawning as inside the ban; that is one person's reading.
- Note the asymmetry in the live text: the "route requests through" prohibition names "Free, Pro, or Max plan credentials", while the OAuth-purpose sentence names Free, Pro, Max, Team and Enterprise. Team and Enterprise are also governed by the Commercial Terms. Whether that omission is deliberate is not stated; it should not be relied on.
- "Advertised usage limits ... assume ordinary, individual usage" is the hook Anthropic used in April 2026 (Cherny: "weren't built for the usage patterns of these third-party tools"). A persistent HPC process that only acts when a student asks is closer to "ordinary, individual usage" than an autonomous agent loop; the text gives no threshold.
- The February 2026 wording reported by The Register ("in any other product, tool, or service — including the Agent SDK") was broader than the current wording. The rewrite added the explicit carve-outs for API keys and for "signing in to the unmodified Claude Code binary", which suggests Anthropic deliberately distinguished "runs the real Claude Code" from "is a harness". That is an inference from the diff, not a statement Anthropic has made.
- Feature (4), local `claude -p` with web search on the student's own machine and account, is the same act as feature (1) minus the token copy, so it inherits the "on behalf of their users" question but not the credential-storage one if the extension relies on the login Claude Code already holds rather than storing a token itself.
- The "contact sales" sentence is Anthropic's stated route for "questions about permitted authentication methods for your use case". Given the ambiguity above, that is the only text-backed way to get a binding answer.

### Gaps
- No Anthropic text defines "third-party harness", "intermediate", or "on behalf of their users" with enough precision to classify a VS Code extension that launches the unmodified CLI.
- The archived February–March 2026 version of the legal page could not be fetched (web.archive.org and archive.ph both blocked here); the earlier wording rests on The Register's quotation.
- No support article was found that says in Anthropic's own words whether `claude -p` (headless) use draws on the subscription; the closest is the Authentication page's statement that the setup-token "authenticates with your Claude subscription" and the GitHub Actions page's "If you authenticate with an OAuth token, runs use your Claude subscription instead of API billing."
- The `/login` credential lifetime is not stated in the docs (only the three-day warning). A user report says the refresh token lasts about 28 days (see Key question 4); that is not an Anthropic statement.

## Key question 4: Remote Control — requirements, plans, account binding, running on a server, session ownership, security

### Takeaway
Remote Control requires a claude.ai subscription login (Pro, Max, Team or Enterprise; "API keys are not supported") performed with `claude auth login`/`/login`; a setup-token cannot establish it ("Remote Control requires a full-scope login token"). The docs expressly contemplate running it on a remote machine over SSH inside `tmux`/`screen`. A session binds to the signed-in account: "Auto-connect signs in with your own claude.ai account, so a session it starts appears only in your own account's Claude apps and grants no one else access." Transcripts are stored on Anthropic servers while connected.

### Cited Findings
- Requirements: "Subscription: available on Pro, Max, Team, and Enterprise plans. API keys are not supported. On Team and Enterprise, an Owner must first enable the Remote Control toggle in Claude Code admin settings." — [Remote Control](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/remote-control)
- Authentication: "run `claude` and use `/login` to sign in through claude.ai if you haven't already. - The docs contain no clause on who may start a Remote Control server for whom, and no statement that the machine must be one the user physically sits at; the only ownership statement is the "your own claude.ai account... grants no one else access" sentence above. — [Remote Control](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/remote-control)

### Inferences
- Feature (3) as designed (a `claude auth login` performed by the student on the HPC node, then `claude remote-control` under `tmux`) matches the documented pattern for a remote machine. The doc's own words for the fix to every credential problem are "Run `claude auth login`", which is what Ashley proposes.
- Because the setup-token from feature (1) cannot establish Remote Control, the node will hold two credentials: the long-lived token (for the `-p` worker) and the full-scope `/login` credential (for Remote Control). Per the precedence list, `CLAUDE_CODE_OAUTH_TOKEN` outranks the `/login` credential in any process where the variable is set, so the two processes must have different environments; the user report in #96076 describes exactly this collision.
- The full-scope login's expiry (three-day warning in the docs, roughly 28-day refresh per the user report) means the Remote Control host will need the student to re-run `claude auth login` periodically; the docs say an unattended session "stops making progress once the credential expires and can't recover until you sign in again." Ashley would need a re-login prompt, and cannot automate it without handling the credential (see Key question 3).
- Trusted Devices can be turned on by a Pro/Max user themselves; if a student enables it, the phone must be enrolled before it can steer the node session.

### Gaps
- No Anthropic text states whether a Remote Control server that a third-party tool launched (as opposed to the user typing `claude remote-control`) is treated differently. The one-time `Enable Remote Control? (y/n)` consent is a terminal prompt the tool would have to let the student answer.
- Trusted Devices section body (lines on enrolment mechanics) was not quoted in full.

## Key question 9: How the retrieved text maps onto Ashley's six features (synthesis; "this likely means", not advice)

### Takeaway
On the texts alone: features (4), (6) and the vendor-side half of (5) have clear support; feature (3) matches a documented pattern but must use a separate full-scope login that expires; feature (2) is permitted but must be engineered around credential precedence; feature (1) sits on the two sentences Anthropic added in 2026 ("route requests through Free, Pro, or Max plan credentials on behalf of their users" and "may not collect, store, or intermediate Claude.ai credentials or session tokens") and no retrieved text resolves whether an extension that stores and copies a user-minted setup-token and drives the unmodified `claude -p` is inside or outside them.

### Cited Findings
- The single most relevant paragraph, again, verbatim: "Anthropic does not permit third-party developers to offer Claude.ai login into their own applications, or to route requests through Free, Pro, or Max plan credentials on behalf of their users. Moreover, developers may not collect, store, or intermediate Claude.ai credentials or session tokens — sign-in to a Claude account must complete through Anthropic's own flow." — [Legal and compliance](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/legal-and-compliance)
- The carve-out that Ashley's `-p` worker most resembles: "Nor does it prevent an end user from signing in to the unmodified Claude Code binary with their own Claude subscription, including where a platform hosts Claude Code". — [Legal and compliance](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/legal-and-compliance)
- The route to a binding answer: "For questions about permitted authentication methods for your use case, please contact sales". — [Legal and compliance](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/legal-and-compliance)
- Anthropic's stated enforcement posture: "Anthropic reserves the right to take measures to enforce these restrictions and may do so without prior notice." and Consumer Terms Section 12: suspension or termination "at any time without notice to you if we believe that you have breached these Terms." — [Legal and compliance](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/legal-and-compliance); [Consumer Terms of Service](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/consumer-terms)
- Enforcement is technical as well as contractual: since April 4, 2026 "third-party tools will draw from extra usage instead of subscription limits". — [The Register, 2026-04-06](https://www.theregister.com/software/2026/04/06/anthropic-closes-door-on-subscription-use-of-openclaw/5222854)

### Inferences
- Feature (1): the design fact that matters most is who touches the token. If the student runs `claude setup-token` and pastes the token into Ashley, Ashley then "store[s]" it (keychain) and moves it (HPC). The lower-exposure variants suggested by the texts are (a) let the student place the token in the HPC environment themselves and have Ashley only launch `claude -p` where Claude Code already finds a credential, or (b) let the student run `claude auth login` on the node (Anthropic's own flow, credentials stored by Claude Code itself), accepting the shorter expiry. Neither variant is declared safe by any text; both avoid the vendor storing the token.
- Feature (1), the persistent process: "ordinary, individual usage" and the April 2026 statements point to the same practical test Anthropic has applied so far, which is workload shape. A process that idles until a student asks is closer to Claude Code's ordinary use than an autonomous loop; the text gives no threshold and Anthropic has enforced by diverting billing, not only by contract.
- Feature (2): permitted by the texts; must keep `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` out of the subscription worker's environment because in `-p` mode "the key is always used when present".
- Feature (3): the documented pattern is `claude auth login` on the node plus `tmux`; setup-tokens cannot do it; the login will expire and needs the student, not the tool, to renew it; the session "grants no one else access".
- Feature (4): the same act as (1) without token copying; if Ashley launches `claude -p` against whatever credential the student's own Claude Code install holds, the vendor stores nothing.
- Feature (5): the vendor-side pipeline on the vendor's key is textbook Commercial Terms use. Any student-machine diagnosis on student credentials should be student-initiated and opt-in, because the vendor is the beneficiary and the "on behalf of their users" sentence is the one Anthropic has enforced.
- Feature (6): charging for the tool is not "pay[ing] for, resell[ing], or intermediat[ing] Claude usage"; it is not addressed and not prohibited by the texts retrieved. It does not change the analysis of (1).
- Given that the legal page names "contact sales" for exactly this question and that the February and April 2026 rewrites show the wording is still moving, a written answer from Anthropic on the specific pattern (extension stores a user-minted setup-token, copies it to the user's own HPC account, runs unmodified `claude -p`) is the only thing that would settle feature (1).

### Gaps
- No Anthropic text classifies "runs the unmodified CLI on the user's behalf" one way or the other; this is the central unknown.
- The exact enforcement mechanism (how Anthropic distinguishes a third-party harness from Claude Code) has not been published; press accounts describe "spoofing" safeguards and billing diversion but not the detection rule.
- Not retrieved: an archived February–March 2026 copy of the legal page; the Consumer Terms' Section 2 in full; the Commercial Terms' D.1/D.2 and data sections; the Trademark Guidelines' marks list; any Anthropic statement defining "third-party harness".
