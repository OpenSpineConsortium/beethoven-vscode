# Anthropic's credential rule draws Ashley's operating boundary

Ashley can exist as a launcher, a license gate, a diagnostics channel and a phone bridge, but not as a credential broker: every policy examined tolerates a VS Code extension that starts the unmodified Claude Code binary under a student's own subscription, and only one policy, Anthropic's Claude Code legal page, speaks directly to what Ashley's wizard does today, which is capture the `claude setup-token` output, store it in the keychain, copy it to the grid home, and rotate across several accounts in `~/.ashley/accounts`. That page says "developers may not collect, store, or intermediate Claude.ai credentials or session tokens" and that "Anthropic does not permit third-party developers ... to route requests through Free, Pro, or Max plan credentials on behalf of their users", while carving out "an end user ... signing in to the unmodified Claude Code binary with their own Claude subscription, including where a platform hosts Claude Code" ([Legal and compliance](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/legal-and-compliance)). Since April 4, 2026 Anthropic has enforced this technically, diverting third-party-tool traffic off subscriptions and stating that "Using Claude subscriptions with third-party tools isn't permitted under our Terms of Service" ([The Register, 2026-04-06](https://www.theregister.com/software/2026/04/06/anthropic-closes-door-on-subscription-use-of-openclaw/5222854)). Wayne State's rules bound where Ashley runs (inside a Slurm allocation, never on Warrior), what it may be called (not "WSU" or "Wayne State" without Marketing and Communications permission), and who may own it (a 1984 Board statute plus the Tenet Code of Conduct both reach work done with institutional support), and the University's 2000 acceptable-use policy bars "personal for-profit purposes" on its IT resources ([UP 00-1](https://wayne.edu/policies/acceptable-use)). GitHub and Hugging Face bind the student, not the tool, and neither addresses a user's own token moved to a second machine the user controls. A Michigan click-through EULA can allocate risk to the student (no refunds for enumerated external events, support only while licensed, indemnity for the student's own breaches) but cannot bind Anthropic, WSU, Microsoft, GitHub, Hugging Face or a Michigan attorney general, cannot waive the Michigan Consumer Protection Act, and cannot turn a readable-JavaScript key check into a DMCA access control in the Sixth Circuit. Of the five desired features, four fit with modest changes; the first, per-person keys on a worker that holds student tokens, fits only after the token handling is redesigned, the licensor is a real legal person, WSU's technology office has been told, and Anthropic sales has answered in writing. This report is research, not legal advice; places where a lawyer must decide are marked **Counsel**.

All quotations are from pages live on 2026-09-28 unless dated otherwise. Where a note relied on an extraction pass rather than raw page text, or a verification pass corrected a finding from the earlier research, that is said.

## Anthropic's texts permit the launcher and forbid the broker, and the middle ground is unwritten

Three Anthropic documents govern a student's Claude Pro or Max account, and they layer. The Consumer Terms ("Effective October 8, 2025") forbid sharing "your Account login information, Anthropic API key, or Account credentials with anyone else", forbid making "your Account available to anyone else", and forbid access "through automated or non-human means, whether through a bot, script, or otherwise" except "via an Anthropic API Key or where we otherwise explicitly permit it"; they also let Anthropic "suspend or terminate your access to the Services (including any Subscriptions) at any time without notice to you if we believe that you have breached these Terms" ([Consumer Terms](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/consumer-terms)). The Usage Policy ("Effective September 15, 2025") reaches "anyone who can submit inputs to Anthropic's products and/or services, including via any authorized resellers or passthrough access", and its agentic companion article (updated March 16, 2026) lists as prohibited "Access or modify another person's account using their stored credentials without authorization", "Install malware, backdoors, or monitoring software without authorization", and "Create or manage multiple accounts to evade detection or circumvent platform safeguards" ([Usage Policy](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/aup); [Using Agents According to Our Usage Policy](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/12005017-using-agents-according-to-our-usage-policy)). The Claude Code legal page is the operative text. Anyone "preinstalling or running Claude Code in your products or services (e.g. in hosted sandboxes or other agent infrastructure)" must agree to the Commercial Terms and meet three conditions: the binary "must not be modified" and no sign-in method removed; customers "may not pay for, resell, or intermediate Claude usage on their end users' behalf"; and "Each end user must authenticate with their own Anthropic API key, Claude subscription plan credentials, or 3P inference provider credential" ([Legal and compliance](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/legal-and-compliance)).

The two sentences that decide Ashley's worker are on the same page. "OAuth authentication is intended exclusively for purchasers of Claude Free, Pro, Max, Team, and Enterprise subscription plans and is designed to support ordinary use of Claude Code and other native Anthropic applications", and "Anthropic does not permit third-party developers to offer Claude.ai login into their own applications, or to route requests through Free, Pro, or Max plan credentials on behalf of their users. Moreover, developers may not collect, store, or intermediate Claude.ai credentials or session tokens — sign-in to a Claude account must complete through Anthropic's own flow" ([Legal and compliance](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/legal-and-compliance)). The carve-outs are equally explicit: customers may provision "their own API keys ... for use by the customer's own authorized users", and nothing "prevent[s] an end user from signing in to the unmodified Claude Code binary with their own Claude subscription, including where a platform hosts Claude Code". The page adds that "Anthropic reserves the right to take measures to enforce these restrictions and may do so without prior notice" and that "For questions about permitted authentication methods for your use case, please contact sales". `claude setup-token` itself is documented for "CI pipelines, scripts, or other environments where interactive browser login isn't available"; it "does not save the token anywhere; copy it and set it as the `CLAUDE_CODE_OAUTH_TOKEN` environment variable", requires "a Pro, Max, Team, or Enterprise plan", and "can only make model requests, so it can't establish Remote Control sessions" ([Authentication](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/authentication)). Bare mode, which Anthropic says "will become the default for `-p` in a future release", "never reads OAuth credentials or the system keychain" ([Run Claude Code programmatically](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/headless)).

Read against that text, Ashley today does three distinct things. Launching the unmodified `claude -p` on the student's node is the carved-out act. Capturing the setup-token in the wizard, storing it in the OS keychain and copying it to the grid home is at least arguably the vendor "collect[ing], stor[ing]" a session token, and no retrieved text has a carve-out for tokens the user minted themselves; the closest analogue is Anthropic's own GitHub Actions guidance telling the user to store a setup-token as a repository secret, which is the user storing it, not a vendor ([Claude Code GitHub Actions](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/github-actions)). Rotating across several subscription accounts on cooldown, which the earlier code review found in `grid/ashleyd.js`, has no defensible reading: if the accounts belong to different people it is making an account "available to anyone else", and in any case it is "manag[ing] multiple accounts to ... circumvent platform safeguards", the very pattern the agentic article names. The enforcement history shows the line moving toward Ashley, not away from it: safeguards "against spoofing the Claude Code harness" in January 2026, a terms clarification on February 20 that named OpenCode, Cline, Cursor and Pi, and the April 4 billing diversion with the statement that subscriptions "weren't built for the usage patterns of these third-party tools" ([VentureBeat, 2026-01-09](https://venturebeat.com/technology/anthropic-cracks-down-on-unauthorized-claude-usage-by-third-party-harnesses); [The Register, 2026-02-20](https://www.theregister.com/2026/02/20/anthropic_clarifies_ban_third_party_claude_access/); [The Next Web, 2026-04-04](https://thenextweb.com/news/anthropic-openclaw-claude-subscription-ban-cost)). What no text settles is whether a product that only orchestrates the official binary is a "third-party harness"; the earlier February wording reported by The Register ("Using OAuth tokens obtained through Claude Free, Pro, or Max accounts in any other product, tool, or service — including the Agent SDK — is not permitted") was broader than the current page, and the rewrite added the "unmodified binary" carve-out, which suggests Anthropic drew a distinction on purpose, but that is an inference from a diff, not a statement. One plugin maintainer reads subprocess spawning under CLI auth as inside the ban ([claude-mem issue #1826](https://github.com/thedotmack/claude-mem/issues/1826)). The only route to a binding answer is the one the page names: a written reply from Anthropic sales on the specific pattern. **Counsel** should frame that request.

Remote Control is the one part of the design that Anthropic's documentation describes almost verbatim. It is "available on Pro, Max, Team, and Enterprise plans. API keys are not supported"; it needs a `/login` session because a setup-token "can only make model requests" and produces the error "Remote Control requires a full-scope login token"; and the docs say outright: "To keep a session running on a remote machine after you disconnect from SSH, start it inside `tmux` or `screen`" ([Remote Control](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/remote-control)). The session binds to one account ("a session it starts appears only in your own account's Claude apps and grants no one else access"), makes "outbound HTTPS requests only and never opens inbound ports", and asks a one-time `Enable Remote Control? (y/n)`. Two facts constrain unattended use: "While Remote Control is connected, the session transcript, including your messages, Claude's responses, and tool activity, is stored on Anthropic servers", and a login within three days of expiry prints a warning, after which "A background session in agent view or a Remote Control session that outlives the login stops making progress once the credential expires and can't recover until you sign in again" ([Authentication](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/authentication)). A user report, not an Anthropic statement, puts the refresh-token life near 28 days and describes the exact collision Ashley will hit when `CLAUDE_CODE_OAUTH_TOKEN` outranks the `/login` credential in the same environment ([anthropics/claude-code issue #96076](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/96076)). For an API-key fallback the Commercial Terms ("Effective June 17, 2025") apply to the key and sit beside the Consumer Terms on the same person; Claude Code documents both credentials coexisting and states that "In non-interactive mode (`-p`), the key is always used when present", so the key must be injected only into the fallback process ([Commercial Terms](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/commercial-terms); [Authentication](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/authentication)). On naming, "You can accurately say, in plain text, that your product has Claude Code preinstalled or that it runs Claude Code", but not use "the Claude Code or Anthropic names or logos as part of your own product, feature, or company name"; the Agent SDK page allows "{YourAgentName} Powered by Claude" and forbids "Claude Code" as a label, and Anthropic's trademark complaint forced the "Clawd" rename in January 2026 ([Legal and compliance](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/legal-and-compliance); [Agent SDK overview](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/agent-sdk/overview); [Wikipedia: OpenClaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenClaw)). "Ashley" passes; a command named "Ask the grid Claude" needs checking against the feature-name rule. Ashley is a research tool and outside the Usage Policy's High-Risk healthcare definition ("healthcare decisions, medical diagnosis, patient care"), but if it ever surfaces output a student could take as medical guidance, the professional-review and AI-disclosure duties attach ([Usage Policy](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/aup)).

## Wayne State regulates where Ashley runs, who owns it, and what it is called

The Grid's written rules are nine numbered items, and one of them is the only Grid rule Ashley could break by design. "You must run all processes on compute nodes by requesting a node through Slurm ... If you are caught running processes on Warrior, the Grid's head node, they will be deleted immediately and your account will be temporarily suspended. If you continuously run processes on Warrior then you will be banned", with a cap of "no more than 20 processes on Warrior" ([HPC Grid Policies, KB 20254](https://services.wayne.edu/TDClient/277/Portal/KB/ArticleDet?ID=20254)). The HPC team already polices VS Code specifically: connecting Remote-SSH without first getting a compute node "will cause VSCode to use resources on the login node Warrior, even if you start an interactive job", and "If you continue to run VSCode this way, your processes on Warrior will be killed" ([KB 20349](https://services.wayne.edu/TDClient/277/Portal/KB/ArticleDet?ID=20349)). Eligibility is "Any WSU student, staff, or faculty member with a valid research-related computational task and a WSU AccessID", at no cost ([Grid FAQs, KB 20229](https://services.wayne.edu/TDClient/277/Portal/KB/Article/20229/Grid-FAQs)). A persistent per-student job is otherwise unaddressed: "A maximum walltime for a job is required under all conditions", with 30 days on the primary QoS and 90 on secondary, and the requeue QoS is "Unlimited use. Your jobs might get killed and requeued though. Single node only" ([KB 20274](https://services.wayne.edu/TDClient/277/Portal/KB/Article/20274/Why-walltime-requirements-are-important); [Introduction to Slurm, KB 20353](https://services.wayne.edu/TDClient/277/Portal/KB/ArticleDet?ID=20353)). Maintenance windows fall in the third week of May, first week of December and first week of March. Nothing in any Grid document speaks to outbound network access from compute nodes, daemons inside allocations, credential files, commercial use or external collaborators; the upstream Merit AUP makes WSU responsible for its "Sub-Users" and bars use "in a manner that precludes or significantly hampers network access by others" ([Merit Acceptable Use Policy](https://www.merit.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Merit_Acceptable_Use_Policy.pdf)). Home directories are "protected from other users", which is the written basis for mode-600 files, and the sanctioned sharing route is a requested group directory ([KB 20254](https://services.wayne.edu/TDClient/277/Portal/KB/ArticleDet?ID=20254)).

University Policy 00-1 (signed May 12, 2000, never revised) is the campus-wide rule, and its §3.3 lists "Using University information technology resources for personal for-profit purposes" alongside "Tampering with software protections or restrictions placed on computer applications or files" and "Subverting restrictions associated with computer accounts"; §3.1 says "Convenience of file or printer sharing is not a sufficient reason for sharing computer accounts" ([UP 00-1](https://policies.wayne.edu/it/00-1-acceptable-use-of-technology)). It has no incidental-use clause and no clause about services or daemons. The for-profit sentence bars a user from using WSU IT for their own profit; a student paying a vendor for a research tool is not on its face within it, but a vendor who is himself a WSU affiliate building, testing and enforcing a paid product on the Grid is the ambiguous case, and the Grid's own eligibility language ("valid research-related computational task") is the nearest workload restriction. Students are disciplined under the Student Code of Conduct, whose §4.17 covers "Misuse or intentional disruption of the University's technological resources" and whose §2.3 requires instructor authorization for AI tools in coursework ([Student Code of Conduct](https://doso.wayne.edu/care/student-conduct/code)). C&IT's own AI page lists Claude as "Purchase approval granted by C&IT exception" through "your department's standard process for software purchase requests"; whether a student's self-funded Pro plan is within that page's scope is not stated ([AI at Wayne State](https://tech.wayne.edu/help/wsuai)).

On identity, no public WSU document addresses third-party applications consuming the Entra tenant sign-in, developer app registrations or user-consent settings. The applicable standard favours what Ashley proposes: "Credentials should utilize central LDAP or Active Directory sources for authentication. Where possible, the central Single-Sign-On (SSO) system should be used", and MFA is mandatory "for any cloud service that processes or stores WSU data" ([Standards for access, authentication, and authorization](https://tech.wayne.edu/policies/access-auth)). VS Code's built-in Microsoft provider signs in through Microsoft's first-party client rather than an app Ashley registers; whether the WSU tenant permits or has admin-consented to it is a tenant setting that has not been tested, and UP 07-1's requirement that systems interacting with enterprise systems be developed "in collaboration with Computing and Information Technology" is written for Banner and e-mail, not editor extensions ([UP 07-1](https://policies.wayne.edu/it/07-01-information-technology-system)). On data, WSU has no tiered classification standard; UP 07-2 lists as confidential "Access ID/ password combinations that permit access to restricted university electronic resources" but not AccessIDs or e-mail addresses alone, and asks that confidential files be "password-protected and/or encrypted" ([UP 07-2](https://policies.wayne.edu/it/07-02-confidential-information)). The Strong Password Standard says "Never save passwords in web browsers or computer programs", and the personal-devices standard bars "accessing or storing administrator or root type credentials to an enterprise system" on personal machines ([Strong Password Standard](https://tech.wayne.edu/about/strong-password-standard); [Personal devices standard](https://tech.wayne.edu/policies/personaldevices)). None mentions API tokens; a token that unlocks a paid Anthropic account is the same kind of secret, and the standards' direction is the standard a license should hold users to.

On naming, APPM 10.12 defines University Trademarks to include "names, nicknames ... and other indicia", permits only "Registered Student Organizations ... and official University departments or groups" to use them "in conjunction with their organization's name", forbids use "in conjunction with the name or trademark(s) of any other entity without the prior written permission of that entity and Wayne State", assigns approval to the Office of Marketing and Communications, and treats each approval as one-time ([APPM 10.12](https://policies.wayne.edu/appm/10-12-licensing-trademarks-and-merchandise)). The current manifest's `ashley-wsu` id and "Ashley: Wayne State Research Environment" display name are the co-branding case; an extension id is permanent once published. On ownership, the earlier research (verified) found that Board of Governors Statute 2.41.04, adopted 1969 and amended July 13, 1984, never names software; its patent part gives WSU "right of first refusal to hold title to all patentable inventions made by non-salaried personnel or other individuals affiliated with the University, if such inventions are made with support of University funds, facilities, or equipment", with students expressly among non-salaried personnel; and its one copyright paragraph asks that "In those cases where the author requests the use of University facilities ... arrangements should be made ... in advance with respect to ... the equity of the University in the finished work" ([BOG 2.41.04](https://bog.wayne.edu/code/2-41-04)). The Technology Commercialization office states "All computer software must be reported to the University for an evaluation of patentability and commercial potential", though the binding union text limits the duty to bargaining-unit members and software that "may be patentable"; its sponsored-research page says copyrightable material "including non-patentable computer software" made by an employee "vests with University"; and its 2026 disclosure form counts "releases via an app store or other download website" as public disclosure and bundles a Memorandum of Understanding that assigns the inventors' rights ([OTC IP policy](https://techtransfer.wayne.edu/techtransfer/researchers-inventors/ip-policy); [OTC template agreements](https://techtransfer.wayne.edu/techtransfer/template-agreement-structures); [2026 disclosure form](https://techtransfer.wayne.edu/documents/modified-wsu-invention-disclosure-form-2026.docx)). The verification pass corrected one point: UP 86-5 §5.2 is a consulting-waiver clause, not a general test, but Grid use still plausibly counts as University support under the statute's own §§.110 and .120 ([UP 86-5](https://policies.wayne.edu/research/86-5-patent-administration)).

The employer side is not clear either, and the verification pass reversed the earlier finding. The DMC resident agreements (public 2023-24, 2024-25 and 2025-26 samples and the author's own 2026-27 agreement) contain no invention, patent, copyright or work-for-hire clause, but §13 binds the resident to Tenet's Standards of Conduct, and the current Tenet Code of Conduct, publicly readable, says: "As an employee, any work you create, in whole or in part, relating to your duties, and/or using Company time, resources, or information, belongs to the Company", listing copyrights, inventions and "Employee work products" ([DMC sample contract 2024-25](https://wsusurgery.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/2024-2025-DMC-Resident-Sample-Contract.pdf); [Tenet Code of Conduct](https://www.tenethealth.com/docs/global/default/code-of-conduct.pdf?sfvrsn=9a67b381_2&c=NS28E)). Whether "Standards of Conduct" means this document, whether a Detroit Education & Research employee is "an employee at Tenet" for that purpose, and whether Ashley "relat[es] to your duties" are open. **Counsel** must resolve which of WSU (affiliate using facilities), Tenet (employee work), or the author holds the copyright before a EULA names a licensor; the copyright line currently names "OpenSpineConsortium", which no source shows to be a legal person (a 2026 paper calls it "a distributed, open-source research collaborative") ([PMC article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13472215/)). Finally, a license keyed to an AccessID lives only as long as the account: non-graduating students are disabled "18 months after the end of the last registered term", employees "on last day of employment", guests at the end of the sponsored period, and DMC residents have their own rows in the matrix; nothing states when a Grid account or home directory is purged, and Tenet ended library services for DMC residents on June 30, 2026 ([Limits on system access v24](https://tech.wayne.edu/depdocs/limits-on-system-access-v24new3122023.pdf); [WSU Libraries DMC guide](https://guides.lib.wayne.edu/dmcwsu)).

## GitHub, Hugging Face and the Marketplace bind the user, not the tool, and none addresses a relocated token

GitHub's Terms of Service (effective April 27, 2026) fix responsibility on the account holder: "Your login may only be used by one person", "You are responsible for all content posted and activity that occurs under your Account", and the API section applies "including use of the API through a third party product that accesses GitHub" ([GitHub ToS](https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/github-terms/github-terms-of-service)). The only token-sharing prohibition is "You may not share API tokens to exceed GitHub's rate limitations." No retrieved GitHub text addresses one person's own token copied to a second machine the same person controls; the configuration is unaddressed rather than permitted. The token Ashley receives through VS Code's `authentication.getSession` is a user access token for Microsoft's own OAuth client (`01ab8ac9400c4e429b23`, hard-coded in the built-in `github-authentication` extension), issued only after the editor's "The extension '{0}' wants to sign in using {1}" consent and per-extension allow-list; VS Code's API describes `accessToken` only as "The access token" and says nothing about where it may go ([VS Code API: authentication](https://code.visualstudio.com/api/references/vscode-api#authentication); [github-authentication config.ts](https://github.com/microsoft/vscode/blob/main/extensions/github-authentication/src/config.ts)). Two consequences follow. A `repo`-scoped token "Grants full access to public and private repositories", so a compromised grid copy exposes the student's whole repository set ([Scopes for OAuth apps](https://docs.github.com/en/apps/oauth-apps/building-oauth-apps/scopes-for-oauth-apps)). And revocation runs through the VS Code app entry in the student's authorized OAuth apps, which revokes it for every extension at once; Ashley cannot revoke its copy independently. GitHub's stated preference is GitHub Apps with "fine-grained permissions ... and ... short-lived tokens", which a long-lived `repo` token on an HPC home is not, though nothing forbids it ([Best practices for creating an OAuth app](https://docs.github.com/en/apps/oauth-apps/building-oauth-apps/best-practices-for-creating-an-oauth-app)). The GitHub Marketplace Developer Agreement governs products listed on GitHub Marketplace and does not reach a Visual Studio Marketplace extension ([GitHub Marketplace Developer Agreement](https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/github-terms/github-marketplace-developer-agreement)).

For the vendor's pipeline, GitHub has no site policy on AI-authored contributions; governance lives in product behaviour. The Actions terms (last updated August 27, 2026) confine hosted runners to "the production, testing, deployment, or publication of the software project associated with the repository" and forbid "Any activity that places a burden on our servers, where that burden is disproportionate" ([GitHub Terms for Additional Products and Features](https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/github-terms/github-terms-for-additional-products-and-features)). Jobs cap at "6 hours of execution time" and `GITHUB_TOKEN` at "1,000 requests per hour per repository" ([GitHub Actions limits](https://docs.github.com/en/actions/reference/limits)). "By default, workflows are not allowed to create or approve pull requests", events from `GITHUB_TOKEN` do not start new runs, and since June 11, 2026 bot-created pull requests run workflows only if a write-access human approves, "a security measure to ensure generated code does not automatically run workflows which may have access to sensitive information" ([Managing GitHub Actions settings](https://docs.github.com/en/repositories/managing-your-repositorys-settings-and-features/enabling-features-for-your-repository/managing-github-actions-settings-for-a-repository); [GitHub Changelog 2026-06-11](https://github.blog/changelog/2026-06-11-bot-created-pull-requests-can-run-workflows-if-approved/)). GitHub's own agent encodes the norms: it "only responds to interactions from users with repository write access", "does not have access to Actions organization or repository secrets", "cannot approve or merge a pull request", and GitHub "Prevents the user who asked Copilot cloud agent to create a pull request from approving it", with an egress firewall because "malicious instructions, could lead to code or other sensitive information being leaked" ([Responsible use of Copilot agents](https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/responsible-use/copilot-coding-agent); [Risks and mitigations](https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/concepts/agents/cloud-agent/risks-and-mitigations); [Customize the agent firewall](https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/how-tos/use-copilot-agents/coding-agent/customize-the-agent-firewall)). The Claude Code GitHub Action follows the same pattern: it "authenticates as the Claude GitHub App" by default, requires that "the triggering user must have write access", rejects bots unless listed in `allowed_bots` ("keeps bots from triggering Claude in a loop"), "does not create pull requests automatically", commits to a `claude/` branch, and for a shared secret says to "authenticate with an API key from the Claude Console rather than an OAuth token" ([Claude Code GitHub Actions](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/github-actions); [claude-code-action security](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/anthropics/claude-code-action/main/docs/security.md)). Private repositories are "confidential to you", subject to GitHub's own automated scanning; GitHub and Hugging Face tokens are secret-scanning partner patterns, but for private organization repositories push protection requires "GitHub Secret Protection" on Team or Enterprise Cloud, so redaction must happen before commit ([GitHub ToS §E](https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/github-terms/github-terms-of-service); [About secret scanning](https://docs.github.com/en/code-security/secret-scanning/introduction/about-secret-scanning)). Two documented incidents define the threat a report-to-PR pipeline inherits: a malicious public issue coerced an agent holding a broad GitHub token into leaking private repositories into a public PR ([Invariant Labs](https://invariantlabs.ai/blog/mcp-github-vulnerability)), and CamoLeak (CVSS 9.6, disclosed October 2025) used hidden Markdown comments in PR text to exfiltrate private source and keys ([Legit Security](https://www.legitsecurity.com/blog/camoleak-critical-github-copilot-vulnerability-leaks-private-source-code)).

Hugging Face's Terms of Service (September 15, 2022) are short: "You may not disclose your password to any third party, and you are solely responsible for any action taken with your Account", and two keyword passes found no clause on automation, tokens, rate limits or one account per person ([Hugging Face ToS](https://huggingface.co/terms-of-service)). Its Content Policy (April 10, 2025) prohibits "Using unauthorized bot APIs or remote management tools" and tunnels "to bypass restrictions" ([Content Policy](https://huggingface.co/content-guidelines)). The constraints are documentary: tokens are used "in place of a password"; "We recommend you create one access token per app or usage ... A local machine. A Colab notebook"; "We also recommend using only fine-grained tokens for production usage" ([User access tokens](https://huggingface.co/docs/hub/security-tokens)). Rate limits are per user in five-minute windows (a free user gets 1,000 API, 5,000 resolver and 200 page requests), and "make sure you always pass a `HF_TOKEN`" is HF's first instruction ([Hub rate limits](https://huggingface.co/docs/hub/rate-limits)). Gated datasets are personal: "Access requests are always granted to individual users rather than to entire organizations", "Requesting access can only be done from your browser", authors "can decide at any time to block your access ... without prior notice", and EU-by-IP gates are evaluated on the downloading IP, so a grid download can fail or breach a condition the student accepted elsewhere ([Gated datasets](https://huggingface.co/docs/hub/datasets-gated)). HF's own client writes the token to a plain file under `HF_HOME`, so Ashley's grid copy matches HF's storage model, and HF's advice cuts toward a dedicated fine-grained token for the grid so it can be revoked alone ([huggingface_hub quickstart](https://huggingface.co/docs/huggingface_hub/quick-start)).

The Visual Studio Marketplace is the third platform, and the earlier research verified its shape. The Publisher Agreement ("Effective Date: June 2021", served from a September 2025 build) makes the publisher "the distributor of your Offerings", "solely responsible and liable for your Offerings ... including all delivery and support", and says "You are solely responsible for ensuring that all fees and charges payable for use of your Offering are billed and collected outside of the Marketplace"; a "Bring-Your-Own-License (BYOL) Offering" is one "for which Customers have obtained, outside of the Marketplace, the right to access or use the Offering" ([Publisher Agreement](https://cdn.vsassets.io/v/M261_20250904.11/_content/Visual-Studio-Marketplace-Publisher-Agreement.pdf)). The verification pass added that `vsce` accepts only `Free` or `Trial` as pricing values, so a key-gated Ashley lists as Trial, and that BYOL offerings are excused from delivering their terms to Microsoft while the Microsoft Standard Agreement, whose link now dead-ends, is the default only for non-BYOL offerings without a EULA ([VS Code publishing docs](https://code.visualstudio.com/api/working-with-extensions/publishing-extension)). Microsoft is "not a party to and ... not bound by any Publisher Terms of Use" ([Marketplace Terms of Use](https://cdn.vsassets.io/v/M146_20190123.39/_content/Microsoft-Visual-Studio-Marketplace-Terms-of-Use.pdf)); it "may remove or suspend the availability of any Offering from the Marketplace for any reason or no reason and at any time"; a privacy policy is required "if (i) your Offering accesses, collects or transmits any Personal Data"; pre-sale disclosures "must" go "in the Offering description field"; the publisher warrants it has "obtained any and all consents, approvals, or licenses ... for your Offering to access any Internet-based or Microsoft-provided services", may not use the Marketplace "to permit others to carry out, any illegal activity or breach of contract", indemnifies Microsoft for claims "relating to the functionality of, the use of, or the inability to use the Offering", and litigates in King County, Washington ([Publisher Agreement](https://cdn.vsassets.io/v/M261_20250904.11/_content/Visual-Studio-Marketplace-Publisher-Agreement.pdf)). The Participation Policies require "commercially reasonable" support for paid offerings and warn that "The Marketplace is currently not able to prevent customers from creating multiple subscriptions to Free Trial offerings", leaving that to the publisher's terms ([Participation Policies](https://cdn.vsassets.io/v/M187_20210610.3/_content/Visual-Studio-Marketplace-Participation-Policies.pdf)). The §10(d) consents warranty puts the Anthropic question on the vendor the day the listing goes live, before any student clicks anything. Open VSX is the same structure under Belgian law, permits proprietary licenses, defaults an unlicensed extension to MIT, and says "You are solely responsible for implementing any technical or security features designed to prevent unauthorized access" ([Open VSX Publisher Agreement](https://www.eclipse.org/legal/documents/eclipse-openvsx-publisher-agreement.pdf)). No written obfuscation rule exists in either Microsoft document; Microsoft's June 2025 statement is that "Clean, and non-obfuscated code in extensions is significantly less likely to trigger a false-positive for malware", and the promised policy update could not be found ([Microsoft Developer Blog, June 2025](https://developer.microsoft.com/blog/security-and-trust-in-visual-studio-marketplace/)).

## A Michigan click-through binds the student, allocates risk, and nothing more

Formation is the first thing the EULA must get right, and the cases are consistent. Terms merely available below a download button did not bind free-software users in *Specht*; a conspicuous hyperlink on every page "without more" was insufficient in *Nguyen*; and the current benchmark, *Berman*, requires "reasonably conspicuous notice of the terms to which the consumer will be bound" plus an act that "unambiguously manifests his or her assent", offering the cure in one sentence: "By clicking the Continue >> button, you agree to the Terms & Conditions" ([Specht](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/779363/specht-v-netscape-communications-corporation/); [Nguyen](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2014/08/18/12-56628.pdf); [Berman](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2022/04/05/20-16900.pdf)). The Sixth Circuit applied Michigan contract law to a checkbox reading "By signing up, you agree to the Terms of Service and Privacy Policy" and to e-mailed notice of changed terms followed by continued log-ins, finding a contract both times and sending a minor's infancy defence to the arbitrator under a delegation clause ([In re StockX](https://www.opn.ca6.uscourts.gov/opinions.pdf/21a0276p-06.pdf)). Michigan's UETA removes any electronic-form objection and the UCC's definition of "conspicuous" supplies the design test ("A heading in capitals equal to or greater in size than the surrounding text, or in contrasting type, font, or color") ([MCL 450.837](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-450-837); [MCL 440.1201](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-440-1201)). Because VS Code extensions activate silently, "install equals accept" is the *Specht* fact pattern; the enforceable moment must be a first-run modal that blocks activation, states in body-size type that the click forms the agreement, links the full text in blue, and records version and timestamp. **Counsel** should decide whether to require an age representation, since some students are minors and a minor can generally disaffirm.

Warranty and liability terms are enforceable within stated limits. Michigan's UCC lets a seller exclude implied warranties with conspicuous language that "must mention merchantability" or use "as is" or "with all faults", lets consequential damages be "limited or excluded unless the limitation or exclusion is unconscionable", but revives general remedies where an exclusive remedy "fail[s] of its essential purpose" ([MCL 440.2316](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-440-2316); [MCL 440.2719](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-440-2719)). The Consumer Protection Act separately makes it an unfair practice to disclaim merchantability "unless a disclaimer is clearly and conspicuously disclosed" ([MCL 445.903](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-445-903)). Whether Article 2 governs a downloadable license at all turns on Michigan's "predominant factor" test, with no clean appellate answer, so the disclaimer should satisfy both regimes at once ([Neibarger](https://law.justia.com/cases/michigan/supreme-court/1992/88206-3.html)); **Counsel**. Michigan public policy will not enforce a release "against gross negligence or willful and wanton misconduct", and unconscionability needs both a procedural and a substantive prong, with terms not unconscionable merely because "foolish for one party and very advantageous to the other" ([Michigan Bar Journal, June 2018](https://www.michbar.org/file/barjournal/article/documents/pdf4article3398.pdf); [Clark v. DaimlerChrysler](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1875818/clark-v-daimlerchrysler-corp/)). Magnuson-Moss revives implied warranties if the supplier "makes any written warranty", so the EULA must promise nothing about performance on the Grid ([15 U.S.C. 2308](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/2308)). A cap of "the amount paid" is zero for a trial user; California courts have enforced such caps for free services, no Michigan authority was found, and a modest fixed floor reduces the argument that the remedy is illusory ([Lewis v. YouTube summary](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=b672795a-34f4-486f-b909-01eef249580b)).

The refund and support terms are where the Consumer Protection Act bites, and it has just grown. Students buying a study tool with their own money are very likely consumers under the Act's definition of "trade or commerce" ([MCL 445.902](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-445-902)). The Act reaches "Causing a probability of confusion or of misunderstanding as to the legal rights, obligations, or remedies of a party", "Failing to reveal a material fact, the omission of which tends to mislead or deceive the consumer", and consumer waivers "unless the waiver is clearly stated and the consumer has specifically consented to it", with recovery of "actual damages or $250.00, whichever is greater, together with reasonable attorney fees", class actions, and a six-year limitations period ([MCL 445.903](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-445-903); [MCL 445.911](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-445-911)). In *Attorney General v. Eli Lilly* the Michigan Supreme Court overruled *Smith v. Globe Life*, so no regulated-industry exemption shelters a software seller ([Michigan Courts case page](https://www.courts.michigan.gov/courts/supreme-court/case-information-20252026-term_1/november-2025-case-information/165961-attorney-general-v-eli-lilly-and-co/)). A no-refund term for external events is a risk allocation rather than a waiver of a statutory right, but Michigan reads force-majeure language by its words: in *Kyocera* the buyer got no relief for events outside the clause's enumerated terms, which means the university network, the student's own Anthropic account, Anthropic's terms changes, Microsoft removal, and AccessID loss must each be named ([Kyocera v. Hemlock](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/mi-court-of-appeals/1719993.html)). If the license renews, ROSCA independently requires clear pre-billing disclosure, "express informed consent", and "simple mechanisms for a consumer to stop recurring charges"; the FTC's click-to-cancel rule was vacated on July 8, 2025 and a fresh rulemaking opened in March 2026, while Michigan's own auto-renewal bill HB 4826 has sat in committee since August 2025 ([15 U.S.C. 8403](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/8403); [WilmerHale, Aug. 2025](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/client-alerts/20250801-eighth-circuit-vacates-the-ftcs-click-to-cancel-rule-but-federal-and-state-regulators-likely-to-remain-active); [HB 4826](https://legislature.mi.gov/Bills/Bill?ObjectName=2025-HB-4826)). A form clause restricting honest reviews is void under the Consumer Review Fairness Act ([FTC CRFA guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-review-fairness-act-what-businesses-need-know)).

Anti-circumvention is the clause most likely to over-promise. A contractual ban on reverse engineering and on defeating the check is enforceable as a contract term under *Bowers* and *Davidson*, and *MDY* makes breach of a license term copyright infringement only where "there must be a nexus between the license condition and the licensor's exclusive rights of copyright" ([Bowers](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/us-federal-circuit/1202776.html); [Davidson v. Jung](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/791820/davidson-associates-doing-business-as-blizzard-entertainment-inc/); [MDY v. Blizzard](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/205002/mdy-industries-llc-v-blizzard-entertainment/)). But the Sixth Circuit, which governs Michigan, held in *Lexmark* that an authentication routine does not "control access" to code that "Anyone ... may read ... directly from the printer memory, with or without the benefit of the authentication sequence", and that § 1201 "does not naturally extend to a technological measure that restricts one form of access but leaves another route wide open" ([Lexmark v. Static Control](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Lexmark_Int%27l_v._Static_Control_Components/Opinion_of_the_Court)). A VSIX is a zip of readable JavaScript, the grid daemon ships as plain text into a student-owned directory, and the earlier research found the daemon reads its trusted key list from a sibling file the student can swap; a key check there is the lock on the back door of a house whose front door is open. The realistic levers are the EULA covenant, termination, and server-side choke points the author controls (lab group membership, a hosted revocation list, signed updates), which is also Keygen's own position that "all applications are susceptible to cracking (given enough motivation)" ([Keygen security](https://keygen.sh/docs/api/security/)). The reverse-engineering clause should carve out § 1201(f) interoperability, the 2024 security-research exemption and "applicable law", as Anthropic's and Microsoft's own terms do ([17 U.S.C. 1201](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/1201); [37 CFR 201.40](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/37/201.40)). Two further facts thin the copyright itself: every Ashley commit carries a Claude co-author trailer, and the Copyright Office's January 2025 report holds that "Copyright does not extend to purely AI-generated material" while protecting human "selection, coordination, or arrangement" and "creative modifications", a position the D.C. Circuit's *Thaler* decision and the Supreme Court's March 2026 certiorari denial leave standing ([Copyright Office Part 2 report](https://www.copyright.gov/ai/Copyright-and-Artificial-Intelligence-Part-2-Copyrightability-Report.pdf); [Mayer Brown, March 2026](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/03/supreme-court-denies-review-in-ai-authorship-case)). Suit on a U.S. work also requires registration first ([17 U.S.C. 411](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/411)). The verification pass also reversed the earlier belief that no MIT copy had left the laptop: the 1.0.0 VSIX with full source maps and an MIT LICENSE was pushed to a private GitHub repository and sits in a mode-755 clone on the Grid inside a group-750 home readable by seven other `advcomp` accounts, so anyone who took a copy holds MIT rights to that version ([MIT License](https://opensource.org/license/mit)).

Third-party rights are the hard boundary of what a EULA can do. A license binds only those who assented to it; *Specht* refused to bind a non-party to Netscape's license, Microsoft is "not a party", and neither Anthropic, GitHub, Hugging Face nor WSU signs anything with the author. The EULA can require the student to comply with each of those parties' terms as amended, can take a representation that the student is the sole holder of each account and token the tool touches, can make the student indemnify the vendor for third-party claims caused by the student's own breach of those covenants, and can state that the vendor is independent of and not endorsed by any of them. Indemnities have been enforced when formed by clickwrap and not unconscionable and have failed when one-sided; Michigan construes them "strictly against the party who drafts them" and will not read them to cover the drafter's own negligence "unless such an intent is expressed clearly and unequivocally" ([Wilk Auslander summary](http://www.wilkauslander.com/?an=137988&anc=3234&format=xml&p=12136&t=40); [Sherman v. DeMaria](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1913578/sherman-v-demaria-building-co/)); **Counsel**, since no Michigan decision on a consumer software indemnity was found. What the EULA cannot do is shield the vendor from WSU's own claims, from Anthropic's enforcement against the vendor or the students, or from the vendor's own duties: Microsoft's "reasonable security measures", Michigan's breach-notification statute for "unencrypted and unredacted personal information", and FTC § 5 ([MCL 445.72](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-445-72); [15 U.S.C. 45](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/45)). A "solely responsible for your tokens" clause is a fair description only if the software does not itself read, cache or transmit them; if it does, the clause must say what the software does with them, or it reads as an attempt to disclaim the vendor's own security duty, which MCPA (1)(n) does not permit.

Data and forum round out the picture. An opt-out diagnostics programme is lawful under federal law when disclosed accurately and the opt-out works; the FTC's flashlight-app order turned on an undisclosed transmission and an option "rendering the option meaningless", and it required just-in-time disclosure and deletion ([FTC, Goldenshores, Dec. 2013](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2013/12/android-flashlight-app-developer-settles-ftc-charges-it-deceived-consumers)). CCPA thresholds ($26.6 million revenue or 100,000 consumers) are irrelevant at this scale, Michigan has no comprehensive privacy statute (SB 359 pending), and FERPA does not reach a vendor that is not "a party acting for the agency or institution", which changes if WSU adopts or contracts for the tool ([CPPA FAQ](https://cppa.ca.gov/faq.html); [SB 359 analysis](https://legislature.mi.gov/documents/2025-2026/billanalysis/Senate/htm/2025-SFA-0359-G.htm); [34 CFR 99.3](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/34/99.3)). Michigan will honour a Michigan choice of law and forum under Restatement § 187 and MCL 600.745 ([Chrysler v. Skyline](https://law.justia.com/cases/michigan/supreme-court/1995/97396-6.html); [MCL 600.745](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-600-745)). An arbitration clause with a class waiver is enforceable under *Concepcion* and *StockX*, but under the AAA consumer schedule the business pays a $375 filing fee, $1,400 case-management fee, $500 hearing fee and $300 per hour of arbitrator time, plus $600 to register the clause and $600 a year, and a few dozen coordinated student filings would be a mass arbitration ([AAA consumer fee schedule](https://www.adr.org/media/3uofn4lu/consumer_rules_and_mediation_procedures_feeschedule.pdf); [Goodwin, Dec. 2025](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/12/insights-technology-cldr-mass-arbitration-the-risk-lurking-in-consumer-agreements)). For a low-price student tool that is a cost centre; a small-claims carve-out, an informal-dispute step and a Michigan forum achieve most of the benefit. **Counsel** must weigh that against class exposure under MCL 445.911. Because the vendor is the sole defendant of record under the Marketplace terms and faces Washington and Belgian forums for platform disputes, forming an LLC and filing an assumed name before taking payment is the consistent recommendation ([MCL 445.1](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-445-1)).

## The five features: what fits as designed and the minimal change each needs

| Feature | Verdict on the texts | Minimal concession |
|---|---|---|
| (1) Per-person keys against WSU sign-in; AccessID-bound trials; paid later | The key, trial and sign-in mechanics fit every platform rule; the worker beneath them does not fit Anthropic's page, and ownership and naming are unsettled | Stop capturing, storing and copying Claude tokens; end multi-account rotation; one account per student; disclose to WSU OTC and name a real licensor before charging; drop "WSU" from name and id or obtain permission; written answer from Anthropic sales |
| (2) Support only while licensed; no refunds for external events | Lawful as a disclosed risk allocation; fails if events are generic, undisclosed pre-sale, or paired with a renewing charge | Enumerate the events by name in listing, checkout and EULA; one-time term licenses (or ROSCA-compliant cancellation); pause or credit for verified extended outages; conspicuous disclaimer and a liability floor |
| (3) Opt-out diagnostics feeding an AI maintenance pipeline on the author's accounts | Lawful and matches .NET/Homebrew practice for crash and error reports; the pipeline is the case Anthropic's and GitHub's documents are written for | Minimal schema with no AccessID, hostname, paths, prompts or environment values; honour VS Code's telemetry setting; per-event consent for anything richer; Console API key, GitHub App identity, write-access gating, no secrets, draft PRs, human merge, egress allowlist |
| (4) Phone access via Remote Control on the grid | Documented pattern (remote machine, `tmux`); requires the student's own full-scope login, which Ashley must not hold | Student runs `claude auth login` on the node and answers the consent prompt; separate environment from the worker token; student-driven re-login; inside a Slurm allocation with walltime; transcript-storage warning |
| (5) Professional private-repo baseline | Fully achievable on GitHub Pro or Team; attestations for private repos need Enterprise Cloud | Branch protection with no bypass, CODEOWNERS, required checks, SHA-pinned actions, release checks for maps and notices, SemVer plus changelog, immutable releases; state the single-maintainer review caveat |

### Feature 1: keys and trials are fine; the worker, the licensor and the name are not

Nothing in the Marketplace, GitHub, Hugging Face or WSU texts objects to a signed per-person key bound to an AccessID. The Marketplace expressly contemplates BYOL offerings billed outside, the Trial label is only a label, and the publisher must restrict multiple trials in its own terms ([Participation Policies](https://cdn.vsassets.io/v/M187_20210610.3/_content/Visual-Studio-Marketplace-Participation-Policies.pdf)). Established single-developer tools do the same thing: the shared Wallaby/Quokka EULA grants use "for evaluation purposes without charge for a period of thirty (30) days from the date of installation", states that the software "contains a feature that will automatically disable Software upon expiration ... Licensee may not disable, destroy, or remove this feature", forbids sharing keys, and reserves the right "to periodically detect use of license key(s)"; the verification pass found the earlier claim that Wallaby documents no trial was wrong, which makes it a better precedent, not a worse one ([Wallaby EULA](https://github.com/wallabyjs/public/blob/master/EULA.md)). Binding the license to institutional identity is closer to GitLens ("Your license is tied to your email address, not to a specific device") than to offline keys ([GitKraken FAQ](https://help.gitkraken.com/gk-dev/gk-dev-faq/)). Ed25519 verification works in the Electron extension host and in Node 22 and 18 (the verification pass corrected only the WebCrypto-experimental aside), and Keygen's advice to hard-code the public key applies with force because the grid daemon currently reads its trusted keys from a student-editable file ([Keygen offline licenses](https://keygen.sh/docs/choosing-a-licensing-model/offline-licenses/)). AccessID binding sits well with UP 00-1's account-sharing rule and the access-auth standard's SSO preference, and because AccessID and e-mail are not confidential under UP 07-2, the license file itself is not restricted data; it is still personal data under Microsoft's privacy-policy trigger. Two mechanics need testing rather than research: whether the WSU Entra tenant allows VS Code's first-party Microsoft sign-in without admin consent, and whether the returned account label yields the AccessID. Because AccessIDs are deprovisioned on the schedules above, trial and license validity should be re-checked against sign-in periodically rather than assumed for the key's life.

The concessions sit under the key, not in it. First, the token handling has to change: the extension must not run `claude setup-token` and capture its output, must not store or copy the token, and must not rotate accounts. The two lower-exposure variants the texts support are letting the student export the token into their own grid environment themselves and having Ashley merely launch `claude -p` where Claude Code already finds a credential, or letting the student run `claude auth login` on the node, which is "Anthropic's own flow" with credentials stored by Claude Code itself. Neither is declared safe by any text; both remove the vendor from the "collect, store" sentence, and the persistent-but-idle worker that acts only when a student asks is closer to "ordinary, individual usage" than an autonomous loop. An API-key edition, where each student provisions a Console key, is the fully written-down alternative. Second, before any paid key is issued, the ownership question must be put to WSU's Office for Technology Commercialization and the Tenet question to **Counsel**, and the licensor named in the EULA must be a legal person. Third, the product name and extension id must lose "WSU" and "Wayne State" or obtain Marketing and Communications approval; descriptive text such as "for the Wayne State University Grid" follows the style guide's "[Entity] at Wayne State University" pattern but is still descriptive use the policy does not expressly exempt ([Editorial style guide](https://universityrelations.wayne.edu/ucam/style-guide)). Fourth, commercial build and test work should move off the Grid, because that is the reading of UP 00-1 §3.3 that most plausibly reaches the author. Charging for the tool is not itself "pay[ing] for, resell[ing], or intermediat[ing] Claude usage"; the texts target Claude usage, not tooling. A free-trial phase can proceed while the ownership and Anthropic questions are being answered, since no money changes hands and the exposure is smaller, but the token redesign should precede even that, because Anthropic's enforcement has fallen on students' subscriptions, not on vendors' invoices.

### Feature 2: no refunds for named events is defensible; a generic clause on a renewing charge is not

The Marketplace side is straightforward. Support obligations attach only to paid offerings, must be "commercially reasonable", and the support channel in the listing must stay accurate "for as long as the relevant Offering is available in the Marketplace or is otherwise provided to Customers"; removing a paid offering needs 30 days' notice; refunds and chargebacks are the publisher's ([Publisher Agreement](https://cdn.vsassets.io/v/M261_20250904.11/_content/Visual-Studio-Marketplace-Publisher-Agreement.pdf)). Tying support to an active license is consistent with that as long as the listing says so. The consumer-law side requires four things. The external dependencies must be disclosed before purchase, in the description field and at checkout, because MCPA (1)(s) and (1)(cc) reach omissions of material facts and Microsoft § 6(b) requires pre-sale disclosures in the description. The events must be enumerated by name, since *Kyocera* gave no relief for events outside the words used: the university blocking network access or Claude, Anthropic changing its terms or enforcing them, Microsoft removing or disabling the extension, the student losing or sharing their AccessID or Claude account, and GitHub or Hugging Face suspending the student's account. The warranty disclaimer must be conspicuous and name merchantability, and the EULA must make no written promise that the tool works on the Grid. And the liability clause should carry a small fixed floor and state the carve-outs Michigan would impose anyway. Two design choices reduce the residual risk further. A one-time term license avoids ROSCA entirely; if a renewing subscription is wanted, the sign-up flow itself must meet ROSCA's three requirements and the vendor should stop charging during a known extended outage, because an "all sales final" term cannot substitute for a cancellation path and a non-functional product billed for a whole period is the fact pattern that "fails of its essential purpose". A pause or pro-rata credit for verified outages caused by named events costs less than defending that argument. One distinction **Counsel** should draw in the text: an outage caused by Anthropic acting against a pattern the vendor chose is not an "outside event" in the same sense as a university firewall change, and a court applying (1)(n) may not treat it the same way; the redesign in Feature 1 is what makes the clause honest.

### Feature 3: opt-out is acceptable for crash and error reports; the pipeline must copy the vendors' five guardrails

The disclosure standard is settled by practice. VS Code requires extensions to "respect the `isTelemetryEnabled` and `onDidChangeTelemetryEnabled` API", never to introduce "a custom telemetry collection solution that does not ask for user consent", never to "Collect Personally identifiable information (PII)", and permits a `telemetry.json` so `code --telemetry` lists the extension's events; its user docs concede that third-party extensions "may be collecting their own usage data and are not controlled by the `telemetry.telemetryLevel` setting", which is why stating conformance in the README earns trust ([VS Code extension telemetry guide](https://code.visualstudio.com/api/extension-guides/telemetry); [VS Code telemetry docs](https://code.visualstudio.com/docs/configure/telemetry)). The two clearest opt-out exemplars both name who collects, say it is aggregate, give a one-line opt-out and a URL, and promise nothing was sent during the run that showed the notice: .NET's first-run banner ("You can opt-out of telemetry by setting the DOTNET_CLI_TELEMETRY_OPTOUT environment variable to '1'") and Homebrew's installer ("No analytics data has been sent yet (nor will any be during this install run)") ([.NET SDK telemetry](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dotnet/core/tools/telemetry); [Homebrew Analytics](https://docs.brew.sh/Analytics)). Their schemas define the ceiling: .NET "doesn't collect personal data, such as usernames or email addresses", hashes the working directory and MAC address, and "doesn't collect the value of any environment variable"; Homebrew's payload "does not contain a user identifier or an IP-address field" and keeps events 365 days; Claude Code's own error reports "redact known patterns of secrets, file paths, email addresses, and other personal information before anything leaves your machine" and are switched off by `DISABLE_ERROR_REPORTING=1` ([Claude Code data usage](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/data-usage)). JetBrains marks the conservative end: "we ask for the user's consent for data collection, even when the information collected is anonymized", and data sharing is "disabled in all release versions" ([IntelliJ IDEA data sharing](https://www.jetbrains.com/help/idea/settings-usage-statistics.html)). The defensible Ashley design is therefore: opt-out only for product version, OS, architecture, the extension's own exception type and stack frames, timings and boolean environment flags; a random install id in place of AccessID or hostname; no paths, prompts, file contents, clipboard or environment values; default to off when VS Code telemetry is off; a first-run notice in the .NET form followed by a settings toggle; per-event consent in the Firefox style ("Include the address of the page I was on", "comments are publicly visible") for anything richer; and a privacy policy stating fields, destination, retention and purpose, because Microsoft § 8(b)(ii) and Open VSX § 6 require it and *Goldenshores* is the checklist of how it goes wrong. Grid-side reports leave campus over a network no Grid rule governs; keeping them to named endpoints and free of anything UP 07-2 calls confidential is the sensible reading of the Merit AUP.

The pipeline is the case the documents were written for, provided it runs on the author's accounts and the students' subscriptions are never touched. Anthropic's texts put a vendor's own automation under the Commercial Terms on a Console key; the GitHub Actions page steers organization use to an API key because a setup-token "is tied to the subscription of the person who ran `claude setup-token`", and the legal page's "ordinary, individual usage" sentence is the other reason not to run a maintenance bot on a Pro or Max token ([Claude Code GitHub Actions](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/github-actions)). The five guardrails common to Copilot, the Claude action and Cursor should all be reproduced: only write-access humans trigger; the agent's identity is a GitHub App with a repository-scoped short-lived token and no repository secrets; output lands on a prefixed branch as a draft PR, never the default branch; the agent cannot approve or merge; egress is allowlisted. Diagnostic reports are attacker-controlled input by definition, so the Actions script-injection list (`body`, `title`, `message` and the rest) must never be interpolated into shell, hidden HTML comments and zero-width characters must be stripped, and tokens must be redacted before commit because private-repo push protection is a paid feature ([Script injections](https://docs.github.com/en/actions/concepts/security/script-injections); [Secure use reference](https://docs.github.com/en/actions/reference/security/secure-use)). For attribution, the kernel's rule that "AI agents MUST NOT add Signed-off-by tags" and its `Assisted-by:` trailer, LLVM's "Contributors must read and review all LLM-generated code or text before they ask other project members to review it", and the Claude action's configurable `attribution.commit` and `attribution.pr` together give the safe set: bot author or co-author, an `Assisted-by` trailer naming tool and model, a PR line stating the extent of assistance, a session-log link, and a human approval ([AI Coding Assistants, kernel docs](https://docs.kernel.org/process/coding-assistants.html); [LLVM AI Tool Use Policy](https://llvm.org/docs/AIToolPolicy.html); [Claude Code settings reference](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/settings-reference)). The kernel's July 2026 experience, with over 1,200 tagged commits and a "significant stream" of untagged ones, shows disclosure is a norm and branch protection is the control ([LWN, 2026-07-21](https://lwn.net/Articles/1083123/)). The one thing to design away from is a student-side "diagnose and fix Ashley" run triggered by the tool on the student's subscription; that is the vendor's product routing requests "on behalf of their users" for the vendor's benefit, and an opt-in, student-initiated command is the only version that stays near "ordinary, individual usage".

### Feature 4: Remote Control on the grid is the documented pattern, if the student holds the login

Anthropic's page describes the deployment Ashley wants, down to the multiplexer, and its troubleshooting section's fix for every credential problem is "Run `claude auth login`", which is what the student must do on the node. The consequences are mechanical. The setup-token from the worker cannot establish Remote Control, so the node holds two credentials, and because `CLAUDE_CODE_OAUTH_TOKEN` outranks the `/login` credential wherever the variable is set, the worker and the Remote Control process need different environments. The login expires and the tool cannot renew it without handling the credential, so Ashley's job is a re-login prompt, not an automated refresh; the three-day warning is documented and the roughly 28-day life is a user report. The one-time `Enable Remote Control? (y/n)` is a terminal prompt the student, not the tool, should answer. Trusted Devices (beta, off by default) can be enabled by a Pro or Max user and would require enrolling the phone; setting `CLAUDE_CODE_DISABLE_NONESSENTIAL_TRAFFIC` or `DISABLE_GROWTHBOOK` disables the feature; `disableRemoteControl` turns it off for users who want that ([Remote Control](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/remote-control)). On the WSU side the process must live inside a Slurm allocation with an explicit `--time`, never on Warrior; on the requeue QoS it is preemptible and single-node, so the tool must tolerate being killed, and the docs give the recovery window: server mode "gives up after roughly 10 minutes" of outage and sessions can be brought back "for about four hours after the server stopped". An allocation that idles waiting for a phone is not prohibited in writing, but KB 20274's stated purpose for walltimes (owners' "timely access to their hardware") is the courtesy rule to design around, and the three maintenance windows will end any long session. The data point students should be told plainly is that the transcript, tool activity included, "is stored on Anthropic servers" while connected; that is a reason to keep IRB data and anything UP 07-2 calls confidential out of Remote Control sessions, and it is why "Organizations with compliance requirements such as Zero Data Retention can't enable Remote Control". Because the session "grants no one else access", the multi-account rotation in the worker cannot coexist with this feature in any case.

### Feature 5: the professional baseline is a GitHub plan tier and a release check away

For a private source repository the baseline is available on GitHub Pro (personal) or Team (organization), which list "Protected branches", "Code owners" and "Required pull request reviewers" for private repositories; the Free plans do not ([GitHub's plans](https://docs.github.com/en/get-started/learning-about-github/githubs-plans)). The `main` rule should require a pull request, at least one approving review with "Require review from Code Owners", required status checks with the branch "up to date with the base branch", conversation resolution, no force-push or deletion, signed commits where practical, and "Do not allow bypassing" so the rule binds the administrator too ([About protected branches](https://docs.github.com/en/repositories/configuring-branches-and-merges-in-your-repository/managing-protected-branches/about-protected-branches); [About code owners](https://docs.github.com/en/repositories/managing-your-repositorys-settings-and-features/customizing-your-repository/about-code-owners)). CI hygiene is the Actions secure-use list: `permissions: contents: read` at the top of every workflow, actions pinned to full-length commit SHAs ("currently the only way to use an action as an immutable release"), no checkout of untrusted code under `pull_request_target`, and Dependabot with its default read-only token and no Actions secrets ([Secure use reference](https://docs.github.com/en/actions/reference/security/secure-use); [Troubleshooting Dependabot](https://docs.github.com/en/code-security/dependabot/troubleshooting-dependabot/troubleshooting-dependabot-on-github-actions)). Releases should follow Semantic Versioning and Keep a Changelog, whose "Security" type is where a telemetry or agent behaviour change belongs; GitHub now shows SHA-256 digests for uploaded assets and, since October 28, 2025, offers immutable releases whose "assets and associated Git tag cannot be changed after publication" ([semver.org](https://semver.org/); [keepachangelog.com](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/); [Immutable releases](https://docs.github.com/en/code-security/concepts/supply-chain-security/immutable-releases)). Sigstore build attestations are the one item the plan tier withholds: private repositories need Enterprise Cloud, so the common split is a private source repository whose release workflow publishes to a public distribution repository, or a detached signature made in the release step ([Artifact attestations](https://docs.github.com/en/actions/concepts/security/artifact-attestations)).

Ashley-specific items come from the earlier code review, all verified. The release check must fail on any `.map` file or `sourceMappingURL` in the VSIX (the 1.0.0 package shipped 5.2 MB of source maps with full TypeScript, and `!dist/**` in `.vscodeignore` re-includes maps whatever the order), on a missing `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md` (esbuild strips every third-party notice and the current bundles contain zero "Copyright" strings, while twelve of the thirteen bundled packages require their notice), on any test signing key, and on any dependency outside a permissive allowlist; the VSIX should stop being committed to git, the grid clones should be `chmod 700`, and the manifest's `SEE LICENSE IN LICENSE.txt` must point at the EULA rather than the MIT file the READMEs still describe ([esbuild legal comments](https://esbuild.github.io/api/#legal-comments); [VS Code publishing docs](https://code.visualstudio.com/api/working-with-extensions/publishing-extension)). Direct VSIX installation "disable[s] auto update by default", so Ashley needs its own signed update channel through `workbench.extensions.installExtension` ([VS Code extension marketplace docs](https://github.com/microsoft/vscode-docs/blob/main/docs/configure/extensions/extension-marketplace.md)). A `CONTRIBUTING` file should state the AI-disclosure posture (the "disclose-and-own" camp, with `Assisted-by`), since the ban-camp projects' rationale is DCO uncertainty that a private repository with its own terms does not share. The honest caveat is structural: GitHub's norm that the person who dispatched the agent cannot be its sole approver cannot be met by a single maintainer; the mitigation is to separate the trigger identity (the bot) from the reviewer (the author), require green checks and a cooling period before merge, and say so in the repository's policy. Two loose ends belong on the same checklist: register the copyright before any enforcement is contemplated, and check whether closed-source software performing SSH and Ed25519 operations is mass-market encryption software under EAR 740.17(b)(1) requiring an annual self-classification report ([BIS mass-market encryption](https://www.bis.gov/learn-support/encryption-controls/mass-market)); **Counsel**.

## Conclusion

The research reframes the question. Ashley's legal position is not decided by how well its EULA is drafted but by one design fact, who touches the Claude credential, and by two ownership facts, who holds the copyright and whose infrastructure built it. Every platform's terms converge on the same architecture: the student signs in through each provider's own flow, the tool orchestrates official binaries and stores nothing it did not need to, automation on the vendor's behalf runs on the vendor's own paid accounts under commercial terms, and the private repository treats every inbound report as hostile. That architecture is also the one that makes the refund clause honest, the "solely responsible for your accounts" clause a description rather than a disclaimer, and the diagnostics programme something a regulator would recognise from .NET or Homebrew. The multi-account rotation and token capture are not edge cases to be papered over; they are the parts Anthropic has spent 2026 enforcing against, and they put students' subscriptions, not the vendor's revenue, in the line of fire.

The sequencing follows from that. Redesign the credential path and remove rotation first, because it is a prerequisite for even the free trial; then obtain the three written answers no research can supply, from Anthropic sales on the orchestration pattern, from WSU's technology office on ownership and Grid use, and from counsel on the Tenet clause and the licensor entity; then rename, form the entity, register the copyright, publish the privacy policy and ship 2.0.0 under a click-through that meets *Berman*. Paid keys come last, and they come as one-time term licenses with the external dependencies named in the listing. What remains open after all of that is real and should be stated to students as such: Anthropic's wording has changed twice this year and may change again, WSU's IT policy predates every service Ashley depends on, and no court has yet ruled on a subscription-orchestrating extension. A tool for researchers can say plainly what is not known; the agreement should too.
