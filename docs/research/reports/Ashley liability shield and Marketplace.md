# Records, not clauses, shield Ashley's author

The strongest lawful protection available to a sole Michigan author selling a per-person VS Code extension to students is not a clause but a stack, and the clause most people reach for first, binding arbitration, is the one piece of it that should be left out at launch. The stack is: a single-member Michigan LLC that is the named licensor on every key, listing, processor and insurance form ($50 to form, $25 a year); a technology errors-and-omissions plus cyber policy at $1M/$1M naming both the LLC and its member, because Michigan holds an owner personally liable for his own torts however the entity is organised; a first-run clickwrap whose checkbox names the button, whose text is versioned and hashed, and whose record is kept for six years, because online-contract cases are decided on screenshots; a 60-day notice-and-cure step that turns most complaints into refunds before any forum is reached; a merchant of record that becomes the seller, tax collector, refund desk and sanctions screen for five percent plus fifty cents; disclosures placed where each statute demands them, including a New Jersey paragraph, a California licence statement at checkout and a "Known Risks" section with its own checkbox; and a Marketplace package that is readable, secret-free, reachable at its repository URL and documented at the top of both the description and the README, since Microsoft may remove any extension "for any reason or no reason" and the practical appeal path is an e-mail alias that answers in boilerplate. The arbitration decision turns on arithmetic: under the AAA consumer schedule a single desk arbitration costs the business at least $3,275 in non-reallocable fees before any lawyer, plus $600 to register the clause and $600 a year, while the Michigan Consumer Protection Act caps a class action at Michigan residents' actual damages, which for a $30 licence and a few hundred students is a smaller number than one coordinated batch of demands. What no contract can do must be said first: no clause stops a filing at its inception, because arbitration, forum and cure clauses are all motions that must be briefed and won; the Michigan Consumer Protection Act, California's CLRA and non-disparagement statute, Texas's DTPA and New Jersey's TCCWNA each void or penalise waivers; regulators, card networks, Microsoft, minors and the author's own negligence survive any agreement. The design below accepts that, as the author has, and buys the best available position on each front without removing a feature. Places where a lawyer must decide are marked **Counsel**. Three topics rest on notes the researchers could not finish: state privacy-law thresholds, the federal ROSCA and "free" rules, and California's CIPA; where those arise the report says what was verified and what was not.

This report builds on the earlier "Ashley terms of service constraints" report, which fixed the operating boundary set by Anthropic's credential rule, Wayne State policy, GitHub, Hugging Face and the Marketplace publisher terms, and specified 23 clauses and 18 exposures. It is not repeated here; where a point below depends on it, the dependency is named. Findings are drawn from four note files, abbreviated in the checklists as CDS (contract and dispute shields, Q1–Q11), EIF (entity, insurance and filings, KQ1–KQ9), MRP (Marketplace removal prevention, KQ1–KQ7) and RME (regulatory and multi-state exposure, §1.1–§10.1).

## No clause stops a filing; four things decide what it costs

Every dispute-resolution device in a consumer contract is a defence, not a barrier. An arbitration clause is asserted by a motion to compel, and since May 2024 a court that grants it must stay rather than dismiss the case, keeping the file open ([Smith v. Spizzirri, LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/22-1218)). A forum clause is a transfer or dismissal motion; a notice-and-cure clause is a motion to dismiss or stay for an unmet condition precedent ([Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2025/01/Tiered-Dispute-Resolution-Clauses-Navigating-Pitfalls-in-the-US-and-UK)). Whether any of them applies is litigated on whether a contract was formed, and formation is decided on the exact screen the user saw ([Berman v. Freedom Financial, 9th Cir.](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2022/04/05/20-16900.pdf)); when a plaintiff swears he never clicked and the business's records lack the agreement text, the Sixth Circuit sends the question to trial ([Boykin v. Family Dollar, 6th Cir.](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca6/20-1153/20-1153-2021-07-01.html)). So the first honest statement is that a student can always file, and the vendor will always spend something answering.

The second is that consumer statutes are drafted to survive the contract. Michigan makes it an unfair practice to enter "a consumer transaction in which the consumer waives or purports to waive a right, benefit, or immunity provided by law, unless the waiver is clearly stated and the consumer has specifically consented to it" ([MCL 445.903(1)(t)](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-445-903)), and gives a Michigan student the greater of actual damages or $250 plus attorney fees, with six years to sue and no pre-suit notice ([MCL 445.911](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-445-911)). The earlier report established that no regulated-industry exemption shelters a software seller after *Attorney General v. Eli Lilly* ([Michigan Courts](https://www.courts.michigan.gov/courts/supreme-court/case-information-20252026-term_1/november-2025-case-information/165961-attorney-general-v-eli-lilly-and-co/)). California declares any waiver of its anti-non-disparagement section "contrary to public policy, and ... void and unenforceable" ([Cal. Civ. Code 1670.8](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=1670.8)); Texas allows a DTPA waiver only in a signed writing by a consumer who is represented by counsel and not in a disparate bargaining position ([Tex. Bus. & Com. Code ch. 17](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/BC/htm/BC.17.htm)); New Jersey penalises the mere presence of a term that violates a clearly established right ([Spade v. Select Comfort, N.J.](https://law.justia.com/cases/new-jersey/supreme-court/2018/a-57-16.html)); and the federal Consumer Review Fairness Act makes it "unlawful for a person to offer a form contract" containing a review-restricting term, whether or not it is ever enforced ([15 U.S.C. 45b](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/45b)). Federal law also removes any pre-dispute arbitration agreement from a sexual-harassment or sexual-assault dispute at the claimant's election ([Pub. L. 117-90](https://www.congress.gov/117/plaws/publ90/PLAW-117publ90.htm)).

The third is that the parties who can hurt the vendor most never sign. Microsoft "may remove or suspend the availability of any Offering from the Marketplace for any reason or no reason and at any time" and may disable an installed extension client-side ([Publisher Agreement §3(g)](https://cdn.vsassets.io/v/M261_20250904.11/_content/Visual-Studio-Marketplace-Publisher-Agreement.pdf)). The card networks give the cardholder a dispute right that the vendor's terms cannot remove; the terms are evidence in representment and nothing more ([Stripe dispute evidence](https://docs.stripe.com/disputes/best-practices)). The FTC, a state attorney general, the Bureau of Industry and Security and OFAC enforce against the vendor regardless of what a student agreed to; a March 2026 OFAC settlement of $1,110,661 against a brokerage whose geo-blocking silently failed is the current illustration ([Paul Hastings](https://www.paulhastings.com/insights/client-alerts/ofac-settlement-highlights-importance-of-testing-and-auditing-sanctions-compliance-controls)). Anthropic, Wayne State, Tenet, GitHub and Hugging Face are the third parties the earlier report mapped; nothing in this layer changes that.

The fourth is that Michigan law itself withholds some protections from any drafter. A release "insulates against ordinary negligence, but not gross negligence" ([Xu v. Gay](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1616125/xu-v-gay/)), a rule the Court of Appeals restated in January 2026 ([Swanson v. Bittersweet, summary](https://www.kallashenk.com/michigan-court-of-appeals-draws-a-clear-line-on-liability-waivers-and-gross-negligence)). A minor's contract "is voidable at the minor's discretion" and a parent cannot bind the child ([Woodman v. Kera](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/mi-supreme-court/1527931.html)). And the entity does not cover the author's own acts: "corporate officials may be held personally liable for their individual tortious acts done in the course of business, regardless of whether they were acting for their personal benefit or the corporation's benefit" ([Dep't of Agriculture v. Appletree Marketing](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/mi-supreme-court/1510187.html)). A student who alleges that the author negligently wrote code that erased her repository is suing the person who wrote it; the LLC channels the contract claim, and only insurance pays the tort.

What follows from these four facts is the design principle for everything below: make the claim cheap to end rather than trying to make it impossible to start. A cure window converts complaints into refunds inside 60 days; an assent record wins the formation motion on paper; the entity and the policy put the money somewhere other than the author's savings; disclosures remove the omission theories that every consumer statute runs on; and the payments and Marketplace choices keep the two counterparties who never sign, the card issuer and Microsoft, from ever needing to act.

## Skip binding arbitration at launch: one AAA claim costs $3,275 against a $30 licence

The federal case for arbitration is as strong as it has ever been. The FAA preempts state rules that condition enforcement on class availability ([Concepcion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/563/333/)), class waivers stand even where individual arbitration costs more than the claim is worth ([Italian Colors](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/570/228/)), a delegation clause is a separately enforceable agreement that must be attacked "specifically" ([Rent-A-Center](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/561/63/)), and there is no "wholly groundless" exception to it ([Henry Schein](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/586/17-1272/)). The Sixth Circuit adds that incorporating the AAA rules is itself "clear and unmistakable evidence that questions of arbitrability are for the arbitrator" ([Blanton v. Domino's](https://www.opn.ca6.uscourts.gov/opinions.pdf/20a0184p-06.pdf)), and, applying Michigan law, it compelled arbitration on a sign-up checkbox reading "By signing up, you agree to the Terms of Service and Privacy Policy," sent a minor's infancy defence to the arbitrator, and credited terms that had carried opt-out instructions since 2015 ([In re StockX, 6th Cir.](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/us-6th-circuit/2153818.html)). A Michigan federal court has sent MCPA claims to arbitration and rejected the argument that the statute forbids it ([West v. Legacy Motors, E.D. Mich.](https://us-arbitration.aoshearman.com/siteFiles/14542/2016.11.2%20West%20v.%20Legacy%20Motors,%202-16-CV-12101%20(E.D.%20Mich.%20Nov.%202,%202016)....pdf)). If the question were only enforceability, the answer would be to include it.

The question is cost, and the cost falls on the business. Under the AAA consumer schedule the consumer's filing fee is capped at $225; the business pays a $375 filing fee, a $1,400 case-management fee "prior to the arbitrator appointment process," and all arbitrator compensation, which is "$1,500 per case" for a documents-only decision or "$2,500 per day of hearing per arbitrator" plus a $500 hearing fee, and "Arbitrator compensation is not subject to reallocation by the arbitrator(s)" ([AAA Consumer Rules, fee schedule](https://localnewsmatters.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Consumer-Arbitration-Rules.pdf)). Registering the clause costs $600 and $600 a year, and an unregistered clause draws a $300 emergency review when a demand arrives; a business that fails to pay may find that "the AAA may decline to administer future consumer arbitrations with that business" ([same](https://localnewsmatters.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Consumer-Arbitration-Rules.pdf)). The arithmetic is **$3,275 for a desk case and $4,775 for a one-day hearing**, before counsel, on a licence priced in the tens of dollars. Ten coordinated demands are already a mass arbitration: the January 2024 schedule charges the business an **$8,125 initiation fee** per mass arbitration plus, per case, a $325 filing fee (cases 1–500), $450 for a merits-arbitrator appointment and a $600 final fee, with merits arbitrators at $300 an hour ([Fenwick](https://www.fenwick.com/insights/publications/aaa-issues-updates-to-consumer-mass-arbitration-rules-and-fee-schedule); [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2024/02/american-arbitration-association-adopts-new-mass-arbitration-rules-and-fee-schedules)). One hundred coordinated student claims would therefore cost roughly $145,000 in provider fees alone. Courts will not police that exposure for the vendor: the Second Circuit holds that "the payment of fees is merely a procedural issue that must be decided and enforced by an arbitration panel" ([Frazier v. X Corp., via Privacy World](https://www.privacyworld.blog/2025/12/2025-mass-arbitration-year-in-review/)), and the Ninth Circuit told a claimant whose 7,300 cases JAMS consolidated for a $1,750 fee that she "got what she agreed to" ([Jones v. Starz, 9th Cir.](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2025/02/28/24-1645.pdf)). The AAA administered 92 mass arbitrations with about 280,000 claims in 2024 ([Privacy World](https://www.privacyworld.blog/2025/12/2025-mass-arbitration-year-in-review/)).

The comparison that decides the matter is between that fee floor and the class ceiling the clause is meant to prevent. Under the MCPA a class action may be brought only "on behalf of persons residing or injured in this state for the actual damages" ([MCL 445.911](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-445-911)); the $250 minimum is an individual remedy. For a product whose entire customer base is a few hundred students paying tens of dollars, actual damages across the whole class are a four-figure number, and the attorney-fee award that makes such a case worth bringing requires the plaintiffs to win. A mass arbitration of the same students starts at five figures the day the demands are filed, whether or not any claim has merit. At Ashley's scale the arbitration clause inverts the protection: it caps nothing and creates the only mechanism by which the plaintiffs' bar could impose six-figure costs. That inversion reverses as the customer base grows, which is why the recommendation carries a threshold.

The protocols drafted to blunt mass filings are the weakest part of any clause. The Ninth Circuit held Ticketmaster's bellwether and batching rules unconscionable, refused to sever them, and took the whole agreement and class waiver down with them; certiorari was denied on October 6, 2025 ([Heckman v. Live Nation, 9th Cir.](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2024/10/28/23-55770.pdf); [SCOTUSblog](https://www.scotusblog.com/cases/case-files/live-nation-entertainment-inc-v-heckman/)). The defects it named were binding claimants to rulings in cases they could not join, a provider that could override arbitrator disqualifications, no discovery and a ten-page limit, and terms that could change "without notice" and apply "retroactively" ([Sidley](https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/newsupdates/2024/10/ninth-circuits-live-nation-decision-evaluates-enforceability-of-mass-arbitration-protocols)). District courts in 2025 split: bellwether clauses survived where the claimant could opt out once a mass filing was declared ([Burkhardt, via Privacy World](https://www.privacyworld.blog/2025/12/2025-mass-arbitration-year-in-review/)), and H&R Block's protocol was struck as a "systemic failure" for providing "no mechanism for relief when unreasonable delays occur" ([Rios v. HRB Digital, via Privacy World](https://www.privacyworld.blog/2025/12/2025-mass-arbitration-year-in-review/)). A bespoke protocol adds unconscionability risk to a clause that already costs more than it saves.

**Recommendation.** Do not include binding arbitration in the launch agreement. Use a Michigan forum, a small-claims carve-out for either side, a stand-alone class waiver drafted to be severable, a separately headed jury waiver, a 60-day notice-and-cure condition precedent, and a one-year period for contract and warranty claims, each described below. Revisit arbitration when two conditions both hold: the LLC can fund at least one AAA proceeding from reserves without touching the author's savings, and the number of paying customers is large enough that a plausible class recovery under MCL 445.911 exceeds the mass-arbitration fee floor for a coordinated filing of, say, a fifth of them. If **Counsel** disagrees and wants arbitration from the start, the form that the cases support is the hybrid in the notes: AAA Consumer Rules incorporated by name, a separately headed delegation paragraph giving the arbitrator "exclusive authority to resolve any dispute relating to the interpretation, applicability, enforceability, formation, scope or validity" of the arbitration agreement, a 30-day opt-out by e-mail (which defeats procedural unconscionability even when "the method is burdensome," [Mohamed v. Uber](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/us-9th-circuit/1761734.html)), a small-claims election for either party (which AAA Rule R-9 already grants, [NCLC](https://library.nclc.org/book/consumer-arbitration-agreements/r-9-small-claims-option-parties)), a carve-out for public injunctive relief under California law (the *McGill* rule is cited in the notes from general knowledge rather than a fetched opinion; the carve-out costs nothing), a statement that the agreement yields where federal law forbids it, no bellwether protocol, reliance on the provider's own consolidation rules (AAA's May 2025 amendments give it discretion to consolidate claims "arising out of same contract as single case," [Privacy World](https://www.privacyworld.blog/2025/12/2025-mass-arbitration-year-in-review/); JAMS charges a flat $7,500 for 75 or more coordinated demands with consumers' aggregate exposure capped at $2,500, [Duane Morris](https://www.duanemorris.com/alerts/2024_sees_newly_enacted_aaa_jams_arbitration_rules_0724.html)), and the class waiver stated to survive if arbitration is opted out of or invalidated. Michigan's arbitration act forbids pre-dispute waiver of the right to a lawyer, to notice of initiation and to arbitrator disclosure ([MCL 691.1684](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-691-1684)), so the clause must not touch those. And no other document, including the checkout page, the refund policy and a merchant of record's buyer terms, may contain a conflicting forum sentence, because where two contracts conflict "a court, not an arbitrator, must decide which contract governs" ([Coinbase v. Suski, SCOTUS](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/23pdf/23-3_879d.pdf)).

### A stand-alone class waiver has no Michigan authority, so draft it to lose gracefully

Without arbitration the class waiver stands on its own, and the honest position is that its enforceability in Michigan is unknown. No Michigan decision either way was found. New Jersey's Supreme Court held in 2024 that stand-alone consumer class waivers "are not per se contrary to public policy" ([Consumer Finance Monitor](https://www.consumerfinancemonitor.com/2024/12/31/new-jersey-supreme-court-enforces-stand-alone-class-action-waiver/)), Arkansas upheld one and Rhode Island refused one because its consumer statute authorises representative actions ([Florida Bar Journal](https://www.floridabar.org/the-florida-bar-journal/are-arbitration-agreements-necessary-for-class-action-waivers-to-be-enforceable/)). The Sixth Circuit voided a collective-action waiver in a separation agreement with no arbitration clause, reasoning that employees may waive collective procedures only "so long as they retain the right to individually arbitrate" ([Killion v. KeHE, 6th Cir.](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/us-6th-circuit/1674682.html)); that case is FLSA-specific, but the MCPA also authorises class actions, and a court could treat the waiver as a "right ... provided by law" that MCL 445.903(1)(t) requires to be clearly stated and specifically consented to. The drafting response is to give the waiver its own heading, its own plain sentence ("You and Ashley Software LLC each agree to bring claims only in an individual capacity and not as a plaintiff or class member in any class or representative proceeding"), its own logged checkbox, an express statement that the student's individual statutory remedies remain available, and a severability sentence that lets it fall without taking the forum, jury and limitation terms with it. Treat it as a term that might work, not one the business relies on.

### Notice-and-cure is the cheapest clause and the one that does the most work

A mandatory, clearly drafted pre-suit step is enforced as a condition precedent; courts have dismissed or stayed claims filed without the required negotiation, and the drafting rules are express condition-precedent language, "defined, objectively identifiable trigger points," realistic timelines, named recipients and no contradictory steps ([Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2025/01/Tiered-Dispute-Resolution-Clauses-Navigating-Pitfalls-in-the-US-and-UK)). The current large-platform model is Amazon's: a Notice of Dispute "personally signed by you, completed accurately and in full, accompanied by documents reasonably necessary to support your claim," a 60-day negotiating period as a prerequisite to any filing, and a statement that "a court can enjoin the initiation or proceeding of an arbitration in breach of these pre-arbitration dispute resolution requirements" ([Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/gp/help/customer/display.html?nodeId=T3eg9AqRtYxwA0PxpP)); NAM-administered clauses add that limitation periods and fee deadlines are tolled during the informal step ([NAM sample clause](https://www.namadr.com/content/uploads/2024/03/Lose-It-Arbitration-Agmt-2024.pdf)). For Ashley the clause should require a written notice to a named e-mail and postal address containing the student's name, account e-mail, install id, the problem and the specific relief sought; a 60-day good-faith period with one video or telephone conference on request; completion as a condition precedent to any suit other than small claims; tolling; a mirror obligation on the vendor; and a sentence that either party may ask a court to decide whether the step was satisfied, since otherwise that question goes to an arbitrator where one exists ([Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2025/01/Tiered-Dispute-Resolution-Clauses-Navigating-Pitfalls-in-the-US-and-UK)). Its practical value is that a licence-key failure, a university block or an upstream change can be cured with a fix or a refund inside the window. The same window should be run against the statutory clocks in the states that give the vendor one: a California CLRA letter must be answered with "an appropriate correction, repair, replacement, or other remedy" within 30 days to defeat the damages claim ([Cal. Civ. Code 1782](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=1782)), a Texas DTPA notice opens a 60-day window in which a written tender caps damages and fees if the consumer rejects it and later recovers no more ([Tex. Bus. & Com. Code 17.5052](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._bus._and_com._code_section_17.5052)), and a Massachusetts 93A demand answered with "a written tender of settlement" within 30 days limits recovery to the tender if reasonable ([G.L. c. 93A §9](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXV/Chapter93A/Section9)). The internal rule is therefore one sentence: every legal letter gets a written offer of a full refund, and where the complaint is about a term, an undertaking to rewrite it, inside 30 days.

### Jury waiver, shortened period, fees, forum and law

Michigan enforces clear contractual jury waivers as written; the Supreme Court found that "fixed by the court" waived a jury without the words "jury" or "waive" ([Barton-Spencer, via Warner Norcross](https://www.wnj.com/updates/msc-contracting-parties-waive-their-right-to-jury-trial-where-contract-requires-an-issue-to-be-fixed-by-the-court/)), and the Court of Appeals held that courts "are not permitted to override a contractual jury trial waiver" ([Egan v. Prop Services, via Demorest](https://detroitbusinesslaw.com/2018/09/04/the-michigan-court-of-appeals-holds-that-courts-cannot-override-a-contractual-jury-trial-waiver/)). In federal court the Sixth Circuit puts the burden on the party resisting an express waiver to show it was not knowing and voluntary ([Chesterfield Exchange, E.D. Mich.](https://www.mied.uscourts.gov/PDFFIles/07-12254%20Chestefield%20Opinion%20Striking%20Jury%20Demand.pdf)), and the factors are negotiation, conspicuousness, bargaining power and sophistication ([Michigan Bar Journal](https://www.michbar.org/file/barjournal/article/documents/pdf4article692.pdf)). Three of the four cut against any consumer clickwrap, so the waiver rests on conspicuousness and the record: a numbered section headed "WAIVER OF JURY TRIAL," one or two mutual sentences, in capitals or bold, covered by the dispute-section checkbox, stated to survive if any arbitration term is opted out of or struck.

A shortened limitation period is enforceable under *Rory* "as written unless the provision would violate law or public policy," with adhesion irrelevant ([Rory v. Continental](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/mi-supreme-court/1266749.html)), and a one-year period in a supplier's invoice was enforced against a UCC warranty claim ([Liparoto v. General Shale](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/mi-court-of-appeals/1207327.html)). But on July 31, 2025 the Michigan Supreme Court held that "an adhesive boilerplate employment agreement that shortens a limitations period must be examined for reasonableness" under the three-part *Camelot* test and is subject to unconscionability, overruled *Clark*, and confined *Rory* partly on the ground that insurance policies pass through a commissioner's review, which employment contracts do not ([Rayford v. American House, Mich.](https://www.michbar.org/Portals/0/opinions/supreme/2025/073125/84115.pdf)). The majority said its decision "is focused on boilerplate employment agreements"; the dissent said it is "unclear ... what 'close judicial scrutiny' ... might mean in other contexts" ([same](https://www.michbar.org/Portals/0/opinions/supreme/2025/073125/84115.pdf)). Every reason the majority gave applies as strongly to a $30 student licence, so expect the argument. The design that passes *Camelot* comfortably is one year, running from accrual, tolled during notice-and-cure, applied to contract and warranty claims only, with statutory consumer-protection claims expressly excluded; no Michigan case on shortening the MCPA's six-year period was found, and applying the year to statutory claims invites the (1)(t) argument for little gain. **Counsel** decides whether to attempt the broader form.

Michigan enforces contractual attorney-fee clauses subject to reasonableness ([Michigan Judicial Institute benchbook](https://www.courts.michigan.gov/490820/siteassets/publications/benchbooks/civil/civilresponsivehtml5.zip/Civil/Ch_8_Posttrial_Proceedings/Attorney_Fees.htm); [Plunkett Cooney](https://www.plunkettcooney.com/dontbetthebusinessblog/paying-attorney-fees)), but the MCPA gives a prevailing consumer fees by statute and a one-way vendor clause is the kind of term Michigan's two-prong unconscionability test targets ([Clark v. DaimlerChrysler](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/mi-court-of-appeals/1205678.html)). The defensible form is mutual, limited to reasonable fees, inapplicable in small claims and expressly not displacing statutory awards; its function is deterrence of nuisance suits, since fees against a student are rarely collectible.

Michigan applies Restatement §187 to choice-of-law clauses ([Chrysler v. Skyline](https://law.justia.com/cases/michigan/supreme-court/1995/97396-6.html)) and will honour Michigan law for formation, licence scope, disclaimers and caps; it will not displace a home-state consumer statute that carries an anti-waiver rule, and the en banc Ninth Circuit's 2025 holding that an e-commerce platform "expressly aimed" conduct at California by handling a California buyer's data means the vendor should expect to be sued where the student lives ([Briskin v. Shopify, via Proskauer](https://www.proskauer.com/blog/ninth-circuit-reshapes-personal-jurisdiction-standards-for-e-commerce-platforms-in-briskin-v-shopify)). Draft Michigan law and a Wayne County or Eastern District of Michigan forum, with small claims permitted in the student's own county, and write the terms to comply on their face with the California, New Jersey and Texas rules below rather than relying on the clause to escape them. **Counsel** decides between an exclusive and a non-exclusive forum. New York's ban on mandatory consumer arbitration is preempted by the FAA where interstate commerce exists ([Schiffer v. Slomin's](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/6315750/schiffer-v-slomins-inc/)), which matters only if arbitration is later adopted.

### Integration, release and the liability cap

An integration clause is "conclusive" against claims built on prior oral promises, and parol evidence is admissible only where the fraud "would invalidate the merger clause itself or ... the entire contract" ([UAW-GM v. KSL](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/mi-court-of-appeals/1178264.html)); reliance on an oral statement contrary to an integrated writing is unreasonable as a matter of law ([Hamade v. Sunoco](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/mi-court-of-appeals/1238080.html)). What it cannot do is bar fraud that voids the contract ([Custom Data Solutions](https://law.justia.com/cases/michigan/court-of-appeals-published/2006/20061219-c270752-43-28o-270752-opn-coa.html)) or MCPA misrepresentation and omission counts, which look at the listing and the README rather than the contract ([MCL 445.903(1)(bb), (cc), (s)](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-445-903)). So the agreement should contain an express integration sentence naming README text, listing copy, demonstrations and forum posts as superseded, a first-person non-reliance sentence, a no-oral-modification sentence tied to the in-product acceptance flow, and a list of what is not promised (model quality, uptime, university acceptance, grades), and the listing and README should be written to the same standard as the contract because that is where a (1)(bb) claim would come from.

Michigan abolished assumption of risk outside employment and "an express contractual assumption of risk" ([Felgner v. Anderson](https://law.justia.com/cases/michigan/supreme-court/1965/375-mich-23-2.html)), so the "Known Risks You Accept" section works as an express release and a disclosure. It should list each dependency by name with its consequence and remedy, carry its own checkbox, and say that nothing releases gross negligence, wilful misconduct or fraud or limits rights that cannot be limited by law; over-breadth is what invites a court to strike a whole section rather than sever ([Heckman, via Sidley](https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/newsupdates/2024/10/ninth-circuits-live-nation-decision-evaluates-enforceability-of-mass-arbitration-protocols)). The earlier report's *Kyocera* point governs the list: events not named are not covered. One honesty condition carries over from that report: an outage caused by Anthropic acting against a credential pattern the vendor chose is not an external event in the same sense as a university firewall change, and the clause is only as honest as the design behind it.

The liability section must avoid one Michigan trap. Article 2 lets the parties limit remedies to "return of the goods and repayment of the price," lets consequential damages be excluded "unless the limitation or exclusion is unconscionable," and revives all remedies "where circumstances cause an exclusive or limited remedy to fail of its essential purpose" ([MCL 440.2719](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-440-2719)). "Repair or replace" fails of its purpose the day Microsoft de-lists the extension or Anthropic changes its terms, because the vendor can no longer perform it. Make the exclusive remedy one the vendor can always deliver, a pro-rated refund of the current term's fee, with repair at the vendor's election; state that the consequential-damages exclusion and the cap "are independent of, and survive the failure of, any exclusive remedy"; cap aggregate liability at the greater of fees paid in the prior twelve months or a fixed floor of about $100 so the free tier's remedy is not illusory; and carve out liability that cannot be limited by law. Whether Article 2 applies to a downloaded licence at all is undecided in Michigan ([Butzel Long](https://www.butzel.com/alert-are-you-supplying-goods-or-services-the-latest-from-the-michigan-court-of-appeals)), so the disclaimer must satisfy both the UCC's conspicuousness rules and common law. Give no written warranty: Magnuson-Moss forbids implied-warranty disclaimers during the term of any written warranty and treats software distributed to individuals over the Internet as within its rules ([FTC Businessperson's Guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/businesspersons-guide-federal-warranty-law)), and a "money-back guarantee" phrased as a warranty can trigger it; state the refund as a policy and a remedy, disclaim merchantability and fitness under a capitalised heading, and satisfy the MCPA's own disclosure rule for implied-warranty disclaimers, whose text the notes flag for quotation before drafting ([MCL 445.903](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-445-903)).

## A checkbox that names the button, a hash of the text, and a page the student can print

The test is uniform across the circuits that have addressed it: "reasonably conspicuous notice of the terms to which the consumer will be bound" and an action "that unambiguously manifests his or her assent" ([Berman, 9th Cir.](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2022/04/05/20-16900.pdf)). Clickwrap is "routinely" enforced, scrollwrap "usually," browsewrap "consistently" refused, and sign-in wrap fails on details: a link "in a small gray font against a white background," a "By signing up you agree" sentence next to a button that says "continue," an "I agree" line without an if-then structure ([Chabolla v. ClassPass, via Eric Goldman](https://blog.ericgoldman.org/archives/2025/03/the-ninth-circuit-has-a-lot-to-say-about-online-contract-formation-much-of-it-confusing-chabolla-v-classpass.htm)). What passes is an "I agree to the payment terms" control beside a "Confirm and continue" button with the terms bolded, underlined and linked ([Edmundson v. Klarna, via Proskauer](https://www.proskauer.com/blog/thats-a-wrap-second-circuit-upholds-click-wrap-mandatory-arbitration-provision)) or "By tapping 'Play,' I agree to the Terms of Service" ([Keebaugh v. Warner Bros.](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca9/22-55982/22-55982-2024-04-26.html)). Massachusetts applies the same two-part standard and found Uber's registration flow wanting ([Kauders v. Uber](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/ma-supreme-judicial-court/2105839.html)), and a $5 trial that converted to a membership lost because the notices on the "Start my trial" screens were not conspicuous enough to flag an arbitration clause ([Sellers v. JustAnswer](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/2021/d077868.html)). A VS Code extension activates silently, so "install equals accept" is the *Specht* fact pattern the earlier report ruled out ([Specht v. Netscape](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/779363/specht-v-netscape-communications-corporation/)); the Marketplace listing and README are browsewrap and prove nothing.

The flow that follows from those cases has two steps and three checkboxes. Step one renders the agreement in a scrollable pane and enables the controls only after the student has scrolled to the end or opened the full text, converting sign-in-wrap risk into scrollwrap. Step two shows, immediately above the button, an unchecked box labelled "By checking this box and clicking **Accept and Continue**, I agree to the Ashley License Agreement (v[X], [date]), including the Dispute Resolution, Class Action Waiver and Jury Waiver in Section [N]," with the button labelled exactly "Accept and Continue," the link in a contrasting colour and underlined, and no marketing copy on the screen. A second unchecked box reads "I have read the Known Risks in Section [M] and accept them," which is what "clearly stated" and "specifically consented" under MCL 445.903(1)(t) look like in a user interface, and what a pre-*Concepcion* Michigan court penalised the absence of when it struck a clause that "makes no disclosures concerning the limitations on remedies and fails to state that the consumer waives either remedy or forum" ([Lozada v. Dale Baker Oldsmobile](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/FSupp2/91/1087/2510882/)). A third control is the age affirmation. Michigan removes the nonage defence in actions to recover for goods only where the minor's representation was "made in writing in a separate instrument containing only the statement of age, date of signing and the signature" ([MCL 600.1403](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-600-1403)); a checkbox inside the dialog is not that instrument, and the statute is framed around recovering goods rather than a licence dispute, so its help is limited. The cheap version is an un-prechecked "I am 18 or older" control logged with the acceptance; the strong version, worth adopting at the paid purchase, is a separate screen containing only the age statement, the date and a typed name. A typed name is not legally required, since a click adopted with intent is a signature under UETA, but it is the direct evidence of identity that defeats a *Boykin*-style sworn denial more cleanly than a click log; adopt it for the paid licence and for any re-acceptance of changed dispute terms.

The record is where cases are won. StockX proved assent with an executive's affidavit that notification e-mails were sent and that the plaintiffs "have all continued to login to and use their StockX accounts" ([In re StockX](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/us-6th-circuit/2153818.html)); Family Dollar lost because its records showed a click "at 10:00:58 a.m. on July 15, 2013" but "did not include any arbitration agreement" ([Boykin](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca6/20-1153/20-1153-2021-07-01.html)). Store, for every acceptance, an immutable row with the terms version id and the SHA-256 of the exact text shown, the template id of the rendered screen, the UTC timestamp, the checkbox and button events, the account e-mail or an opaque customer id, install and machine ids, IP, extension and VS Code versions, the age-affirmation state, the acceptance surface (native notification or webview) and any typed name; keep the archived text and a screenshot of the rendered flow in light, dark and high-contrast themes for every version; and keep a custodian declaration template. Michigan's UETA gives electronic records and signatures full effect ([MCL 450.837](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/documents/mcl/pdf/mcl-Act-305-of-2000.pdf)), lets attribution be shown "in any manner, including a showing of the efficacy of any security procedure" ([MCL 450.839](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-450-839)), and treats a retention duty as met by a record that "accurately reflects the information" and "remains accessible for later reference" ([MCL 450.842](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-450-842)); E-SIGN's retention standard is the same ([15 U.S.C. 7001(d)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/7001)). Retain acceptance rows for six years after the last transaction, Michigan's period for contract claims ([MCL 600.5807(9)](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-600-5807)), which also covers the three-year tort and four-year UCC windows; keep the per-version artefacts indefinitely; keep the logs pseudonymous with the mapping in one encrypted place under the LLC's own accounts, so that the record proves formation without holding more personal data than the licence needs.

One Michigan rule decides how the text is delivered. "If a sender inhibits the ability of a recipient to store or print an electronic record, the electronic record is not enforceable against the recipient" ([MCL 450.838(3)](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-450-838)). A webview that disables text selection, offers no save path, or links to a URL that later returns 404 hands a Michigan student that argument. Every version of the agreement must live at a stable, versioned URL and as a plain text or Markdown file inside the bundle that opens in an ordinary editor tab; the dialog needs "View license" and "Save a copy"; nothing in the CSS may block selection. E-SIGN's consumer-consent regime is triggered only where a law requires information "be provided or made available to a consumer in writing" ([15 U.S.C. 7001(c)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/7001)), which a one-time licence with no auto-renewal does not do today; California's retention-capable acknowledgment attaches only to automatic-renewal or continuous-service offers ([Cal. B&P 17602](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-bpc/division-7/part-3/chapter-1/article-9/section-17602/)). The elements cost nothing to fold in, so include a sentence on the right to a paper copy, the right to withdraw consent to electronic records, the hardware needed, and a step in which the student opens the licence before "Accept" enables, which is the "reasonable demonstration" the statute describes.

Modifications are handled the way *Heckman* and *Jackson* require. Terms changeable "without notice" and applied "retroactively," effective on posting with continued use as acceptance, were procedurally unconscionable ([Heckman, via Sidley](https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/newsupdates/2024/10/ninth-circuits-live-nation-decision-evaluates-enforceability-of-mass-arbitration-protocols)); the business bears the burden of proving notice of new terms, and "generalized notice that new terms might be coming are not enough" ([Jackson v. Amazon, 9th Cir.](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2023/04/19/21-56107.pdf)). Version the terms; on a material change, block use until the same two-step flow is completed; offer a plain decline with a pro-rata refund of the term; never apply changed dispute terms to disputes already noticed; and keep the prior acceptance row intact. The ALI's Restatement of Consumer Contracts states the same rule, that a modification "is enforceable only if it is proposed in good faith" with notice and a chance to reject ([ALI](https://www.ali.org/news/articles/restatement-law-consumer-contracts-available)).

The accessibility floor and the assent design point at the same implementation. VS Code's own guidance says "Only use webviews when absolutely necessary," not for wizards or promotions, and to honour contrast, ARIA labels and keyboard navigation ([VS Code UX guidelines](https://code.visualstudio.com/api/ux-guidelines/webviews)); the lowest-exposure dialog is native: a modal message with "Accept," "View License" and "Decline," a Quick Pick for the age affirmation, and a command that opens the licence in a read-only editor tab, which is keyboard-, contrast- and screen-reader-accessible and, for MCL 450.838(3), copyable and printable. If a webview is kept for branding it must meet WCAG 2.1/2.2 AA on the criteria a dialog can fail: keyboard operability with no trap, focus order and visible focus, 4.5:1 contrast using VS Code theme tokens, real `<button>` and `<input type=checkbox>` elements with labels, and text error messages, tested in high-contrast mode and against the `vscode-using-screen-reader` body class ([VS Code Webview API guide](https://code.visualstudio.com/api/extension-guides/webview)).

The vendor will sell to students wherever they live, and each brings a statute that the Michigan clause does not switch off. The table condenses the seven researched; every one gives a private right of action, three give the vendor a pre-suit window, and three carry anti-waiver rules.

New Jersey's statute is the drafting trap because it punishes the text itself. A consumer contract may not "state that any of [its] provisions is or may be void, unenforceable or inapplicable in some jurisdictions without specifying which provisions are or are not void, unenforceable or inapplicable within the State of New Jersey," and plaintiffs have attacked "unless prohibited by law," "to the extent allowed by law" and "except as required by law" as triggers ([Faegre Drinker](https://www.faegredrinker.com/en/insights/publications/2016/4/the-landmines-lurking-in-your-website)). Since 2018 only an "aggrieved consumer" who "suffers ... monetary or other harm" may recover ([Spade](https://law.justia.com/cases/new-jersey/supreme-court/2018/a-57-16.html)), which ended no-injury class actions but leaves exposure to any New Jersey student who was in fact refused a refund. The fix is a paragraph headed "New Jersey residents" stating in plain words which of the warranty disclaimer, limitation of liability and remedy limits do not apply to them, so the contract itself answers the question the statute asks; **Counsel** settles the list.

California needs three sentences in three places. A non-disparagement or review-restriction clause must not appear anywhere: it is void and penalised under Civil Code 1670.8, which "applies to any companies or persons doing business in California" ([Fenwick](https://www.fenwick.com/insights/publications/litigation-alert-california-bans-non-disparagement-clauses-in-consumer-contracts)), and the federal CRFA voids the same term, any fee or penalty for a review, and any assignment of review IP beyond a non-exclusive licence, in every state ([15 U.S.C. 45b](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/45b)). Before taking payment an Internet vendor must disclose "the vendor's return and refund policy, the legal name under which the business is conducted" and "the complete street address from which the business is actually conducted," on the first screen, the order screen or "in a manner that gives the user a reasonable opportunity to review"; the section's definition of goods is "tangible chattels," so its reach to a licence is unconfirmed, but the disclosure is free and a home-based vendor should ask **Counsel** whether a registered-agent address satisfies "actually conducted" ([B&P 17538](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&sectionNum=17538)). And since January 1, 2025 it is unlawful to advertise a "digital application" with "buy," "purchase" or any term "a reasonable person would understand to confer an unrestricted ownership interest" unless the seller either obtains an affirmative acknowledgment at each transaction that the buyer "is receiving a license to access the digital good" with a list of restrictions, or gives before each transaction "a clear and conspicuous statement" that buying is a licence with access to the terms; free goods and subscriptions are exempt ([B&P 17500.6](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&sectionNum=17500.6)). The $0 trial is exempt; the paid checkout is not. Label the button "Buy a licence" and place next to the price and again on the checkout page: "You are buying a licence to use Ashley, not the software itself; it is non-transferable and may be terminated for breach of the Licence Terms (link)." The permanent-offline-download exemption is not available to an extension that checks a licence online.

The Michigan "free" rule governs the trial label on every surface. It is unlawful to represent that a consumer will receive goods or services "free or without charge, or using words of similar import ... without clearly and conspicuously disclosing with equal prominence in immediate conjunction with the use of those words the conditions, terms, or prerequisites" ([MCL 445.903(1)(r)](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-445-903)). "Trial" is a word of similar import for a $0 tier. One line beside the tier name in the listing, on the price table, in the first-run dialog and at checkout: "Trial: free for N days. No card, no automatic charge. On day N+1 Ashley stops working until you buy a licence." A discrepancy between the listing and the dialog is a (1)(y) problem in its own right. The trial should take no payment credential and never convert; a licence that is a single charge for a stated term sits outside every automatic-renewal law verified here and outside the federal negative-option regime, whose 2024 rule the Eighth Circuit vacated in July 2025 and whose replacement is at the advance-notice stage after a March 2026 ANPRM with comments closed April 13, 2026 ([Covington, July 2025](https://www.cov.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2025/07/eighth-circuit-vacates-ftc-negative-option-rule); [FTC, March 2026](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/03/ftc-seeks-public-comment-response-advance-notice-proposed-rulemaking-regarding-negative-option); [Covington, March 2026](https://www.cov.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2026/03/ftc-launches-new-rulemaking-on-the-negative-option-rule)). **The ROSCA notes are partial.** The sub-research on ROSCA's text, the FTC's "free" guides at 16 CFR 251, the 2022 dark-patterns report and the 2024–2026 enforcement record was stopped before it wrote anything; only the vacatur, the ANPRM and Michigan's (1)(r) were verified independently. The design conclusion, that a non-converting trial and a one-time licence stay outside those regimes, does not depend on the unverified material, but if a subscription is ever introduced it should be run as a separate compliance project.

Minors are a bounded refund problem, not a drafting problem. Michigan's age of majority is 18 ([MCL 722.52](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-722-52)); a California court let minors disaffirm and demand refunds after consuming virtual goods ([I.B. v. Facebook](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/8720613/ib-ex-rel-fife-v-facebook-inc/)) and another let a minor disaffirm a game EULA to escape its arbitration clause ([Doe v. Epic Games](https://www.casemine.com/judgement/us/5e2e75d64653d01142ca24fd)); the Sixth Circuit, by contrast, sent the infancy question to the arbitrator under a delegation clause ([In re StockX](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/us-6th-circuit/2153818.html)). Because disaffirmance unwinds a licence the vendor can revoke, the downside is the price. Publish a policy that a purchaser under 18 at purchase may cancel for a full refund on request, do not invoke dispute or limitation terms against a self-identified minor, and keep the 18+ affirmation because it defeats a later claim that the vendor knew. COPPA does not reach a developer tool marketed to university students unless the vendor acquires actual knowledge of a user under 13, in which case it must delete or obtain parental consent; the April 2025 amendments, with an April 22, 2026 compliance date, did not change that standard ([FTC COPPA FAQ](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-coppa-frequently-asked-questions); [Hunton](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-information-security-law/ftc-publishes-final-coppa-rule-amendments)). Collect a single 18+ affirmation and no date of birth. FERPA reaches a vendor only when a school discloses education records to it under a contract and "direct control"; a tool the student installs and pays for personally holds no education records, and the Department's guidance "does not address online services or social media that students may use in their personal capacity outside of school" ([PTAC vendor FAQ](https://studentprivacy.ed.gov/sites/default/files/resource_document/file/Vendor%20FAQ.pdf); [PTAC online services guidance](https://studentprivacy.ed.gov/resources/protecting-student-privacy-while-using-online-educational-services-requirements-and-best)). The privacy statement should say the product is sold to individuals, not schools, and claim compliance with neither statute.

Accessibility is a venue question. The Sixth Circuit holds that "A public accommodation is a physical place" ([Parker v. Metropolitan Life](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/us-6th-circuit/1413718.html)), and a California appellate court held an online-only retailer's site is not one, closing the Unruh route ([Martinez v. Cot'n Wash](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/2022/b314476.html)); but a New York federal court found "a substantive right to obtain effective access" to a retailer's website with no physical-nexus requirement ([Andrews v. Blick Art](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/7326837/andrews-v-blick-art-materials-llc/)), and 5,114 digital-accessibility suits were filed in 2025, 38 percent of them in New York and California state courts ([UsableNet](https://info.usablenet.com/2025-year-end-report-on-web-accessibility-lawsuits)). DOJ's 2022 web guidance remains live and points to WCAG ([ADA.gov](https://www.ada.gov/resources/web-guidance/)); the Title II rule (WCAG 2.1 AA, compliance now April 26, 2027 and 2028) and the HHS Section 504 rule (May 11, 2027 and May 10, 2028) bind public universities and HHS-funded schools rather than the vendor ([ADA.gov fact sheet](https://www.ada.gov/resources/2024-03-08-web-rule/); [HHS](https://www.hhs.gov/press-room/hhs-extends-mobile-and-web-accessibility-deadline.html)), which means any department that ever adopts Ashley will ask for a WCAG 2.1 AA conformance report. The floor in the previous section satisfies both audiences.

**The state privacy notes are partial.** That sub-research was stopped; what the coordinator verified is that Michigan has no comprehensive privacy law, that SB 359 has had no action since June 12, 2025 ([Michigan Legislature](https://legislature.mi.gov/Bills/Bill?ObjectName=2025-SB-0359)), and that the Identity Theft Protection Act package SB 360–364, which would add a duty to "implement and maintain reasonable security procedures," passed the Senate on August 26, 2025 and sits in the House ([SFA analysis](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/documents/2025-2026/billanalysis/Senate/htm/2025-SFA-0360-C.htm)). The numeric thresholds of the roughly twenty enacted state laws were recalled, not verified; the working hypothesis that a vendor with a few hundred users falls under every consumer-count threshold is plausible but must be checked against the IAPP tracker, with Texas and Nebraska's "not a small business" tests and California's no-threshold notice law as the first items. Michigan's breach statute, which was fetched, defines personal information as a name plus a Social Security, licence or financial-account number with its code, so a leak of name, e-mail, AccessID and licence id is not a notification event in Michigan, with a $250 per-failure fine capped at $750,000 and no private right of action ([MCL 445.63](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-445-63); [MCL 445.72](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-445-72); [DWT](https://www.dwt.com/gcp/states/michigan)); California, New York and Vermont count an e-mail plus a password ([NCSL](https://www.ncsl.org/technology-and-communication/security-breach-notification-laws)), which is one more reason the licence store should hold no passwords, no card data and an encrypted, minimal record. **The CIPA notes are partial** in the same way: what was verified is that the California Legislature passed SB 690 on August 28, 2026, that as enrolled it makes website and app pen-register claims under §638.51 enforceable only by the Attorney General from January 1, 2027, retroactively for suits filed within the prior two years, and that §631 wiretap claims are untouched because the July 2 amendment deleted the commercial-purpose exemption ([Sidley](https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/newsupdates/2026/09/californias-sb-690-clears-the-legislature-what-it-means-for-cipa-website-tracking-claims)); whether the Governor signed it by September 30 was unknown when the notes were written, and the first-party case law and the FTC's order definitions were not retrieved. The design that minimises every theory that remains is first-party only: error reports go to the vendor's own endpoint, no hosted crash or analytics SDK receives them, the collection is disclosed at first run in a sentence separate from the licence text, VS Code's telemetry setting is honoured, and no report ever contains code, prompts or paths.

## A $50 LLC does not cover the author's own code; a $1,000–$2,500 policy does

Michigan's articles of organization cost $50 and the annual statement $25 through September 30, 2027, then $15; an assumed name is $25 ([MCL 450.5101](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-450-5101)); the statement is due February 15 ([MCL 450.4207](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-450-4207)); and a single-member LLC is disregarded for federal tax by default ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/single-member-limited-liability-companies)). The shield is statutory: a member "is not liable for the acts, debts, or obligations of the limited liability company" unless the operating agreement says otherwise ([MCL 450.4501(4)](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-450-4501)), and courts honour it "even when a single individual owns and operates the entity" ([Green v. Ziegelman](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/mi-court-of-appeals/1700473.html)). The two Michigan piercing cases supply the maintenance list by inversion. In one, members "made no distinction between their own debts and Shelby's debts," distributed money while insolvent, filed a falsified sworn statement and had "capital contributions of only $2,000" against millions of debt ([Florence Cement v. Vettraino](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/mi-court-of-appeals/1593272.html)); in the other the company was "grossly undercapitalized," ran on $630,000 of undocumented owner loans, "did not keep minutes for any meetings" and paid the owner's car lease, phone and hobby supplies ([Green](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/mi-court-of-appeals/1700473.html)). So: capitalise the LLC to cover its foreseeable costs including the insurance deductible; keep a separate bank account that never pays a personal expense; paper any member loan with terms; keep a written operating agreement and dated consents; take no distribution while the company cannot pay its debts; and put every licence, publisher account, processor account, domain and policy in the LLC's name, signed "Ashley Software LLC, by [name], Member." A PLLC is not indicated because distributing a developer tool is not the practice of medicine, and foreign qualification is not needed for online sales into other states, which fall inside every state's "interstate commerce" carve-out ([MCL 450.5008](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-450-5008)); sales-tax nexus is a separate and lower bar.

Because *Appletree* leaves the author exposed for his own negligent act, the policy is the part of the structure that pays. Published 2026 figures for micro software businesses put technology E&O at about $110 a month on average with typical limits of $1M per occurrence and $1M aggregate and a $2,500 deductible, cyber at $179 a month, and general liability at $37 ([Insureon, technology](https://www.insureon.com/technology-business-insurance/cost)); E&O across all small businesses averages $1,051 a year with 43 percent paying under $75 a month ([Insureon, E&O](https://www.insureon.com/small-business-insurance/errors-omissions/cost)); a software-developer quote page gives $990 for E&O, $1,799 for cyber and $365 for GL ([TechInsurance](https://www.techinsurance.com/technology-business-insurance/software-development/cost)); and an aggregator prices a "Solo SaaS founder (pre-revenue or <$25K ARR)" at $700 to $1,800 for $1M/$1M, naming Hiscox, NEXT, Vouch and Embroker ([PLIC](https://professionalliabilityinsurancecost.com/by-profession/software-developers)). Vouch's own list of what tech E&O covers maps onto the plausible student claims: "Errors or system failures that cause client downtime or lost revenue," "Breach of contract or warranty," "Accidental infringement of copyright, trademark, or other intellectual property rights," and "Third-party data breaches resulting from flaws or vulnerabilities in your product," with regulatory fines excluded ([Vouch](https://www.vouch.us/blog/tech-errors-and-omissions-insurance)). The realistic budget is one combined E&O plus cyber policy at $1M/$1M for roughly $1,000 to $2,500 a year, with GL added only if a landlord or university contract demands it. Two conditions matter more than the premium: the application must name the member as an insured and describe the product truthfully (students, personal data held, SSH into a university cluster), because misdescription is a rescission risk; and the author's personal umbrella is not a backstop, since "most personal umbrella policies explicitly exclude business activities" whatever the entity form ([FoCoIns](https://www.focoins.com/faqs/does-umbrella-cover-business-activities)). Because E&O is claims-made, keep every policy and application permanently.

Copyright registration is the precondition to the one suit the vendor might bring. No infringement action may be instituted until registration ([17 U.S.C. 411](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/411)), and statutory damages and fees are unavailable for infringement that begins before registration unless registration follows "within three months after the first publication" ([17 U.S.C. 412](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/412)). The first Marketplace release is publication on the ordinary reading, so the clock starts there. Because the code is AI-assisted, the Single Application is unavailable: applicants "must use the Standard Application," must not list an AI system as an author, and must exclude "AI-generated content that is more than de minimis" in the Limitation of Claim field, with cancellation and a §411(b) challenge as the penalty for knowing omission ([Copyright Office AI guidance](https://www.copyright.gov/ai/ai_policy_guidance.pdf)). The Standard Application costs $65 today and is scheduled to become $85 in fall 2026, the Single Application $45 to $55 ([Copyright Office fees](https://www.copyright.gov/about/fees.html); [proposed fee schedule](https://www.copyright.gov/rulemaking/feestudy2026/proposed-fee-schedule.pdf)). Circular 61 lets a closed-source program be registered with 25 plus 25 pages of object code and ten consecutive unredacted source pages, or 50 pages with trade secrets blocked out so long as less than half is redacted, and treats each version with new authorship as a separate work needing its own application ([Circular 61](https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ61.pdf)). Keep a contemporaneous record of which files the author wrote or substantially rewrote; the Office's 2025 report holds that "prompts do not alone provide sufficient control" while protecting human selection, arrangement and modification ([Copyright Office Part 2 report](https://www.copyright.gov/ai/Copyright-and-Artificial-Intelligence-Part-2-Copyrightability-Report.pdf)), so the record is what proves the claim. The earlier report's finding that version 1.0.0 already left under MIT stands; 2.0.0 is the first version worth registering.

Michigan taxes the paid licence. "Prewritten computer software" is tangible personal property "delivered by any means," and "delivered electronically" means by means other than tangible media ([MCL 205.51a](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-205-51a)); after *Auto-Owners*, software "electronically downloaded in its entirety" is taxable while remote access is not ([Allyn](https://www.allynintl.com/news-publications/entry/michigan-changes-policy-on-cloud-software)), and RAB 2023-10 treats an in-app purchase inside downloaded software as a purchase of prewritten software ([SALT Shaker](https://www.stateandlocaltax.com/digital-economy/michigan-updates-sales-and-use-tax-guidance-on-computer-software-and-digital-goods/)). A key that unlocks a locally installed .vsix is on the taxable side of that line. The LLC has Michigan nexus from its first sale because it is located here; register with Treasury before the first paid sale and charge 6 percent to Michigan buyers ([Hands Off Sales Tax](https://handsoffsalestax.com/michigan-sales-tax-nexus/)). Other states attach at $100,000 or, in the 14 that still have it, 200 transactions ([Avalara](https://www.avalara.com/blog/en/north-america/2025/06/states-eliminating-economic-nexus-transaction-thresholds.html); [DHJJ](https://www.dhjj.com/learningcenter/state-by-state-guide-to-economic-nexus)); at $30 a licence, Ohio's 200-transaction test is the one that bites first, at $6,000 of revenue. A yearly count of transactions by state from the processor is the compliance record, and a merchant of record, below, removes the whole question.

Export classification depends on one dependency. A closed-source product that bundles an SSH library encrypts sessions for confidentiality and is an encryption item; if it meets the mass-market criteria it self-classifies as 5D992.c under License Exception ENC §740.17(b)(1), which carries an annual self-classification report to BIS and the NSA coordinator by February 1 for items exported the prior year, in CSV form per Supplement No. 8, with a "nothing has changed" e-mail in later years ([15 CFR 740.17](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/15/740.17); [BIS annual self-classification](https://www.bis.gov/learn-support/encryption-controls/annual-self-classification); [BIS ENC table](https://www.bis.gov/media/documents/new-v2-740.17-enc-table-.pdf)). The public-source exemption does not apply to a closed product that merely incorporates open code ([15 CFR 742.15](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/15/742.15)), and the Ed25519 licence check alone is authentication, which is outside the controls, so the SSH channel is what decides it. If the extension instead shells out to the student's own OpenSSH binary and uses only OS-provided TLS, the notes' regulatory analysis points to EAR99 with no report; that is an engineering choice rather than a feature loss, and **Counsel** should sign off on the classification once the final dependency list is fixed. A worldwide listing makes a download by a student abroad an export, so the report should be treated as due. A trademark filing in Class 9 costs $350 per class with first action in about 4.45 months and disposition in about 10 ([USPTO fees](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/fees-payment-information/summary-2025-trademark-fee-changes); [USPTO pendency](https://www.uspto.gov/subscription-center/2026/reducing-trademark-pendency-improving-processing)); its value is standing, because Microsoft's infringement process is open only to "the copyright or trademark owner or their authorized representative" ([Microsoft infringement](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/legal/intellectualproperty/infringement)), but "Ashley" is a common name with a large existing ASHLEY portfolio in another field, so a clearance search precedes any filing and a compound name would clear more easily; the applicant should be the LLC.

The conflict-of-interest steps are disclosures, and three rule-sets overlap. The resident's employer is Detroit Education & Research, whose appointment agreement prohibits "unauthorized extracurricular professional activities," makes the program director, the DIO and the GME office the deciders, and makes the stipend the "sole source of compensation" except for authorised activities ([DMC GME agreement](https://wsusurgery.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/2023_2024_Example-GME_Resident_Fellow_Agmt-DMC.pdf)); the ACGME defines moonlighting as "compensated, medically related work" ([ACGME glossary](https://www.acgme.org/globalassets/pdfs/ab_acgmeglossary.pdf)), which a software business is not, but the agreement's language is broader than the glossary's and the program director is the named decision-maker. The Tenet Code requires that anyone who "may have a potential or actual conflict of interest ... report it promptly," with "disciplinary action up to and including termination" for failing to, and claims that "any work you create, in whole or in part, relating to your duties, and/or using Company time, resources, or information, belongs to the Company" ([Tenet Code of Conduct](https://s27.q4cdn.com/421561748/files/doc_downloads/2023-Tenet-Code_ADA-Updated.pdf)). At Wayne State, the research FCOI policy treats "any equity in non-publicly traded companies" as a significant financial interest for key personnel on sponsored research, disclosable within 30 days of acquisition and annually ([WSU FCOI policy](https://research.wayne.edu/integrity/fcoipolicy)), and the School of Medicine's policy applies to "all faculty, staff, and students" including affiliate and voluntary faculty ([WSU SOM COI policy](https://www.med.wayne.edu/policies/faculty/conflict-of-interest-policy_final_11_24_14a.pdf)). The steps are a one-page description of Ashley to the program director and GME office asking whether it needs authorisation; a potential-conflict report to Tenet Ethics and Compliance framed to ask for a written acknowledgment that the product is outside the IP clause; a WSU FCOI disclosure within 30 days of forming the LLC if the author is key personnel on any award; and, as a matter of record, never developing, testing or supporting Ashley on DMC or Tenet devices, networks or e-mail or during scheduled duty hours, with commit timestamps and hardware records that prove it. **Counsel** words the disclosures.

## Let a merchant of record be the seller, and refund before a $15 dispute fee arrives

The payments choice is the single decision that removes the most obligations. With Paddle the buyer contracts with Paddle: "Paddle is an authorised reseller of Products for Suppliers, which means you purchase the Product from Paddle" ([Paddle Buyer Terms](https://www.paddle.com/legal/buyer-terms)), Paddle "is the company you are purchasing from and is responsible for charging you" ([Paddle](https://www.paddle.com/about/why-has-paddle-charged-me)), and "As merchant of record, Paddle calculates, collects, and remits taxes for all supported countries — you have zero sales tax liability for any Paddle transaction," while "Transactions from countries under international sanctions ... are automatically blocked by Paddle" ([Paddle supported countries](https://www.paddle.com/help/start/intro-to-paddle/which-countries-are-supported-by-paddle)). The price is "5% + 50¢ per Checkout transaction," including tax compliance, fraud protection and chargeback defence, with the caveat that "Products under $10 ... require contacting Paddle for custom pricing" ([Paddle pricing](https://www.paddle.com/pricing)). Under that model the vendor is a supplier with no acquirer, no cardholder-data environment and no PCI attestation; the buyer's refund right is Paddle's, a 14-day withdrawal that lapses once the buyer "started downloading, streaming, using or benefiting from the Product" after agreeing to immediate access, and otherwise refunds are at Paddle's discretion or as law requires ([Paddle Buyer Terms](https://www.paddle.com/legal/buyer-terms); [Paddle refund policy](https://www.paddle.com/legal/refund-policy)). The licence agreement must therefore say that refunds are handled by the reseller under its policy rather than stating a conflicting "no refunds" rule; the "external events" language remains as a description of what is not a defect. Lemon Squeezy is being folded into Stripe Managed Payments, with its own founders describing slower support and fewer updates ([Lemon Squeezy](https://www.lemonsqueezy.com/blog/2026-update)), so it is a poor 2026 launch choice; FastSpring and Polar figures in the notes are third-party and were not confirmed on the vendors' own pages.

Plain Stripe is cheaper per transaction and costlier in obligations. The processing fee is "2.9% + 30¢ per successful transaction for domestic cards," plus 1.5 percent for international cards and 1 percent for currency conversion, with Stripe Tax at 0.5 percent per transaction where the vendor is registered and disputes at "$15 per dispute received" plus "$15 per dispute you respond to manually," the second returned only on a win ([Stripe pricing](https://stripe.com/pricing)). Stripe does not register the vendor with tax authorities ([Stripe Tax registrations](https://docs.stripe.com/api/tax/registrations)), so the Michigan licence, returns and the per-state count are the vendor's. PCI scope is manageable but real: a vendor with fewer than 20,000 online transactions a year is Level 4 and validates with SAQ A when Stripe-hosted Checkout or Elements keep card data in Stripe's iframe ([Stripe PCI guide](https://stripe.com/guides/pci-compliance)); since April 1, 2025 the revised SAQ A drops the script-management requirements 6.4.3 and 11.6.1 but adds an eligibility criterion that the merchant "has confirmed that their site is not susceptible to attacks from scripts," which "applies only to e-commerce merchants using embedded payment pages (iframes)" and "does NOT apply to merchants using redirects or fully outsourced payment functions" ([PCI SSC FAQ 1588](https://blog.pcisecuritystandards.org/faq-clarifies-new-saq-a-eligibility-criteria-for-e-commerce-merchants); [PCI SSC announcement](https://blog.pcisecuritystandards.org/important-updates-announced-for-merchants-validating-to-self-assessment-questionnaire-a)). A full redirect to a Stripe-hosted page or a Payment Link is the path that needs no affirmation; embedded Checkout needs Stripe's written confirmation. The recommendation is a merchant of record at launch, with Stripe as the fallback if Paddle declines the price point, and a switch to Stripe only when volume makes five percent expensive enough to justify taking on tax filings, disputes and the SAQ.

Chargebacks are the reason the refund policy must be operational rather than contractual. The network dispute right belongs to the cardholder and the issuer; the vendor's terms are evidence in representment and count only if they were disclosed near the pay button or on a click-to-accept page and the vendor "can prove the cardholder received and acknowledged the policy" ([Chargebacks911 on Visa's guidelines](https://chargebacks911.com/visa-chargeback-rules/)). Stripe's advice for digital goods is to submit an IP address or system log proving the customer downloaded or used the software, "a clean screenshot of how you present your terms of service during checkout, with the relevant policy clearly emphasized," and not the whole terms; it warns that "the card networks don't consider how many disputes you win or lose, only how many you receive," and rates the best case at about 60 percent ([Stripe dispute evidence](https://docs.stripe.com/disputes/best-practices)). Visa's monitoring program counts fraud plus disputes over settled card-not-present transactions with a merchant "Excessive" threshold of 1.5 percent from April 1, 2026 and about $8 per item once enrolled ([Merchant Risk Council](https://merchantriskcouncil.org/learning/resource-center/member-news/blog/2026/stricter-vamp-ratio-thresholds-are-now-in-effect-heres-how-to-stay-compliant)). On a $29 licence a $15 non-refundable fee plus a $15 at-risk counter fee means every dispute costs more than the sale even when won. The rule is to refund on request inside a stated window, reserve disputes for clear fraud, keep activation logs, and store the checkout acceptance (timestamp, IP, policy version) with each order. Advertise the licence at one price and add only tax at checkout; the 2024 federal fee rule is confined to tickets and lodging ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/12/federal-trade-commission-announces-bipartisan-rule-banning-junk-ticket-hotel-fees)), and the California and Minnesota all-in pricing laws, cited in the notes without a re-fetch, exclude government taxes but not a "processing fee" added at the last step.

Sanctions compliance is proportionate but not optional. OFAC's approach is risk-based, yet every e-commerce enforcement action says the same two things: block sanctioned geographies by IP and test that the block works. TradeStation paid $1,110,661 in March 2026 for about 500 trades from Iran, Syria and Crimea after "the second-tier geo-blocking control inadvertently became ineffective" (it read the company's own server IP) and an employee left a firewall control disabled for a year ([Paul Hastings](https://www.paulhastings.com/insights/client-alerts/ofac-settlement-highlights-importance-of-testing-and-auditing-sanctions-compliance-controls)); SAP and Tango Card were faulted for failing to adopt or extend IP geo-blocking ([Descartes](https://www.visualcompliance.com/blog/recent-ofac-actions-make-companies-doing-business-on-the-internet-consider-ip-address-geolocation-screening/)). The comprehensively sanctioned set is Cuba, Iran, North Korea and the Crimea, Donetsk and Luhansk regions; Syria's program was terminated effective July 1, 2025 with a narrower list-based program remaining ([OFAC](https://ofac.treasury.gov/recent-actions/20250825)), and the June 2024 Russia determination bans IT consultancy and cloud delivery of enterprise-management and design-and-manufacturing software, which a student developer tool is not ([OFAC FAQ 1187](https://ofac.treasury.gov/faqs/1187)). Stripe screens accounts and refuses restricted-country payments ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/resources/more/what-is-sanctions-screening-here-is-what-businesses-should-know)) and Paddle blocks sanctioned buyers ([Paddle sanctions](https://www.paddle.com/help/legal/sanctions)), but the vendor owns the download and activation endpoints and the Marketplace listing. Geo-block the embargoed list at purchase and at activation, re-test after every infrastructure change, run any manual sale through the free SDN search and keep the screenshot ([OFAC Sanctions List Search](https://sanctionssearch.ofac.treas.gov/)), write a one-page policy, and, as the simplest posture, block Russia and Belarus too.

## Microsoft removes "for any reason"; readable code, a reachable repository and top-of-README disclosure keep a listing up

The contractual position is unconditional and the enforcement record is mechanical. The Publisher Agreement lets Microsoft remove any Offering "for any reason or no reason and at any time," with a non-exhaustive list that includes "inconsistency between your Offering and its Listing Information," IP complaints under the notice-and-takedown process, "complaint(s) about the content or quality of your Offering," failure to support, and "an allegation of or your violation of any applicable law"; a second paragraph lets Microsoft "disable your Offering from operating with the In-Scope Products" where it determines harm ([Publisher Agreement §3(g)](https://cdn.vsassets.io/v/M261_20250904.11/_content/Visual-Studio-Marketplace-Publisher-Agreement.pdf)). The Participation Policies add removal for an Offering that "has not been provisioned by any customers for six or more months," "consistently receives negative customer feedback" or "many support tickets" ([Participation Policies §5(a)](https://cdn.vsassets.io/v/M187_20210610.3/_content/Visual-Studio-Marketplace-Participation-Policies.pdf)), and the Terms of Use allow termination "without notice" where use is "harmful to our interests or the interests ... of another person, entity, or the developer community" or "for any reason whatsoever" ([Terms of Use §5](https://cdn.vsassets.io/v/M264_20251020.18/_content/Microsoft-Visual-Studio-Marketplace-Terms-of-Use.pdf)). Approval is not clearance: "Microsoft's approval of an Offering does not constitute any representation or acknowledgment by Microsoft that the Offering complies," and Microsoft "may periodically test and evaluate your Offering" ([Publisher Agreement §3(c), (e)](https://cdn.vsassets.io/v/M261_20250904.11/_content/Visual-Studio-Marketplace-Publisher-Agreement.pdf)). Recovery from Microsoft is capped at $5,000, the publisher indemnifies Microsoft for claims "relating to the functionality of, the use of, or the inability to use the Offering," claims must be filed within a year, and the forum is King County, Washington ([Publisher Agreement §11, §14](https://cdn.vsassets.io/v/M261_20250904.11/_content/Visual-Studio-Marketplace-Publisher-Agreement.pdf)).

What removal looks like in practice is a scanner verdict followed by a client-side kill. Microsoft's pipeline scans every upload with Defender-class tooling, re-scans after publication and periodically Marketplace-wide, runs packages in a "clean room VM," blocks live secrets since September 22, 2025, blocks look-alike names, and removes confirmed extensions to a block list so that VS Code "forces the uninstall" ([Microsoft Developer Blog, June 2025](https://developer.microsoft.com/blog/security-and-trust-in-visual-studio-marketplace/); [VS Code runtime security](https://code.visualstudio.com/docs/configure/extensions/extension-runtime-security); [vsmarketplace #1442](https://github.com/microsoft/vsmarketplace/discussions/1442)). The control file VS Code fetches carries a `malicious` array of roughly 700 publisher.extension strings ([marketplace.json](https://main.vscode-cdn.net/extensions/marketplace.json)), and the public ledger records 2,114 removals between January 2 and September 26, 2026 in categories that include Malware, Impersonation, Untrustworthy, Spam, Deprecated and Expired Domain ([RemovedPackages.md](https://github.com/microsoft/vsmarketplace/blob/main/RemovedPackages.md)). Every non-malware category is one a legitimate publisher can trip, and "Expired Domain" means the verified domain is a maintenance obligation. Since July 2026 new publisher accounts and metadata changes pass automated risk checks that block on "elevated risk signals," and submissions that "appear to imitate or closely resemble existing publishers, extensions, branding, icons, names, URLs" may be blocked or taken down ([vsmarketplace #2036](https://github.com/microsoft/vsmarketplace/discussions/2036); [#2042](https://github.com/microsoft/vsmarketplace/discussions/2042)).

Three documented cases define the behaviours to avoid, and they are close to what Ashley does. Material Theme, with about nine million installs, was removed and force-uninstalled in February 2025 for "heavily obfuscated code and unreasonable dependencies including a utility for running child processes"; Microsoft later said "our investigation came to the wrong conclusion," and reinstatement took two weeks, a public issue and press coverage ([SC Media](https://www.scworld.com/news/vs-code-theme-with-nearly-4m-installs-removed-due-to-security-red-flags); [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/microsoft/microsoft-apologizes-for-removing-vscode-extensions-used-by-millions/)). nuBond was blocked within 24 hours of first approval in May 2026; its own root-cause named a build step that synthesised a package into `node_modules`, path traversal in `main`, and a 3.7 MB minified bundle containing dynamic `require()`, `process.env` reads and `require("inspector")`, and support replied with "identical boilerplate" ([vsmarketplace #2152](https://github.com/microsoft/vsmarketplace/issues/2152)). transitrix was removed as Malware in August 2026 because one of four platform builds failed validation, and after a "mandatory 30-day cooldown" was told it "would not be reinstated" with no evidence ([vsmarketplace #2204](https://github.com/microsoft/vsmarketplace/issues/2204)). Spawning terminals that run `ssh` and `claude auth login`, installing scripts on a remote host, fetching a revocation list and posting error reports to a publisher endpoint are all "network to unknown host plus child_process" patterns to a static scanner. Nothing in the record prohibits them; the record says they draw a manual review, and the manual review reads the README and the repository.

The minification question has no written answer and a clear practical one. Microsoft's March 2025 clarification says obfuscation "absolutely contributes to suspicion within anti-virus and anti-malware tools" and delays review, without defining it or distinguishing minification ([vsmarketplace #1196](https://github.com/microsoft/vsmarketplace/discussions/1196)), and its June 2025 guidance says "Clean, and non-obfuscated code in extensions is significantly less likely to trigger a false-positive" ([Developer Blog](https://developer.microsoft.com/blog/security-and-trust-in-visual-studio-marketplace/)). The notes suggest minifying only with source maps; the earlier report's release check fails on any `.map` file because the 1.0.0 package shipped 5.2 MB of full TypeScript. Both are satisfied by not minifying: bundle to readable, unminified JavaScript with legal comments preserved, ship no source maps, and avoid `eval`, `new Function`, dynamic `require`, `require("inspector")`, build-time generation into `node_modules`, and any download-and-execute path. The earlier report's Sixth Circuit finding that readable JavaScript is not a §1201 access control anyway means the secrecy given up is small; the removal risk avoided is a month of downtime and a forced uninstall from every student's editor.

The private repository URL is answered the same way. `vsce` refuses to package without a `repository` field unless `--allow-missing-repository` is passed ([vsce package.ts](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/microsoft/vscode-vsce/main/src/package.ts)); Microsoft tells users to check "Repository, issues tracker, and license availability" before installing ([VS Code runtime security](https://code.visualstudio.com/docs/configure/extensions/extension-runtime-security)); its Don'ts include duplicating repository links, and a 2024 scan counted 2,304 extensions "using another publisher's GitHub repository" as a risk signal ([BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/malicious-vscode-extensions-with-millions-of-installs-discovered/)). No Microsoft document says a 404 repository URL is itself a flag; the evidence is indirect. The design that satisfies the reviewer without opening the source is the split the earlier report already recommended for build attestations: a private source repository whose release workflow publishes to a public, LLC-owned distribution repository holding the README, CHANGELOG, LICENSE, THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES, the issue tracker and immutable releases with SHA-256 digests, and `repository`, `bugs` and `homepage` pointing there.

Disclosure placement is prescribed. Pre-sale consumer disclosures go "in the Offering description field" and must be "sufficiently prominent as is required by local law" ([Publisher Agreement §6(b)](https://cdn.vsassets.io/v/M261_20250904.11/_content/Visual-Studio-Marketplace-Publisher-Agreement.pdf)); behaviour a user would not expect must be "clearly documented at the top of both the package description and package README" ([Terms of Use §4(v)](https://cdn.vsassets.io/v/M264_20251020.18/_content/Microsoft-Visual-Studio-Marketplace-Terms-of-Use.pdf)); a privacy policy is mandatory if the Offering "accesses, collects or transmits any Personal Data" and must be submitted for display ([§8(b)(ii)](https://cdn.vsassets.io/v/M261_20250904.11/_content/Visual-Studio-Marketplace-Publisher-Agreement.pdf)); and the EULA or a link "must be delivered to Microsoft for publication" except for BYOL offerings, failing which the Microsoft Standard Agreement applies, whose aka.ms link currently redirects to a Bing search page ([§4(c)](https://cdn.vsassets.io/v/M261_20250904.11/_content/Visual-Studio-Marketplace-Publisher-Agreement.pdf); [aka.ms/vsm-standard-agreement](https://aka.ms/vsm-standard-agreement)). Because `pricing` may only be `Free` or `Trial` ([Extension Manifest](https://code.visualstudio.com/api/references/extension-manifest)) and the Marketplace "is currently not able to prevent customers from creating multiple subscriptions to Free Trial offerings," the one-trial-per-person restriction must live in the vendor's own terms ([Participation Policies §4](https://cdn.vsassets.io/v/M187_20210610.3/_content/Visual-Studio-Marketplace-Participation-Policies.pdf)). Open VSX, the second channel, defaults an unlicensed offering to MIT and requires "a full and complete description of what data you collect, for what purposes it is used, with whom it is shared and how long it is retained" in the listing ([Open VSX Publisher Agreement](https://www.eclipse.org/legal/documents/eclipse-openvsx-publisher-agreement.pdf)). The first screen of both the README and the description should therefore carry, in order: the trial sentence; the licence link and `SEE LICENSE IN LICENSE`; the privacy-policy link; and a "What this extension accesses and does" section modelled on Remote-SSH's documentation, which names what is installed on the remote host, lists the outbound hosts, and states that "Passwords and other tokens entered for alternate authentication methods are not saved" ([Remote-SSH docs](https://code.visualstudio.com/docs/remote/ssh)). For Ashley that section names the Microsoft and GitHub auth providers used, where tokens are stored and where they are copied, the terminals it opens and the commands they run, the scripts installed remotely, the Slurm jobs, the error-reporting endpoint with fields, retention and the off switch, and the licence-check and revocation-list endpoints. Because no comparator discloses copying credentials to a remote host, the extension is first-of-kind there and should over-document.

The remaining fields and trust signals are a short list. Set `capabilities.untrustedWorkspaces` to `false` or `limited` with a description that the extension opens terminals and runs `ssh`, since an extension that omits the field is disabled in Restricted Mode ([Workspace Trust guide](https://code.visualstudio.com/api/extension-guides/workspace-trust)); set `extensionKind` deliberately; use a PNG icon of at least 128 pixels, HTTPS non-SVG README images, and badges only from the allow-list; keep `qna` on; never mention a competing editor in the listing or install flow; mark every notification as coming from the publisher and never change a Microsoft-managed setting ([Participation Policies §1(d), (e), §3(b)](https://cdn.vsassets.io/v/M187_20210610.3/_content/Visual-Studio-Marketplace-Participation-Policies.pdf)). The verified-publisher badge requires an extension on the Marketplace for six months and a domain at least six months old, a DNS TXT record and a manual review "within 5 business days," and "any changes to the publisher display name will revoke the verified badge" ([Publishing Extensions](https://code.visualstudio.com/api/working-with-extensions/publishing-extension)); so the display name is chosen once, before the first publish, and the first public release should go up as early as the listing is ready, because the clock only starts then. Publishers must publish at least one Offering within 60 days of executing the agreement ([Participation Policies §2](https://cdn.vsassets.io/v/M187_20210610.3/_content/Visual-Studio-Marketplace-Participation-Policies.pdf)), so the publisher account should not be created before that release is ready. Since VS Code 1.97 a first install from a new publisher shows a trust dialog, and the substitutes for the badge during the six-month window are a reachable repository, a live Q&A channel, the access disclosure and honest reviews; ratings may not be manipulated ([Publisher Agreement §3(j)](https://cdn.vsassets.io/v/M261_20250904.11/_content/Visual-Studio-Marketplace-Publisher-Agreement.pdf)).

There is no published appeal procedure. The path is an e-mail to vsmarketplace@microsoft.com and vsmagent@microsoft.com, the aliases the agreement names for notices ([Publisher Agreement §14(b)](https://cdn.vsassets.io/v/M261_20250904.11/_content/Visual-Studio-Marketplace-Publisher-Agreement.pdf)), containing the publisher id, extension id, version, the action attempted, timestamps or correlation ids and a remediation summary, which are the fields Microsoft's own July 2026 posts ask for, plus a public issue on the vsmarketplace repository; expect the undocumented 30-day cooldown and boilerplate ([#2036](https://github.com/microsoft/vsmarketplace/discussions/2036); [#2204](https://github.com/microsoft/vsmarketplace/issues/2204)). Two rules protect the name: since mid-March 2026 deleted extension names "will not be available for reuse," even by the original publisher, so a listing is unpublished, never deleted ([#1708](https://github.com/microsoft/vsmarketplace/discussions/1708)), and the publisher's obligations to "report suspected security events ... at the earliest opportunity," to "promptly respond" to referred IP claims and to "use best efforts to respond to any inquiry received by Microsoft" mean the aliases are monitored daily and answered the same business day ([Publisher Agreement §8(d), §3(h), §6(c)](https://cdn.vsassets.io/v/M261_20250904.11/_content/Visual-Studio-Marketplace-Publisher-Agreement.pdf)). Resilience against removal is a second channel and an owned one: publish to Open VSX in parallel with the same README, a stated licence and the §6 data disclosure; keep an installable VSIX with its SHA-256 on the LLC's own site with a signed update channel, since directly installed extensions do not auto-update (earlier report); and for any department that adopts the tool, point to the `extensions.allowed` policy and the Private Marketplace announced in November 2025, both of which bypass public-listing risk ([Enterprise extensions](https://code.visualstudio.com/docs/enterprise/extensions); [Private Marketplace](https://code.visualstudio.com/blogs/2025/11/18/PrivateMarketplace)). Existing customers of a removed non-service offering keep using it under the agreement, which is why removal belongs in the Known Risks list as a named event rather than a promise.

## Conclusion

The research changes the shape of the question. The author asked which clauses protect him; the answer is that at his scale the clauses are the least of it and one of them, arbitration, is a liability. A $30 licence sold to a few hundred students sits at a point where the class-action ceiling under Michigan's consumer statute is a smaller number than the provider fees a single coordinated batch of arbitration demands would generate, so the device that protects Ticketmaster exposes Ashley. That inversion is arithmetic, not doctrine, and it reverses as volume grows, which is why the arbitration decision is a threshold to revisit rather than a setting to fix. What protects the vendor now is the ability to end a dispute cheaply and to prove what happened: a refund offered inside every statutory window, an acceptance record that survives a sworn denial, an entity and a policy that put the money somewhere other than the author's account, and a merchant of record that takes the sale, the tax, the chargeback and the sanctions screen off the LLC's books for five percent.

The Marketplace layer teaches the same lesson in a different register. Microsoft's contract gives no rights, its scanner gives no reasons and its appeal path gives no answers, so the listing is kept up not by arguing after removal but by never presenting the scanner or the reviewer with anything to decide: readable code, a repository that resolves, a disclosure section at the top of the page that describes every terminal, token and endpoint, and a name and domain that are maintained rather than obtained. The one design change this layer asks for, unminified bundles and a public distribution repository, gives up a secrecy that the Sixth Circuit had already declined to protect. Three topics remain incompletely researched, state privacy thresholds, the federal free-trial rules and California's CIPA, and each has been kept out of the load-bearing conclusions: the privacy and telemetry design is minimal enough not to depend on the thresholds, and the trial and licence are structured to stay outside the negative-option regime whatever its eventual text. What is still unknown is stated as unknown, and the agreement should say the same to the students who accept it.
